Homemade High Protein Muffins (Greek Yogurt & Egg White Magic)

Published May 15, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade high protein muffins recipe is the move when you want something that actually keeps you full without spending a ton of money or dirtying every pan in the kitchen. We're talking fluffy, satisfying muffins packed with Greek yogurt and egg whites — no protein powder required. They work for breakfast, a post-workout snack, or honestly just because your fridge has yogurt that needs a purpose.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
22 mins
Total
32 mins
Serves
12 muffins

Ingredients

  • 1 cup plain Greek yogurt (full fat or 2% — the good stuff)
  • 4 large eggs
  • 3 egg whites (or just use 5 whole eggs total if you want to keep it simple)
  • 1/3 cup honey or maple syrup
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 3/4 cups rolled oats (blended into flour, or use oat flour)
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon
  • 1/2 cup mix-ins of your choice (chocolate chips, blueberries, diced banana, or chopped walnuts)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 375°F and grease a 12-cup muffin tin or line it with paper liners. If you're using rolled oats, toss them in a blender and pulse until you get a coarse flour — takes about 30 seconds and costs half what oat flour does at the store.
  2. 2In a large bowl, whisk together the Greek yogurt, eggs, egg whites, honey, and vanilla until smooth and combined. Don't stress about it being perfectly silky — a few lumps from the yogurt are totally fine.
  3. 3Add the oat flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon directly into the wet ingredients. Stir everything together until just combined — overmixing is the enemy of fluffy muffins, so stop as soon as you don't see dry streaks.
  4. 4Fold in your mix-ins gently. Chocolate chips and blueberries both work beautifully here. If you're using banana, mash half of it into the batter and slice the other half for the tops — makes them look like you really tried.
  5. 5Divide the batter evenly among the 12 muffin cups, filling each about 3/4 of the way full. If you want, press a few extra mix-ins on top so people know what's inside before they even bite.
  6. 6Bake for 20 to 22 minutes, until the tops are golden and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Let them cool in the tin for 5 minutes before moving to a rack — they firm up as they cool and the texture gets even better.
💡 Improv Tip

These muffins actually taste better the next day once everything settles, so make a batch Sunday night and you've got breakfast handled through Wednesday. Store them in an airtight container on the counter for 2 days or refrigerate up to 5 days. Want to boost the protein even more without buying supplements? Swap 1/4 cup of the oat flour for unflavored or vanilla collagen peptides — it dissolves right in, you won't taste it, and the texture stays perfect.
