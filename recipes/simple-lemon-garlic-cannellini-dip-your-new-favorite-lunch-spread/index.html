Simple Lemon Garlic Cannellini Dip (Your New Favorite Lunch Spread)

Published October 22, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple lemon garlic cannellini dip is the kind of thing you'll make once and then wonder how you ever got through a week without it — bright, creamy, and ready in about 10 minutes flat. Spread it thick on toasted bread, dollop it into a wrap, or use it as the base of a lazy but genuinely satisfying lunch plate with whatever crunchy vegetables are rolling around your crisper drawer. It's pantry magic at its most effortless: one can of white beans, a lemon, some garlic, and olive oil — and suddenly lunch feels intentional.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
10 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 (15 oz) can cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 3 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
  • 2 cloves garlic, roughly chopped (or 1 large clove if yours are monsters)
  • 3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice (about 1 large lemon)
  • 1 teaspoon lemon zest
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional, but recommended)
  • 2 tablespoons water, to loosen the texture
  • Fresh parsley or chives for topping (optional)
  • Toasted crusty bread, pita, or crackers for serving
  • Sliced cucumber, celery, radishes, or carrots for dipping

Instructions

  1. 1Drain and rinse your cannellini beans really well under cold water — give them a good 30-second rinse. This washes off the canning liquid and keeps the dip tasting clean and fresh rather than tinny.
  2. 2Add the beans, olive oil, chopped garlic, lemon juice, lemon zest, salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes to a food processor or blender. If you only have an immersion blender and a tall jar, that works too.
  3. 3Blend everything together until smooth, stopping to scrape down the sides once. Stream in the water one tablespoon at a time until you hit your preferred texture — thick and spreadable is the goal, not runny. Taste it right now. Does it need more lemon? More salt? Adjust it until it tastes bright and a little punchy.
  4. 4Transfer the dip to a bowl or plate. Use the back of a spoon to swirl it dramatically — this is non-negotiable, it makes everything taste 15% better. Drizzle generously with olive oil, scatter on your herbs if using, and add a pinch more red pepper flakes.
  5. 5Build your lunch plate: toast a couple of slices of crusty bread or warm some pita, slice up whatever crunchy vegetables you have, and serve the dip front and center as the star. A handful of olives or a few cherry tomatoes alongside make this feel genuinely complete.
💡 Improv Tip

If raw garlic feels too sharp for you — totally valid, it can be aggressive — just drop the chopped garlic into a small dry skillet over medium-low heat for about 60 seconds before blending. You're not cooking it through, just taking the edge off. It'll give the dip a slightly mellower, nuttier garlic flavor that's still really present but won't knock you sideways at noon.
