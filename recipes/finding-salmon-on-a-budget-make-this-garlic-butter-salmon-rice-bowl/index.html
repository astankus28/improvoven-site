Finding Salmon on a Budget? Make This Garlic Butter Salmon Rice Bowl

Published August 2, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you've been finding salmon budget recipe ideas that don't feel like a compromise, this one's going to change your weeknight game. We're using canned salmon — the unsung hero of affordable seafood — and turning it into a glossy, garlicky pan-fried patty situation served over fluffy white rice with a simple cucumber-soy drizzle. It tastes like something you'd order at a fast-casual spot, but it comes together in under 30 minutes for about three bucks a serving.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 cans (14.75 oz each) pink or red salmon, drained and deboned
  • 1 cup plain breadcrumbs
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce, divided
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, grated (or 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger)
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil (vegetable or canola), for pan-frying
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 cups long-grain white rice, cooked according to package
  • 1 medium cucumber, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons rice vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 2 green onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 teaspoon sesame seeds (optional but worth it)
  • Sriracha or chili flakes, to serve

Instructions

  1. 1Cook your rice first so it has time to rest. Fluff it with a fork and set aside — keeping it covered with a clean towel keeps it from getting sticky.
  2. 2Drain the canned salmon well. Open the cans and press the lids down firmly over the sink to squeeze out as much liquid as possible. Tip into a large bowl, then use a fork to flake through and pick out any visible bones or bits of dark skin you don't want. Don't stress about getting every tiny bone — they're soft and edible, but do your best for texture.
  3. 3Add the breadcrumbs, beaten eggs, minced garlic, 1 tablespoon of the soy sauce, sesame oil, ginger, and black pepper to the bowl. Mix everything together until well combined — the mixture should hold its shape when pressed. If it feels too wet, add another tablespoon of breadcrumbs. If it feels too dry and crumbly, wet your hands slightly before forming the patties.
  4. 4Divide the mixture into 8 equal portions and press each into a round patty about 3/4-inch thick. Set them on a plate while you heat the pan.
  5. 5Heat the neutral oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Once shimmering, add the patties in a single layer — work in two batches if your pan is crowded. Cook undisturbed for 3 to 4 minutes until a deep golden crust forms on the bottom. Flip carefully and cook another 3 minutes on the second side.
  6. 6Reduce the heat to medium-low. Add the butter to the pan and let it melt around the patties. Spoon the foamy butter over the tops of the patties for about 30 seconds — this is the move that makes them taste restaurant-worthy. Remove from heat.
  7. 7While the patties cook, quickly make the cucumber drizzle. In a small bowl, stir together the remaining 1 tablespoon soy sauce, rice vinegar, and sugar until the sugar dissolves. Toss the sliced cucumber in this mixture and let it sit for a few minutes.
  8. 8To build the bowls: scoop rice into each bowl, nestle two salmon patties alongside, and pile some of the quick-pickled cucumbers on top. Drizzle any remaining dressing from the cucumber bowl over everything. Finish with sliced green onions, sesame seeds, and a drizzle of sriracha if you want heat. Serve immediately.
💡 Improv Tip

Canned salmon varies a lot by brand — red salmon (sockeye) has a richer flavor and holds together better in patties, while pink salmon is milder and usually cheaper. Either works great here. The real budget move is buying whichever is on sale and stocking a few cans at a time. They last for years in the pantry and are genuinely one of the best ingredients you're probably walking past at the grocery store.
