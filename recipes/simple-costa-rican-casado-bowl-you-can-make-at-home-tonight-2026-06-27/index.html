Simple Costa Rican Casado Bowl You Can Make at Home Tonight

Published June 27, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple Costa Rican casado bowl recipe is the kind of home cooking that makes you forget restaurants exist — everything in one bowl, full of flavor, and built from stuff you probably already have. Casado literally means 'married man' in Spanish, named after the classic plate Costa Rican wives would pack for their husbands — rice, beans, protein, plantains, all together like a happy family. We're doing it bowl-style so it's even easier to pull off on a weeknight.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 cups long-grain white rice
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 lb chicken thighs (boneless, skinless) or ground beef
  • 2 ripe plantains, peeled and sliced into coins
  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
  • 1 small white onion, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 small green bell pepper, diced
  • 2 roma tomatoes, diced
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon achiote powder or sweet paprika
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 2 cups shredded cabbage
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Fresh cilantro for serving
  • Hot sauce for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Cook your rice according to package directions. While that's going, you've got time to handle everything else — this is all about timing your moves right.
  2. 2Season your chicken thighs generously with salt, pepper, cumin, and achiote or paprika on both sides. Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a skillet over medium-high heat and cook the chicken 5 to 6 minutes per side until golden and cooked through. Let it rest for 5 minutes then slice or shred it. If you're using ground beef, just brown it in the same pan with the seasonings until fully cooked.
  3. 3In that same pan (don't waste those drippings!), add another tablespoon of oil over medium heat. Sauté the onion, garlic, and green pepper for about 3 minutes until softened. Add the diced tomatoes and cook another 2 minutes. Pour in the black beans, add the oregano, a pinch of cumin, and salt to taste. Let everything simmer together for 5 minutes so the beans soak up all that flavor.
  4. 4Heat the remaining tablespoon of oil in a separate small skillet over medium heat. Add your plantain coins and cook 2 to 3 minutes per side until they're golden and caramelized on the edges. Don't rush this — that golden color is where all the sweetness lives. Season with a tiny pinch of salt.
  5. 5Make your quick cabbage slaw: toss the shredded cabbage with lime juice and a pinch of salt. That's it. Simple, bright, cuts right through the richness of everything else in the bowl.
  6. 6Build your bowls: start with a base of rice, spoon the bean sofrito over one side, lay your sliced chicken or beef next to it, tuck in a few plantain coins, and pile a little cabbage slaw on top. Finish with fresh cilantro and as much hot sauce as your heart desires. That's a casado bowl, mi gente.
💡 Improv Tip

The real secret to a great casado is not letting the beans cook alone — they need that sofrito base of onion, garlic, pepper, and tomato to taste like something. Canned beans are totally fine here, but give them at least 5 minutes simmering in that flavor base and they'll taste like they've been cooking all day. Also, don't skip the cabbage slaw — it sounds plain but that lime hit is what ties the whole bowl together.
