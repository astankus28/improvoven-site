Simple Puerto Rican Sancocho Soup

Published June 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple Puerto Rican sancocho soup is the kind of meal that makes your whole house smell like somebody's abuela is in the kitchen — even if you're just working with what you've got. It's a hearty, soul-warming stew packed with root vegetables, tender chicken, and that deep sofrito flavor that hits different on a rainy day. Budget-friendly, forgiving, and absolutely delicious.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
55 mins
Total
75 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs bone-in chicken thighs or drumsticks
  • 1 large yuca (cassava), peeled and cut into chunks — about 2 cups
  • 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 2 ears of corn, cut into thirds
  • 1 large carrot, sliced thick
  • 1 green plantain, peeled and cut into rounds
  • 1 medium white onion, roughly chopped
  • 1 green bell pepper, roughly chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 tablespoons sofrito (store-bought or homemade)
  • 1 packet Sazón con culantro y achiote
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 2 teaspoons salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 8 cups water or low-sodium chicken broth
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or vegetable oil
  • Fresh cilantro for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Season your chicken pieces all over with salt, pepper, cumin, oregano, and the Sazón packet. Let it sit for at least 10 minutes while you chop your vegetables — this little rest makes a difference.
  2. 2Heat the oil in your biggest, heaviest pot over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken pieces in batches, about 3 minutes per side, until golden. Don't crowd the pot — you want color, not steam. Remove chicken and set aside.
  3. 3In the same pot, drop in the onion and bell pepper. Sauté for about 3 minutes until softened, scraping up all those browned bits from the bottom — that's pure flavor right there.
  4. 4Add the garlic and sofrito, stir everything together, and cook for another 2 minutes until fragrant. Your kitchen should smell incredible by now.
  5. 5Return the chicken to the pot and pour in the water or broth. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to medium, cover, and let it simmer for 20 minutes.
  6. 6Add the yuca, plantain, and carrot to the pot. These take the longest to cook, so they go in first. Simmer uncovered for 10 minutes.
  7. 7Add the potatoes and corn. Continue simmering for another 20 to 25 minutes, until all the root vegetables are fork-tender and the broth has thickened slightly and turned that beautiful golden color.
  8. 8Taste the broth and adjust salt as needed. If the soup looks too thick, splash in a little more water. If it looks too thin, let it bubble uncovered for a few extra minutes.
  9. 9Ladle into deep bowls, finish with fresh cilantro, and serve with white rice or crusty bread on the side. Make sure everyone gets a piece of corn — that's non-negotiable.
💡 Improv Tip

Yuca is the secret weapon here — as it cooks it releases starch that naturally thickens the broth and gives it that rich, velvety body that makes sancocho feel like a hug in a bowl. If you can't find fresh yuca, frozen works just fine and is usually cheaper. Just add it still frozen and give it a few extra minutes.
