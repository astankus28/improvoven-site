Easy Creole Rice Budget Recipe with Sausage & Peppers (One Pan, Under $8)

Published November 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This creole rice budget recipe brings serious Southern comfort to your table without touching a $20 bill — smoky andouille-style sausage, the holy trinity of peppers, onion, and celery, all cooked down into one deeply seasoned, saucy pan of rice that tastes like it took all day. It's the kind of weeknight dinner that makes your kitchen smell incredible and your wallet stay fat. Think New Orleans soul with a little Miami heat thrown in for good measure.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
40 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil or olive oil
  • 12 oz smoked sausage or andouille-style sausage, sliced into coins
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 2 stalks celery, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes, with juices
  • 1 1/2 cups long-grain white rice, uncooked
  • 2 1/2 cups chicken broth (or water + 1 bouillon cube)
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp Creole or Cajun seasoning (store-bought is totally fine)
  • 1/2 tsp dried thyme
  • 1/2 tsp dried oregano
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper (more if you want the heat)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • Salt to taste
  • 2 green onions, sliced (for topping)
  • Hot sauce for serving (optional but encouraged)

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the oil in a large, deep skillet or wide-bottomed pot over medium-high heat. Add the sausage slices and let them sear without touching for about 2 minutes, then flip and cook another 2 minutes until they've got some good color on both sides. That browning is flavor — don't rush it. Remove the sausage to a plate and set aside.
  2. 2In the same pan with all those beautiful drippings, add the diced onion, bell pepper, and celery. This is your holy trinity — the backbone of every great Creole dish. Cook over medium heat for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables soften and start to look a little golden around the edges.
  3. 3Add the minced garlic and stir it in for about 30 seconds until fragrant. Then add the smoked paprika, Creole seasoning, thyme, oregano, cayenne, and black pepper. Stir everything together and let those spices toast in the pan for another 30 seconds — you'll smell the difference immediately.
  4. 4Pour in the can of diced tomatoes with all its juices and stir to combine, scraping up any bits stuck to the bottom of the pan. Let this cook for 2 minutes so the tomatoes break down slightly into the veggie mixture.
  5. 5Add the uncooked rice and stir it into the tomato-veggie base so every grain gets coated in that seasoned mixture. Then pour in the chicken broth, stir once more, and nestle the reserved sausage back in on top.
  6. 6Bring the whole pan to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover tightly with a lid, and let it cook undisturbed for 20 minutes. No peeking — that steam is doing the work.
  7. 7After 20 minutes, remove the lid and check the rice. If there's still a little liquid, replace the lid and cook another 3 to 5 minutes. If the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed, you're done. Fluff gently with a fork, taste for salt, and adjust seasoning as needed.
  8. 8Scatter the sliced green onions over the top and serve straight from the pan with hot sauce on the side. Dinner is done.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to creole rice that doesn't turn into mush is resisting the urge to lift that lid. Every time you peek, you lose steam and mess with the rice's cooking time. Set a timer, walk away, do something else — the pan knows what it's doing. Also, if your grocery store sells smoked sausage in the cheap rope-style packages near the breakfast meats, that works just as well as andouille here and usually runs under $3 for the whole thing.
