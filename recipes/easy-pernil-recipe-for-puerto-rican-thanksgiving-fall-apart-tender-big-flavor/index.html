Easy Pernil Recipe for Puerto Rican Thanksgiving (Fall-Apart Tender, Big Flavor)

Published November 18, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy pernil recipe is the Puerto Rican Thanksgiving centerpiece your holiday table has been waiting for — slow-roasted pork shoulder rubbed down with a punchy homemade adobo paste until it's crackling on the outside and melt-in-your-mouth tender on the inside. It takes some patience (the oven does the heavy lifting), but the prep is genuinely simple and the payoff is enormous. Whether you're cooking for a full holiday crowd or want leftovers that'll last all week, this one delivers.

Prep
20 mins (plus overnight marinating)
Cook
5–6 hours
Total
6 hours 20 mins active (plus overnight)
Serves
10–12

Ingredients

  • 7–8 lb bone-in pork shoulder (picnic or Boston butt), skin-on if possible
  • 8 cloves garlic, minced or mashed into a paste
  • 2 tablespoons dried oregano
  • 1 tablespoon ground cumin
  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • 1 tablespoon onion powder
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon white pepper (or extra black pepper)
  • 1 tablespoon white vinegar or apple cider vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 tablespoon salt (plus more for the skin)
  • 1 teaspoon adobo seasoning (store-bought is fine — use what you've got)
  • Juice of 1 orange
  • Juice of 1 lime

Instructions

  1. 1Make your adobo paste: In a small bowl, combine the minced garlic, oregano, cumin, smoked paprika, onion powder, black pepper, white pepper, salt, adobo seasoning, vinegar, olive oil, orange juice, and lime juice. Stir it all together until it forms a thick, fragrant paste. Smell that — that's the soul of Puerto Rican cooking right there.
  2. 2Score and stuff the pork: Using a sharp paring knife, poke deep slits all over the pork shoulder — go in at an angle and wiggle the knife to open up pockets. Push a small spoonful of the adobo paste deep into each slit. This is what gives pernil its signature flavor from the inside out, so don't be shy.
  3. 3Rub and marinate: Slather the remaining adobo paste all over the outside of the pork, getting into every crevice. If you have skin-on pork, rub extra salt directly onto the skin to help it crisp up later. Place the pork in a large zip-lock bag or cover it tightly with plastic wrap in a roasting pan. Refrigerate overnight — or for at least 4 hours if you're short on time. Longer is always better here.
  4. 4Bring it to room temp and preheat: About 45 minutes before you're ready to roast, pull the pork out of the fridge and let it sit on the counter. Preheat your oven to 325°F (163°C). Low and slow is the whole game with pernil.
  5. 5Roast low and slow: Place the pork skin-side up in a roasting pan. Add about half a cup of water to the bottom of the pan (this keeps things from scorching and creates a little steam). Cover tightly with foil and roast at 325°F for about 1 hour per pound — so a 7-pound shoulder goes for roughly 6 to 7 hours. You're looking for the internal temp to hit 180–190°F, which is where the collagen breaks down and the meat gets truly fall-apart tender.
  6. 6Blast it for the skin (the best part): Once your pork is tender, remove the foil and crank the oven up to 425°F (220°C). Roast uncovered for another 30–45 minutes until the skin is deeply golden, crackled, and gloriously crispy. If you don't have skin-on pork, just let the outside caramelize and get a little charred at the edges — still incredible.
  7. 7Rest before you carve: Pull the pernil out of the oven and let it rest for at least 20–30 minutes before you touch it. This is non-negotiable — it lets the juices redistribute so the meat stays moist when you carve or pull it apart. Use this time to warm up your sides, set the table, and accept compliments that haven't happened yet.
  8. 8Serve and celebrate: Use two forks to pull the meat apart, or carve it in thick slices. Spoon some of those pan drippings over the top. Serve alongside rice and beans, tostones, mashed yuca, or whatever your holiday spread looks like.
💡 Improv Tip

The overnight marinade is where most of the flavor lives — if you skip it, you'll still get a good pork roast, but you won't get pernil. Plan ahead and let that adobo do its thing. No fresh citrus? A splash of bottled orange juice and lime juice works just fine. And if your pork shoulder doesn't have the skin on, don't stress — just crank the heat at the end anyway and get that outside nice and caramelized. The inside will still blow people away.
