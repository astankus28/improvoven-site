Beef Mixed Budget Recipe: Hearty Beef & Vegetable Stir-Fry with Soy-Ginger Sauce

Published October 27, 2026 · Improv Oven

This beef mixed budget recipe is exactly what weeknight dinners were made for — strips of affordable beef tossed with whatever vegetables are hanging out in your fridge, all pulled together with a punchy soy-ginger sauce that tastes like you actually tried. It comes together in under 30 minutes, costs next to nothing, and works over rice, noodles, or honestly just eaten straight from the pan. Classic Asian-American takeout energy, totally homemade.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb beef sirloin or flank steak, thinly sliced against the grain (or budget chuck — freeze it for 20 mins first to slice easier)
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce (for the beef marinade)
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil (optional but worth it)
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, grated (or 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger)
  • 1 medium onion, sliced into half-moons
  • 1 bell pepper, any color, sliced (or 2 if small)
  • 2 cups broccoli florets (fresh or frozen — both work)
  • 1 cup snap peas or green beans, trimmed
  • 1 medium carrot, thinly sliced on the diagonal
  • 3 tablespoons soy sauce (for the sauce)
  • 1 tablespoon oyster sauce (or an extra tablespoon of soy sauce if you don't have it)
  • 1 tablespoon honey or brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon rice vinegar or white vinegar
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional, for heat)
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water (slurry)
  • Cooked white or brown rice, for serving
  • Sesame seeds or sliced green onions, for garnish (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Thinly slice your beef against the grain — this is the move that makes budget cuts tender. Toss the slices in a bowl with 2 tablespoons soy sauce, 1 teaspoon cornstarch, and the sesame oil if using. Let it sit for at least 5 minutes while you prep everything else. This quick marinade makes a real difference.
  2. 2In a small bowl or measuring cup, whisk together the 3 tablespoons soy sauce, oyster sauce, honey, vinegar, and red pepper flakes. Set the sauce aside. Mix your cornstarch slurry in a separate small cup and keep it nearby — you'll need it at the end.
  3. 3Heat a large skillet or wok over high heat until it's genuinely hot — like, a drop of water should sizzle and vanish immediately. Add 1 tablespoon of vegetable oil, then add the beef in a single layer. Don't crowd it. Let it sear undisturbed for about 60 seconds, then stir and cook another 30 seconds until just browned. It doesn't need to be fully cooked through yet. Remove the beef to a plate and set aside.
  4. 4Add the remaining tablespoon of oil to the same pan. Toss in the garlic and ginger and stir constantly for about 30 seconds — just until fragrant, not burned. Add the onion and carrot and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
  5. 5Add the broccoli, bell pepper, and snap peas or green beans. Stir-fry everything together over high heat for another 3 to 4 minutes, until the vegetables are just tender but still have a little bite. If the pan looks dry, splash in a tablespoon of water to help them steam slightly.
  6. 6Return the beef to the pan along with any juices that collected on the plate. Pour the sauce over everything and toss to coat. Let it bubble for about 30 seconds, then stir in the cornstarch slurry. Cook another 30 to 60 seconds, stirring, until the sauce thickens and everything is glossy and gorgeous.
  7. 7Serve immediately over steamed rice. Scatter sesame seeds or green onions on top if you've got them — it makes the whole thing look like you put in a lot more effort than you did.
💡 Improv Tip

The real secret to a good stir-fry on a budget is HOT pan and dry beef. Pat your beef slices dry with a paper towel before marinating — moisture is the enemy of a good sear. And swap in literally whatever vegetables you have: zucchini, mushrooms, bok choy, frozen corn, even cabbage. The sauce makes everything taste intentional.
