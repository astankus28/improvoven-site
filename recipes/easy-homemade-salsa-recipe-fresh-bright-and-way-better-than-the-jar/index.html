Easy Homemade Salsa Recipe (Fresh, Bright, and Way Better Than the Jar)

Published October 29, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy homemade salsa recipe takes about 10 minutes and a handful of pantry staples — and the result is so good you'll never go back to the store-bought stuff. We're talking bright, chunky, deeply flavored salsa that works as a dip, a taco topper, an egg situation, or honestly just eaten straight off a spoon. No fancy equipment required, just a knife, a bowl, and a willingness to taste as you go.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
10 mins
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 1 can (28 oz) whole peeled tomatoes, drained (or 4–5 fresh Roma tomatoes if they're looking good)
  • 1/2 medium white or yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, peeled
  • 1–2 jalapeños, stems removed (keep the seeds for heat, ditch them for mild)
  • 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, loosely packed (stems and all — the stems have great flavor)
  • 2 tablespoons fresh lime juice (about 1 lime)
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • Pinch of sugar (optional, balances acidity)

Instructions

  1. 1Add the garlic and jalapeño to a blender or food processor first. Give them a few quick pulses so they get broken down evenly — you don't want a big chunk of raw garlic sneaking up on someone.
  2. 2Add the drained canned tomatoes (or fresh tomatoes, roughly chopped), onion, cilantro, lime juice, salt, and cumin. Pulse 8–12 times depending on how chunky you like it. For restaurant-style smooth salsa, blend a little longer. For pico-style chunky, keep it short and aggressive with your pulses.
  3. 3Taste it. This is the most important step. Does it need more salt? More lime? A tiny pinch of sugar if the tomatoes are really acidic? Adjust now, not after you've already poured it over everything.
  4. 4Transfer to a bowl or jar and let it sit for at least 10 minutes before serving — even that short rest lets the flavors meld and the salt do its job. If you can refrigerate it for 30 minutes, even better.
  5. 5Serve with tortilla chips, spoon over scrambled eggs, pile it on tacos, stir it into rice, or use it as a base for a quick shakshuka. It keeps in the fridge for up to 5 days in a sealed jar.
💡 Improv Tip

Canned tomatoes are genuinely your friend here — they're consistent, affordable, and often tastier than off-season fresh tomatoes that were picked three weeks ago. Fire-roasted canned tomatoes (same price at most stores) will give you a slightly smokier, more complex salsa with zero extra effort. Worth grabbing if you spot them.
