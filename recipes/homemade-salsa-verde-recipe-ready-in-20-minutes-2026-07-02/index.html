Homemade Salsa Verde Recipe (Ready in 20 Minutes)

Published July 2, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade salsa verde recipe is the kind of thing that makes people think you spent way more time in the kitchen than you actually did — we're talking bright, tangy, smoky green magic from a handful of tomatillos and a blender. Down here in Miami we put this on everything: tacos, eggs, rice bowls, a spoon if nobody's looking. Once you make it fresh, the jar stuff just doesn't hit the same.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs tomatillos, husked and rinsed
  • 1 medium white onion, quartered
  • 4 garlic cloves, unpeeled
  • 2 jalapeños (or serrano peppers for more heat)
  • 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, loosely packed
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lime juice
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or neutral oil

Instructions

  1. 1Set your oven to broil and line a baking sheet with foil. Place the tomatillos, quartered onion, jalapeños, and unpeeled garlic cloves in a single layer on the sheet. You want everything in contact with the pan so it gets some real color on it — don't crowd it too much.
  2. 2Broil for 8 to 10 minutes, then flip everything over and broil another 5 to 7 minutes until the tomatillos are blistered, slightly charred, and starting to break down. The onion should have some dark edges and the jalapeños should look roasted and soft. That char is flavor — don't be scared of it.
  3. 3Pull the pan out and let everything cool for about 5 minutes so you're not blasting hot steam into your face when you blend. Peel the garlic cloves — they'll slip right out of the skin now.
  4. 4Add the roasted tomatillos, onion, jalapeños, and peeled garlic into a blender along with all the juices that collected on the foil — that liquid is pure flavor, don't leave it behind. Add the cilantro, lime juice, salt, and cumin.
  5. 5Blend on high for about 30 to 45 seconds until smooth. Taste it. Adjust salt and lime as needed. If it feels too thick, add a tablespoon of water at a time until it's the consistency you like — some people want it pourable, some want it scoopable.
  6. 6Heat the oil in a medium saucepan over medium-high heat. Carefully pour the blended salsa into the hot oil — it will sizzle and pop, that's normal and that's the move. This step is called 'frying the salsa' and it deepens the flavor dramatically. Stir and cook for 3 to 5 minutes until the color darkens slightly and the salsa smells incredible.
  7. 7Remove from heat and let it cool before transferring to a jar or container. Store in the fridge for up to 10 days — if it lasts that long.
💡 Improv Tip

The single biggest upgrade you can make is frying the salsa in hot oil after blending — most people skip this step and wonder why their homemade version tastes flat compared to a restaurant's. That 3-minute sizzle in the pan is the whole secret. Also, tomatillos vary in tartness depending on the batch, so always taste and balance with a little extra lime or a tiny pinch of sugar if it's too sharp.
