Easy Trinidadian Curry Chickpeas (Budget-Friendly and So Good)

Published June 13, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Trinidadian curry chickpeas recipe is one of those dishes that tastes like it took all day but costs you maybe three dollars to make — that's the magic right there. Trini-style curry has this deep, toasted warmth that's completely different from other curries, and chickpeas soak it up like they were made for each other. Serve it over rice or with some soft roti and you've got a full meal that'll have everyone asking what your secret is.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 3 tablespoons curry powder (Caribbean or Trinidadian-style if you can find it)
  • 1 medium onion, finely diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, grated (or 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger)
  • 1 small scotch bonnet or habanero pepper, minced (use half if you want it milder)
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil (vegetable or canola)
  • 1 cup water or vegetable broth
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 stalks green onion, sliced (for serving)
  • Fresh cilantro, roughly chopped (for serving)
  • Cooked white rice or roti, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Start by blooming your curry — this is the move that makes Trini curry taste like Trini curry. Add the oil to a wide skillet or pot over medium heat. Once it shimmers, add the curry powder and cumin directly into the oil and stir constantly for about 60 to 90 seconds until it darkens slightly and smells incredible. Don't walk away here, you're building the whole flavor base.
  2. 2Add the onion to the toasted curry oil and cook for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring often, until softened and starting to turn golden around the edges.
  3. 3Add the garlic, ginger, and scotch bonnet pepper. Stir everything together and cook for another 2 minutes until fragrant. Your kitchen should smell absolutely amazing right now.
  4. 4Add the drained chickpeas and stir well to coat every single one in that curry base. Let them cook for 2 minutes so they can start absorbing all those flavors.
  5. 5Pour in the water or broth, add the salt and black pepper, and stir to combine. Bring everything up to a gentle simmer, then reduce the heat to medium-low and cook uncovered for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and clings to the chickpeas. If it gets too thick before the time is up, just splash in a little more water.
  6. 6Taste and adjust salt. Serve hot over white rice or with roti, topped with sliced green onion and fresh cilantro.
💡 Improv Tip

The single biggest upgrade you can make here is finding a Trinidadian or Caribbean curry powder — brands like Chief or Turban are common in Caribbean grocery stores and some international aisles. That blend has a different ratio of spices than Indian curry powder and it gives you that authentic flavor you're chasing. If you only have standard grocery store curry powder, it'll still be delicious, just add a tiny pinch of allspice to bring it a little closer to that Caribbean warmth.
