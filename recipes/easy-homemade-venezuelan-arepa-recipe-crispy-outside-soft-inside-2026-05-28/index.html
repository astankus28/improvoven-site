Easy Homemade Venezuelan Arepa Recipe (Crispy Outside, Soft Inside)

Published May 28, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Venezuelan arepa homemade recipe is one of those things that sounds fancy but is literally just two ingredients and about 30 minutes of your life — you got this. Arepas are the ultimate budget meal: gluten-free, filling, and a blank canvas for whatever you've got in the fridge. Think of them as Venezuela's answer to the everything bagel, except you made them yourself and that's way cooler.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 cups masarepa (pre-cooked white cornmeal, like P.A.N. brand — find it at any Latin grocery or Walmart)
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 to 2.5 cups warm water
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil or butter (for the pan)
  • Optional fillings: shredded rotisserie chicken, black beans, avocado, shredded cheese, fried egg

Instructions

  1. 1In a large bowl, combine the masarepa and salt. Give it a quick stir so the salt is evenly distributed throughout the dry flour.
  2. 2Pour in 2 cups of warm water and mix with your hands or a fork until a soft dough forms. It should feel like soft Play-Doh — not sticky, not crumbly. If it's cracking when you shape it, add water a tablespoon at a time until it comes together smoothly.
  3. 3Divide the dough into 8 equal balls. Press each ball between your palms into a round disc about half an inch thick — roughly the size of a hockey puck. Don't stress about perfection here, rustic is the vibe.
  4. 4Heat a skillet, griddle, or cast iron pan over medium heat and add your oil or butter. Once hot, add the arepas in a single layer — don't crowd the pan, work in batches if needed.
  5. 5Cook for 4 to 5 minutes per side until a golden-brown crust forms and the arepa sounds hollow when you tap it. That hollow tap is your signal — that means they're done and the inside is fully cooked.
  6. 6Optional but highly recommended: transfer the cooked arepas to a 375°F oven for 5 to 8 minutes to make the inside even fluffier and fully set. Venezuelans call this the secret step.
  7. 7Let them cool for just a minute, then slice open like a pita pocket and stuff with whatever your fridge is offering today. Shredded chicken with avocado, refried beans and cheese, a fried egg with hot sauce — all of it works, all of it slaps.
💡 Improv Tip

The brand matters here — grab P.A.N. masarepa (the yellow or white bag). Do NOT use regular cornmeal, corn flour, or masa harina for tamales — they are not the same thing and your arepas will not cooperate. Masarepa is pre-cooked, which is what gives the arepa its texture. You can find it at almost any Latin market for under $3 a bag, and one bag makes like 30 arepas. That's basically free food.
