Easy Homemade Gravy Recipe for Thanksgiving (Ready in 20 Minutes)

Published November 7, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy homemade gravy recipe is the one Thanksgiving dish people always assume is hard — but it's genuinely one of the simplest things you can make from scratch. We're talking a rich, velvety pan gravy built from a simple roux and good stock, with a little sofrito-inspired warmth that makes it taste like it simmered all day. Spoon it over a turkey sandwich at lunch, pour it on mashed potatoes, or just drink it straight — no judgment here.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
20 mins
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 1/2 cups low-sodium chicken or turkey broth (store-bought is totally fine)
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, or to taste
  • 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce
  • Optional: drippings from your roasting pan (up to 2 tablespoons, skimmed of excess fat)

Instructions

  1. 1Melt the butter in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Once it's melted and starting to bubble slightly, add the flour all at once. Whisk constantly for about 2 minutes until the mixture turns a light golden color and smells a little nutty — this is your roux, and cooking out the raw flour taste is the most important step. Don't rush it.
  2. 2Slowly pour in about half a cup of the broth while whisking hard. It's going to look thick and clumpy at first — that's normal, keep whisking. Once it smooths out, pour in the remaining broth in a steady stream, whisking the whole time to keep it lump-free.
  3. 3Add the garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, thyme, black pepper, Worcestershire sauce, and pan drippings if you're using them. Stir everything together and bring the gravy up to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
  4. 4Let it cook for 5 to 8 minutes, stirring frequently, until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon. If it gets too thick, splash in a little more broth and whisk it in. Taste and adjust salt as needed — this is where you make it yours.
  5. 5Pull it off the heat and serve immediately. This gravy is perfect spooned over leftover turkey sandwiches on toasted bread with a smear of cranberry sauce, poured over a plate of fluffy mashed potatoes, or drizzled over a simple open-faced turkey and stuffing lunch situation that feels way more put-together than the effort it took.
💡 Improv Tip

The smoked paprika is the little Miami move here — it adds this subtle depth that people can't quite place but always notice. If your gravy develops lumps at any point, don't panic. Just take it off the heat and whisk vigorously, or run an immersion blender through it for 15 seconds. It'll come back together. And if you have actual turkey drippings from your roasting pan, please use them — even a tablespoon makes this gravy taste like it took hours.
