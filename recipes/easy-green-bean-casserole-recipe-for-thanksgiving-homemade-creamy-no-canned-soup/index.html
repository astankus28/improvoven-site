Easy Green Bean Casserole Recipe for Thanksgiving (Homemade, Creamy, No Canned Soup)

Published November 25, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy green bean casserole recipe is the Thanksgiving side dish everyone hovers around — but this version skips the canned condensed soup and tastes so much better for it. We're making a quick homemade mushroom cream sauce that comes together in about 10 minutes, topped with those crispy fried onions everyone loves. It's still simple, still comforting, still wildly affordable — just a little more 'you actually made this' and a little less 'you opened three cans.'

Prep
15 mins
Cook
35 mins
Total
50 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs fresh or frozen green beans, trimmed and cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1 medium yellow onion, finely diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 8 oz cremini or white button mushrooms, sliced
  • 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1.5 cups whole milk (or any milk you have)
  • 0.5 cup chicken or vegetable broth
  • 0.5 teaspoon onion powder
  • 0.5 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 1.5 cups crispy fried onions (store-bought, divided)
  • Optional: pinch of nutmeg for the sauce

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 375°F. If using fresh green beans, blanch them first: bring a large pot of salted water to a boil, drop in the beans, and cook for 4–5 minutes until bright green and just barely tender. Drain and immediately transfer to a bowl of ice water to stop the cooking. Drain again and set aside. If using frozen green beans, just thaw and pat them dry — no blanching needed.
  2. 2In a large oven-safe skillet or saucepan over medium heat, melt the butter. Add the diced onion and cook for 4–5 minutes until softened and translucent. Add the garlic and mushrooms, season with a pinch of salt, and cook another 5–6 minutes until the mushrooms have released their liquid and started to brown. Don't rush this step — those browned mushrooms are where the flavor lives.
  3. 3Sprinkle the flour over the mushroom mixture and stir to coat everything evenly. Cook for about 1 minute to get rid of the raw flour taste. Then slowly pour in the milk and broth, whisking or stirring constantly to prevent lumps. Add the onion powder, garlic powder, a few cracks of black pepper, and the optional pinch of nutmeg. Let the sauce simmer on medium-low for 3–4 minutes, stirring often, until it thickens to a creamy, pourable consistency. Taste and adjust salt.
  4. 4Add the drained green beans to the sauce and stir to coat everything well. If your pan is oven-safe, great — you can bake right in it. If not, transfer the mixture to a greased 9x13 baking dish. Spread it out evenly.
  5. 5Scatter about 1 cup of the crispy fried onions over the top in an even layer. Slide the dish into the preheated oven and bake uncovered for 20–25 minutes, until the sauce is bubbling around the edges and the onions on top are deep golden brown.
  6. 6Pull it out of the oven and immediately scatter the remaining half cup of fresh crispy onions over the top — this two-layer onion move gives you crunch on crunch. Let it rest for 5 minutes before serving so the sauce can settle. Serve hot straight from the dish.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to avoiding a watery casserole is making sure your green beans are as dry as possible before they go into the sauce — whether you blanched and drained fresh ones or thawed frozen ones, give them a good pat-down with paper towels. Extra moisture is what turns a creamy casserole into a soupy one, and nobody wants to explain that at the Thanksgiving table.
