Simple Baked Ziti Recipe (Cheesy, Saucy, and Crowd-Pleasing)

Published September 30, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple baked ziti recipe is the kind of dinner that makes everyone at the table go quiet in the best possible way — bubbling cheese, hearty tomato sauce, and tender pasta all in one glorious dish. It comes together with pantry staples you probably already have, and it feeds a crowd without wrecking your grocery budget. Think of it as lasagna's easier, no-fuss cousin who shows up to the party every time.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
40 mins
Total
55 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ziti pasta (or penne if that's what you've got)
  • 1 lb ground beef or Italian sausage (sweet or hot)
  • 1 jar (24 oz) marinara sauce
  • 1 can (14 oz) crushed tomatoes
  • 1 cup whole-milk ricotta cheese
  • 2 cups shredded mozzarella cheese, divided
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese, divided
  • 1 egg
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried Italian seasoning
  • 1/2 teaspoon crushed red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Fresh basil or parsley for garnish (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 375°F and lightly grease a 9x13-inch baking dish with a little olive oil or cooking spray. Get a large pot of salted water boiling for the pasta.
  2. 2Cook the ziti about 2 minutes less than the package says — you want it just shy of al dente because it'll finish cooking in the oven. Drain it and set aside.
  3. 3While the pasta cooks, heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef or sausage and break it up as it browns, about 5-6 minutes. Drain off excess fat if needed.
  4. 4Add the minced garlic to the meat and cook for another minute until fragrant. Pour in the marinara sauce and crushed tomatoes, then stir in the Italian seasoning, red pepper flakes, salt, and pepper. Let that simmer on low for about 5 minutes to let everything get acquainted.
  5. 5In a medium bowl, stir together the ricotta, egg, half the mozzarella (1 cup), and half the Parmesan (1/4 cup). Season with a pinch of salt and pepper. This is your creamy layer and it's going to do a lot of heavy lifting.
  6. 6Add the drained pasta into the meat sauce and stir to coat everything evenly. Spread half of that mixture into the bottom of your prepared baking dish.
  7. 7Dollop the ricotta mixture evenly over the pasta layer — you don't need to make it perfect, rustic is fine. Spoon the remaining pasta and sauce on top and spread it out gently.
  8. 8Sprinkle the remaining 1 cup of mozzarella and 1/4 cup of Parmesan across the top in an even layer. Cover the dish tightly with aluminum foil.
  9. 9Bake covered for 20 minutes, then remove the foil and bake for another 15-20 minutes until the cheese on top is melted, golden in spots, and the edges are bubbling. Let it rest for 5-10 minutes before scooping — this helps it hold together when you serve it.
  10. 10Garnish with fresh basil or parsley if you have it, and serve straight from the dish. Leftovers reheat beautifully the next day, possibly even better than the first round.
💡 Improv Tip

Don't skip the rest time after it comes out of the oven — I know it's tempting to dig right in, but 5-10 minutes lets everything set up so you get clean, scoopable portions instead of a sauce avalanche. Also, if your jar of marinara is on the smaller side (around 20 oz), just add an extra splash of water or chicken broth to the sauce to keep it saucy enough — dry baked ziti is a tragedy we're actively trying to prevent here.
