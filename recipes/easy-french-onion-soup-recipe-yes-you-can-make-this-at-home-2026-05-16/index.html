Easy French Onion Soup Recipe (Yes, You Can Make This at Home)

Published May 16, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy French onion soup recipe is the one I make when I want something that feels fancy but costs almost nothing — we're talking onions, butter, and whatever cheese you have melting in the fridge. It's deeply savory, a little sweet from the caramelized onions, and that broiled cheesy toast on top? Mami, that alone is worth making this. No fancy restaurant, no special occasion — just your stove and about 45 minutes.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
35 mins
Total
45 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 4 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce
  • 4 cups beef broth (store-bought is totally fine)
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 4 thick slices of bread (baguette, Cuban bread, whatever you have)
  • 1 cup shredded Gruyere, Swiss, or mozzarella cheese

Instructions

  1. 1Melt the butter in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the sliced onions and stir to coat them in the butter. Sprinkle in the sugar and salt — the sugar helps the onions caramelize without burning, trust the process.
  2. 2Cook the onions low and slow, stirring every few minutes, for about 25 minutes total. You want them to go from pale and crunchy to deeply golden, jammy, and sweet. Don't rush this part — this is where all the flavor comes from. If they start sticking, add a tiny splash of water and scrape up those bits.
  3. 3Once the onions are beautifully caramelized, add the minced garlic and cook for 1 more minute until fragrant.
  4. 4Pour in the beef broth, water, Worcestershire sauce, thyme, and bay leaf. Stir everything together and bring to a boil, then reduce heat and let it simmer for 10 minutes so all those flavors get to know each other. Taste and adjust salt and pepper.
  5. 5While the soup simmers, toast your bread slices in the oven or toaster until they're golden and a little firm — this keeps them from getting completely soggy when they hit the soup.
  6. 6Turn your oven broiler on high. Ladle the soup into oven-safe bowls or crocks. Remove the bay leaf. Place a slice of toasted bread on top of each bowl, then pile on a generous handful of shredded cheese.
  7. 7Place the bowls on a baking sheet and slide them under the broiler for 2 to 3 minutes, watching closely, until the cheese is melted, bubbly, and starting to get those beautiful golden spots on top.
  8. 8Pull them out carefully — the bowls will be extremely hot. Let them sit for a minute before serving, and warn whoever you're feeding because that first spoonful will absolutely burn your tongue if you don't wait. Worth it though.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to French onion soup that actually tastes like something is patience with those onions — 25 minutes feels long but that caramelization is the whole flavor base of the dish. If your broth tastes a little flat at the end, a tiny splash more of Worcestershire sauce or even a pinch of soy sauce will deepen it right up. And if you don't have oven-safe bowls, just broil the cheesy toasts separately on a baking sheet and float them on top of the soup — same vibe, still delicious.
