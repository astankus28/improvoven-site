Easy Pumpkin Flan Recipe — The Latin Thanksgiving Dessert You Didn't Know You Needed

Published November 4, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy pumpkin flan recipe is the Latin Thanksgiving dessert that quietly steals the show every single year — silky, caramel-crowned, and way more interesting than another slice of pie. It's basically a marriage of classic Cuban flan and American pumpkin pie, and trust me, the combination is everything. Best part? It comes together with a can of pumpkin and a handful of pantry staples, no fancy equipment required.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
55 mins
Total
1 hr 10 mins (plus 4 hrs chilling)
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar (for the caramel)
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1 (15 oz) can pure pumpkin puree
  • 1 (14 oz) can sweetened condensed milk
  • 1 (12 oz) can evaporated milk
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • Pinch of ground cloves

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 325°F. Set a 9-inch round cake pan or pie dish nearby — not greased, just sitting there ready. Also grab a larger roasting pan or baking dish that the cake pan will fit inside. This is your water bath setup and it's what gives flan that legendary silky texture.
  2. 2Make the caramel: Add the sugar and water to a small heavy-bottomed saucepan over medium heat. Don't stir — just let it do its thing. Swirl the pan gently if you need to, but keep the spoon out of it. After about 6 to 8 minutes it'll turn a deep amber color, like iced tea. The second it hits that color, pour it immediately into your cake pan and tilt the pan to coat the bottom evenly. Work fast — caramel waits for no one. Set it aside to harden, about 5 minutes.
  3. 3In a blender or large bowl, combine the pumpkin puree, sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, eggs, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, salt, and cloves. If using a blender, blend on low for about 30 seconds until completely smooth. If mixing by hand, whisk very thoroughly until no streaks remain. The goal is a silky, uniform custard — no lumps, no egg streaks.
  4. 4Pour the pumpkin custard gently over the hardened caramel in your pan. Place the cake pan inside the larger roasting pan, then carefully pour hot water into the outer pan until it comes about 1 inch up the sides of the cake pan. This water bath prevents the flan from cracking and keeps it impossibly creamy.
  5. 5Carefully transfer the whole setup to the oven — go slow so you don't slosh water into the flan. Bake for 50 to 55 minutes, until the edges are set but the center still has a gentle jiggle, like Jell-O. It'll finish setting up as it cools. Don't overbake — a wobbly center is your friend here.
  6. 6Carefully remove the cake pan from the water bath. Let it cool at room temperature for about 30 minutes, then cover it with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight. This chill time is non-negotiable — it's what lets everything firm up and the flavors deepen.
  7. 7To unmold, run a thin knife or offset spatula around the entire edge of the flan. Place a rimmed plate or platter — something with a little lip to catch that caramel sauce — upside down on top of the pan. In one confident motion, flip the whole thing over. Give it a second, and you'll hear that satisfying thud as it releases. Lift the pan to reveal a gorgeous caramel-glazed pumpkin flan. Slice and serve cold, spooning extra caramel from the plate over each slice.
💡 Improv Tip

Don't skip the overnight chill if you can help it — flan made the day before Thanksgiving is not only easier on your kitchen-day stress levels, it actually tastes better. The custard gets denser and the caramel soaks down into it just a little, making every slice more luscious than the last. This one is a genuine make-ahead hero.
