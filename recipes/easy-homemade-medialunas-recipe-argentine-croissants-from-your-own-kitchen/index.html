Easy Homemade Medialunas Recipe (Argentine Croissants from Your Own Kitchen)

Published November 14, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy medialunas recipe homemade version brings the magic of an Argentine café straight to your kitchen — flaky, buttery, and glazed with that signature honey shine. Medialunas are Argentina's beloved answer to the French croissant: a little sweeter, a little softer, and honestly more forgiving to make at home than you'd think. Once you taste one warm from your own oven, you'll never look at a bakery case the same way.

Prep
40 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
3 hrs (includes resting time)
Serves
16 medialunas

Ingredients

  • 2 1/4 tsp (1 packet) active dry yeast
  • 1/2 cup warm whole milk (about 110°F)
  • 1/4 cup warm water
  • 3 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • 2 large eggs, divided (1 for dough, 1 for egg wash)
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, cold, cut into small cubes
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter, softened (for laminating)
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • 2 tbsp warm water (for glaze)
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar (for glaze)

Instructions

  1. 1Activate the yeast: Combine warm milk and warm water in a small bowl. Sprinkle in the yeast and a pinch of sugar, stir gently, and let sit for 5–10 minutes until it looks foamy and alive. If nothing happens, your yeast is old — grab a fresh packet before moving forward.
  2. 2Make the dough: In a large bowl, whisk together flour, sugar, and salt. Add the yeast mixture, one egg, and vanilla extract. Mix with a wooden spoon or your hands until a shaggy dough forms. Turn it out onto a lightly floured surface and knead for about 5 minutes until smooth. The dough should be soft but not sticky — add flour one tablespoon at a time if it's clinging to everything.
  3. 3Add the cold butter: This is the key to that flaky texture. Work the cold butter cubes into the dough by flattening them in with the heel of your hand, folding the dough over, and repeating. It'll look messy for a few minutes — that's fine. Keep working until the butter is fully incorporated and the dough is smooth and pliable, about 5–7 minutes.
  4. 4First rest: Shape the dough into a ball, place it in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with plastic wrap or a clean towel, and let it rest at room temperature for 1 hour, or until roughly doubled in size.
  5. 5Roll and laminate: On a lightly floured surface, roll the dough into a large rectangle, about 12x18 inches. Dot the surface with the softened butter and use your fingers to spread it in a thin, even layer. Fold the dough in thirds like a letter, rotate 90 degrees, and roll it out again. Fold in thirds once more. Wrap in plastic and refrigerate for 30 minutes — this rest makes the layers distinct and keeps the butter from melting before it hits the oven.
  6. 6Shape the medialunas: Remove the dough from the fridge. Roll it out into a large circle or rectangle about 1/4 inch thick. Use a knife or pizza cutter to cut triangles — aim for about 3 inches at the base and 5–6 inches long (like pizza slices but thinner). Starting from the wide base, roll each triangle up toward the tip, pressing gently to seal. Curve the ends slightly toward each other to form that classic crescent shape.
  7. 7Second rise: Place the shaped medialunas on a parchment-lined baking sheet, leaving 2 inches between each one. Cover loosely and let rise for 30–45 minutes at room temperature. They should look puffed and a little pillowy.
  8. 8Preheat and egg wash: Preheat your oven to 375°F. Beat the second egg with a splash of milk and brush it gently over each medialuna. Don't press hard or you'll deflate all that beautiful rise.
  9. 9Bake: Bake for 18–22 minutes until deep golden brown. Every oven is different, so start peeking at 18 minutes. You want real color here — pale medialunas are sad medialunas.
  10. 10Make the honey glaze: While the medialunas are baking, combine honey, warm water, and sugar in a small saucepan over low heat. Stir until the sugar dissolves, about 2 minutes. As soon as the medialunas come out of the oven, brush them generously with the warm glaze. It goes on glossy and sets into that signature sticky, shiny finish as they cool. Let them rest for at least 10 minutes before eating — if you can.
💡 Improv Tip

Cold butter is non-negotiable. If at any point your dough feels warm and the butter starts melting into it rather than staying in distinct pieces, just pop it in the fridge for 15 minutes and pick up where you left off. That's the whole secret to layers — keeping the butter cold enough to create steam pockets in the oven. Also, the honey glaze while hot is not optional. That sticky shine is what makes a medialuna a medialuna and not just a little croissant.
