Simple Calabaza en Tacha Recipe (Mexican Candy Pumpkin Done Right)

Published October 30, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple calabaza en tacha recipe — Mexican candy pumpkin braised low and slow in piloncillo syrup with cinnamon and orange — is one of those dishes that makes your whole kitchen smell like a hug. It's a traditional Mexican sweet that shows up around Día de los Muertos, but honestly once you taste it, you'll be making it all year long. Four ingredients, one pot, zero drama.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
55 mins
Total
65 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 3 lbs calabaza (Mexican pumpkin or West Indian pumpkin), cut into large wedges or chunks, seeds removed
  • 8 oz piloncillo (1 large cone or 2 small ones), roughly broken up
  • 2 cinnamon sticks
  • 3 whole cloves
  • 1 orange, zested and juiced (about 1/3 cup juice)
  • 1 cup water
  • Pinch of salt

Instructions

  1. 1Cut your calabaza into large wedges or 3-inch chunks. You don't need to peel it — the skin softens beautifully and holds everything together while it braises. If the skin is especially tough, score it a few times with a knife so the syrup can get in.
  2. 2In a wide, heavy-bottomed pot or deep skillet, combine the piloncillo, water, orange juice, orange zest, cinnamon sticks, cloves, and a pinch of salt. Set over medium heat and stir occasionally until the piloncillo fully dissolves into a rich, dark syrup — about 5 to 7 minutes. Your kitchen will start smelling incredible right about now.
  3. 3Nestle the calabaza pieces into the pot in a single layer, skin-side up if possible. Spoon some of the syrup over the top of each piece so they get coated right from the start.
  4. 4Cover the pot, reduce heat to medium-low, and let the calabaza braise for 35 to 40 minutes, basting with the syrup every 10 minutes or so. The pumpkin is ready when it's completely fork-tender and has soaked up that dark, spiced syrup throughout.
  5. 5Uncover the pot and increase heat to medium. Let everything bubble uncovered for another 8 to 10 minutes, basting constantly, until the syrup reduces into a thick glaze that clings to the pumpkin like candy. Watch it closely — you want glossy and sticky, not burnt.
  6. 6Serve warm or at room temperature. Spoon extra syrup from the pot over each piece. Traditionally served as-is, but it is also incredible alongside a scoop of vanilla ice cream, a spoonful of crema, or just a cup of black coffee.
💡 Improv Tip

Piloncillo is the real deal here — it's that dark, unrefined Mexican cane sugar sold in hard cones at Latin grocery stores and most large supermarkets. It has a deep, almost molasses-y flavor that regular brown sugar just can't replicate. That said, if you're truly in a pinch, use packed dark brown sugar and add a tiny drizzle of molasses. Also: calabaza is sold by the wedge or the pound at most Latin and Caribbean markets in Miami, so you don't have to buy a whole pumpkin — just grab exactly what you need.
