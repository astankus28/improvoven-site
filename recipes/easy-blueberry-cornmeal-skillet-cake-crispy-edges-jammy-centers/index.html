Easy Blueberry Cornmeal Skillet Cake (Crispy Edges, Jammy Centers)

Published September 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy blueberry cornmeal skillet cake is the kind of dessert that makes people think you really know what you're doing — when really, you just threw pantry staples into a cast iron pan and let the oven do the work. The cornmeal gives it this gorgeous golden crust with a slightly crispy edge, while the blueberries sink in and get all jammy and sweet in the middle. It's humble, it's beautiful, and it tastes like a bakery item that costs twelve dollars a slice.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1 cup yellow cornmeal
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup whole milk (or any milk you have)
  • 1/3 cup sour cream (or plain yogurt)
  • 1/4 cup unsalted butter, melted — plus a little extra for the skillet
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp lemon zest (optional but really worth it)
  • 1 1/2 cups fresh or frozen blueberries
  • 1 tbsp sugar for topping

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 375°F. Put a 10-inch cast iron skillet (or any oven-safe skillet) in the oven while it heats up — a hot pan is the secret to those crispy golden edges.
  2. 2In a large bowl, whisk together the cornmeal, flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt until evenly combined.
  3. 3In a separate bowl or big measuring cup, whisk together the eggs, milk, sour cream, melted butter, vanilla, and lemon zest if using. Mix until smooth.
  4. 4Pour the wet ingredients into the dry and stir gently with a spatula until just combined. A few lumps are totally fine — don't overwork it or the cake gets tough.
  5. 5Carefully pull the hot skillet out of the oven and add a small pat of butter, swirling it around to coat the bottom and slightly up the sides. It'll sizzle — that's what you want.
  6. 6Pour the batter into the hot buttered skillet and spread it out evenly with your spatula. Scatter the blueberries all over the top, then sprinkle the tablespoon of sugar evenly over everything.
  7. 7Bake for 22 to 26 minutes, until the edges are deep golden brown, the center is set (not jiggly), and a toothpick comes out with just a few moist crumbs. If your blueberries are releasing a lot of juice, check at 22 minutes and give it extra time if needed.
  8. 8Let it cool in the skillet for at least 10 minutes before slicing — this lets everything firm up and makes it way easier to cut. Serve warm, straight from the pan, with a dusting of powdered sugar, a scoop of vanilla ice cream, or a drizzle of honey.
💡 Improv Tip

Frozen blueberries work just as well as fresh here — don't thaw them first, just toss them straight onto the batter frozen. They'll release their juices as they bake and create these gorgeous purple swirls through the top. If you do use frozen, add 3 to 4 extra minutes to your bake time since they'll cool the batter down slightly.
