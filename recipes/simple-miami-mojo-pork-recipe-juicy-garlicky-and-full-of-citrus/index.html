Simple Miami Mojo Pork Recipe (Juicy, Garlicky, and Full of Citrus)

Published August 5, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple Miami mojo pork recipe is the kind of dish that makes your whole kitchen smell like a Cuban grandmother lives there — in the best possible way. Mojo is all about that punchy combo of sour citrus, loads of garlic, and earthy oregano, and it works like magic on a humble pork shoulder. Whether you're feeding a weeknight crowd or just want leftovers that actually get better the next day, this one belongs in your regular rotation.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
2 hrs 30 mins
Total
2 hrs 45 mins (plus optional overnight marinade)
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 3 to 3.5 lbs bone-in or boneless pork shoulder (also called pork butt)
  • 10 cloves garlic, minced or pressed
  • 1/2 cup fresh orange juice (about 2 oranges) — or bottled 100% OJ works fine
  • 1/4 cup fresh lime juice (about 3 limes)
  • 1/4 cup olive oil (or any neutral oil you have)
  • 2 teaspoons dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more for seasoning the meat
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon crushed red pepper flakes (optional but recommended)
  • 1/2 cup water or chicken broth (for the roasting pan)

Instructions

  1. 1Make your mojo marinade: In a bowl or large zip-lock bag, combine the minced garlic, orange juice, lime juice, olive oil, oregano, cumin, salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes. Stir or shake it together — it should smell absolutely incredible already.
  2. 2Score and season the pork: Pat the pork shoulder dry with paper towels. Using a sharp knife, cut 1-inch-deep slashes all over the meat — about 10 to 12 cuts. This isn't just for looks; it lets the mojo get deep into the pork and infuse every bite. Season the outside generously with a little extra salt and pepper.
  3. 3Marinate the pork: Place the pork in the bag or a container and pour the mojo all over it, making sure it gets into those slashes. Seal it up and let it marinate in the fridge for at least 2 hours — but if you can swing it, overnight is where the magic really happens. Don't stress if you're short on time though; even a 30-minute marinade makes a big difference.
  4. 4Preheat your oven to 325°F. Pull the pork out of the fridge about 20 minutes before cooking so it can take the edge off the cold — this helps it cook more evenly.
  5. 5Set up for roasting: Place the pork in a roasting pan, Dutch oven, or any oven-safe pot with a lid. Pour the remaining marinade right over the top, then add 1/2 cup of water or broth to the bottom of the pan. This keeps things moist and creates a little sauce situation as it roasts. Cover tightly with the lid or foil.
  6. 6Roast low and slow: Cook covered at 325°F for about 2 hours, until the pork is very tender and almost falling apart. At the 2-hour mark, remove the lid or foil, crank the oven up to 400°F, and roast uncovered for another 25 to 30 minutes to get that beautifully caramelized, golden-brown crust on top.
  7. 7Rest before serving: Take the pork out of the oven and let it rest, loosely tented with foil, for at least 10 to 15 minutes. This is non-negotiable — it lets the juices redistribute so every slice stays incredibly moist. Use two forks to pull it apart, or slice it if you prefer.
  8. 8Serve it up: Spoon some of those pan juices right over the pork — that concentrated mojo at the bottom of the pan is liquid gold. Serve alongside white rice, black beans, fried plantains, or just pile it onto a roll for the most legit Cuban-inspired sandwich you've ever made at home.
💡 Improv Tip

Bottled orange juice is 100% acceptable here — fresh is lovely, but the magic of mojo is really in the garlic-to-citrus ratio, not whether you squeezed the orange yourself. If your citrus is looking a little sad in the fridge, this is exactly the recipe to rescue them with.
