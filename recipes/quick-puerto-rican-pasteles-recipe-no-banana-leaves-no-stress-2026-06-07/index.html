Quick Puerto Rican Pasteles Recipe (No Banana Leaves, No Stress)

Published June 7, 2026 · Improv Oven

This quick Puerto Rican pasteles recipe is for everyone who loves the real thing but doesn't have all day to spend in the kitchen — we're talking a streamlined version that keeps the soul of the dish completely intact. We're using masa made from green bananas and yautía, stuffed with a killer sofrito-braised pork filling, and wrapped in foil instead of banana leaves so you can make these on a Tuesday night without losing your mind. Think of this as your abuela's recipe with a practical Miami apartment-kitchen makeover.

Prep
45 mins
Cook
1 hour
Total
1 hour 45 mins
Serves
12 pasteles

Ingredients

  • 4 green bananas, peeled and roughly chopped
  • 2 cups yautía (taro root), peeled and roughly chopped
  • 1/2 cup achiote oil (or vegetable oil with 1 tsp achiote powder stirred in)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 cup chicken broth (for masa consistency)
  • 1 lb pork shoulder, cut into small 1/2-inch cubes
  • 1/2 cup sofrito (store-bought or homemade)
  • 1 packet Sazón con achiote
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup tomato sauce
  • 1/4 cup pimento-stuffed olives, sliced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 12 sheets aluminum foil, cut into roughly 12x12-inch squares
  • Cooking twine or extra foil strips for tying

Instructions

  1. 1Make the pork filling first so it has time to cool. Heat olive oil in a medium skillet over medium-high heat. Season pork cubes with salt, pepper, oregano, and the Sazón packet. Sear the pork for about 4-5 minutes until browned on all sides.
  2. 2Add the sofrito and garlic to the pork and cook for 2 minutes until fragrant — your kitchen is about to smell incredible. Stir in the tomato sauce and olives, reduce heat to medium-low, cover, and let it simmer for 25-30 minutes until the pork is tender and the sauce is thick. Remove from heat and let it cool while you make the masa.
  3. 3For the masa, add the green bananas and yautía to a food processor. Pour in the achiote oil, salt, and chicken broth. Blend until you get a thick, smooth paste — it should be the consistency of very thick mashed potatoes. If it feels too stiff, add chicken broth one tablespoon at a time. Taste it and adjust salt.
  4. 4Set up your assembly station: foil squares laid flat, masa, filling, and a little extra achiote oil nearby. Lightly brush the center of each foil square with achiote oil — this prevents sticking and gives the outside of the pastel that beautiful orange color.
  5. 5Scoop about 3 heaping tablespoons of masa onto the center of a foil square and spread it into a rough oval shape about 1/4 inch thick. Don't spread it all the way to the edges — leave a border so you can fold everything up.
  6. 6Add 2 generous tablespoons of the pork filling right down the center of the masa oval. Now fold the foil up from the long sides first so the masa wraps around the filling and seals itself, then fold up the short ends tightly. You want a neat rectangular package — like a little gift. Repeat with all 12.
  7. 7Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Carefully lower the pasteles into the boiling water. They need to be fully submerged, so use a large pot and work in batches if needed. Boil for 45-50 minutes.
  8. 8Remove the pasteles with tongs and let them rest for 5 minutes before unwrapping — they firm up as they cool slightly and the masa will hold together much better. Carefully open the foil (watch the steam!) and serve immediately.
💡 Improv Tip

The masa texture is everything here — if your green bananas are on the slightly riper side (you see even a tiny bit of yellow), the masa will be softer and stickier. That's actually fine, just add a little less broth. The real trick though? Make the filling the night before. Cold filling is so much easier to handle when you're assembling, and the flavors deepen overnight in the fridge. Also, pasteles freeze beautifully still wrapped in their foil — boil them straight from frozen for 1 hour and you've got a future weeknight dinner handled.
