Best Cuban Sandwich Pressed Recipe (Crispy, Melty, and Totally Authentic)

Published September 24, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you've been searching for the best Cuban sandwich pressed recipe, you've landed in the right kitchen — this is the real deal, built with layers of mojo-marinated pork, deli ham, Swiss cheese, yellow mustard, and briny pickles, then pressed until golden and impossibly crispy. No fancy sandwich press required, just a heavy skillet and a little patience. This is Miami lunch culture in sandwich form, and once you make it at home, you'll wonder why you ever paid $14 for one at a café.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 loaf Cuban bread (or a soft Italian or French hoagie roll, split in half lengthwise)
  • 3/4 lb thinly sliced deli ham (honey or smoked both work great)
  • 3/4 lb thinly sliced roasted pork (leftover pernil, deli roast pork, or rotisserie pork all work)
  • 6 slices Swiss cheese
  • 1/2 cup dill pickle slices (thin rounds, not chips — you want coverage)
  • 3 tablespoons yellow mustard
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened
  • Salt and black pepper to taste

Instructions

  1. 1Set up your pressing station first: you'll need a large heavy skillet (cast iron is perfect) and another heavy pan or pot to press down on the sandwich. If you have a panini press, even better — but you absolutely don't need one.
  2. 2Slice your bread loaf lengthwise into 4 individual sandwich portions, about 6–7 inches each. Open each one like a book. If the bread is very thick and doughy in the middle, scoop out a little of the interior so the sandwich stays flat and presses evenly.
  3. 3Spread a generous layer of yellow mustard on both the top and bottom cut sides of each roll — don't skip this, the mustard is non-negotiable in an authentic Cuban. Season lightly with black pepper.
  4. 4Layer the sandwich from the bottom up: Swiss cheese first (it melts against the warm bread), then the ham (fold it so it mounds slightly), then the roasted pork, then the pickle slices laid flat in a single even layer. Close the sandwich and press down gently with your hand.
  5. 5Butter the outside of both the top and bottom of each sandwich generously with the softened butter. This is what gives you that gorgeous golden, shatteringly crispy crust.
  6. 6Heat your large skillet over medium heat. Once hot, add the sandwiches in batches — don't crowd the pan. Place your second heavy pan or a foil-wrapped brick directly on top of each sandwich and press down firmly. You want real pressure here.
  7. 7Cook for 3–4 minutes on the first side, pressing down occasionally, until the bottom is deep golden and crispy. Flip carefully, press again with the heavy pan, and cook another 3–4 minutes until the second side is equally golden and the cheese is fully melted.
  8. 8Remove from heat and let the sandwiches rest for 1–2 minutes before slicing — this helps the cheese set slightly so everything stays together. Slice diagonally and serve immediately while hot and crispy.
💡 Improv Tip

The single biggest mistake people make with a Cuban is going too hot, too fast — you want medium heat so the bread crisps up slowly while the cheese melts all the way through. Rushing it on high heat gives you burnt bread with cold cheese in the middle, and that's a heartbreak nobody needs at lunch. Low and slow pressure is your friend here.
