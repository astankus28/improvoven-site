What to Make with Pasta and Olive Oil: Aglio e Olio with a Miami Twist

Published November 10, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you're staring at a box of pasta and a bottle of olive oil and wondering what to make, you're about five ingredients away from one of the most satisfying meals in existence. Pasta aglio e olio — garlic, olive oil, pasta, and a little magic — is the kind of dish that sounds too simple to be this good, until you taste it. We're keeping the soul of the Italian classic but adding a little Miami heat with red pepper flakes, a squeeze of lime, and fresh cilantro because down here, we can't help ourselves.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb spaghetti or linguine
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • 8 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes (more if you like heat)
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more for pasta water
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 cup pasta water, reserved
  • Juice of 1/2 lime
  • 1/4 cup fresh parsley or cilantro, roughly chopped
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan or Pecorino Romano (optional but highly recommended)
  • Zest of 1/2 lime (optional, for extra brightness)

Instructions

  1. 1Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil — it should taste like the ocean. Cook your pasta according to package directions until just al dente. Before you drain it, scoop out at least 1/2 cup of that starchy pasta water and set it aside. This is liquid gold and you will need it.
  2. 2While the pasta cooks, heat the olive oil in a large skillet or wide saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the sliced garlic and let it cook slowly, stirring often, for about 4 to 5 minutes. You want it golden and fragrant, not brown and bitter — keep the heat gentle. The moment it starts smelling amazing in your kitchen, you're on the right track.
  3. 3Add the red pepper flakes to the garlic oil and let them bloom for about 30 seconds. This is where the Miami heat lives. Give it a quick stir and pull the pan off the heat for a second if the garlic looks like it's moving too fast.
  4. 4Drain your pasta and add it straight into the skillet with the garlic oil. Toss everything together over medium heat. Add the reserved pasta water a splash at a time — start with about 1/4 cup — and toss vigorously. The starchy water and olive oil will emulsify into a silky sauce that coats every strand. Keep tossing for about 1 to 2 minutes until it looks glossy and saucy, not dry.
  5. 5Squeeze in the lime juice, add the lime zest if you're using it, season with salt and black pepper, and toss again. Taste it. Adjust the salt, the heat, the lime — make it yours.
  6. 6Pull the pan off the heat and stir in the fresh parsley or cilantro. Plate it up and finish with grated Parmesan or Pecorino if you've got it. Serve immediately because pasta aglio e olio is at its absolute best the moment it hits the bowl.
💡 Improv Tip

The number one mistake people make with this dish is cranking up the heat and burning the garlic — once it's bitter, there's no coming back. Keep your flame low and patient, and treat that garlic like it's the most important thing in the pan (because it is). Also, don't skip saving the pasta water before you drain — seriously, set a reminder on your phone if you have to. That starchy water is what transforms this from 'oily noodles' to an actual silky, restaurant-worthy sauce.
