Crockpot Beef and Vegetable Stew (Microwave Feed Budget Recipe for Busy Weeknights)

Published August 23, 2026 · Improv Oven

This crockpot microwave feed budget recipe is the kind of dinner that works even when you forgot to plan — dump everything in the slow cooker in the morning and come home to a rich, hearty beef stew that feeds four for under ten bucks. The microwave shortcut here is a game-changer: steam your potatoes in five minutes flat before adding them so they actually absorb the broth instead of turning to mush. Warm, filling, and built entirely from affordable pantry staples and whatever vegetables are hanging on in your fridge.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
7 hrs (crockpot) + 5 mins (microwave prep)
Total
7 hrs 20 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs beef stew meat (or chuck roast cut into 1-inch chunks)
  • 3 medium potatoes, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 3 large carrots, peeled and sliced into coins
  • 2 stalks celery, sliced
  • 1 medium yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced (or 1 tsp garlic powder)
  • 2 cups beef broth (or 2 cups water + 1 bouillon cube)
  • 1 can (15 oz) diced tomatoes, undrained
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp dried rosemary
  • 1 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 2 tbsp cornstarch
  • 2 tbsp cold water
  • Optional: 1 cup frozen peas, added in the last 30 minutes

Instructions

  1. 1Start with the microwave shortcut: place your cubed potatoes in a microwave-safe bowl with 2 tablespoons of water. Cover loosely with a damp paper towel and microwave on high for 4–5 minutes until just barely fork-tender but not fully cooked through. This pre-steaming step keeps the potatoes from getting waterlogged and falling apart in the crockpot over the long cook — trust the process.
  2. 2While the potatoes cool slightly, layer your beef stew meat on the bottom of the crockpot. Season the meat with salt, pepper, garlic powder, and onion powder directly in the pot.
  3. 3Add the par-cooked potatoes, carrots, celery, and onion on top of the beef. Scatter the minced garlic over everything.
  4. 4In a small bowl or measuring cup, whisk together the beef broth, canned diced tomatoes, tomato paste, and Worcestershire sauce. Pour the whole mixture over the meat and vegetables.
  5. 5Sprinkle the dried thyme and rosemary over the top. Give everything a gentle stir just to distribute the liquid — don't overthink it. Put the lid on.
  6. 6Cook on LOW for 7–8 hours or HIGH for 4–5 hours. The beef is done when it pulls apart easily with a fork. If you're home, stir once halfway through.
  7. 7About 20 minutes before serving, make a quick slurry: whisk 2 tablespoons cornstarch with 2 tablespoons cold water in a small cup until completely smooth. Stir it directly into the stew, replace the lid, and let it cook on HIGH for another 15–20 minutes. This thickens the broth into a gorgeous gravy without any flour or fuss.
  8. 8If you're adding frozen peas, toss them in during this last 20-minute window — they just need to heat through.
  9. 9Taste and adjust salt and pepper before serving. Ladle into bowls and serve with crusty bread, over egg noodles, or totally on its own. This is the stew that makes the whole house smell incredible.
💡 Improv Tip

Beef stew meat is almost always cheaper per pound at the counter than pre-packaged, so ask — you might save a dollar or two right there. And if your crockpot runs hot (some do), check it at the 6-hour mark on LOW; you want the beef pull-apart tender, not dry. The other thing nobody tells you: this stew is genuinely better the next day, so if you have leftovers, you're actually winning.
