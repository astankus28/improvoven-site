Easy Peruvian Lomo Saltado Recipe for Weeknights

Published July 25, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Peruvian lomo saltado recipe is exactly what weeknight cooking should look like — big, bold flavors hitting the table in under 30 minutes. It's a classic stir-fry of beefy strips, blistered tomatoes, and onions in a savory soy-aji amarillo sauce, served right over rice and fries like they do it in Lima. Yes, fries ON the plate. No notes.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs flank steak or sirloin, sliced thin against the grain
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tbsp aji amarillo paste (or 1 tsp hot sauce + 1 tsp yellow mustard as a swap)
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil, divided (vegetable or canola)
  • 1 large red onion, cut into thick wedges
  • 3 roma tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • 2 cups cooked white rice, for serving
  • 2 cups frozen french fries, cooked per package instructions
  • Salt to taste
  • Lime wedges, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Get your frozen fries in the oven or air fryer first — they take the longest, and you want everything landing at the same time. Set them going before anything else touches a pan.
  2. 2Slice your steak as thin as you can manage, cutting against the grain. Season it with a pinch of salt and pepper. In a small bowl, whisk together the soy sauce, red wine vinegar, aji amarillo paste, and cumin. Set that sauce aside — it goes in fast at the end.
  3. 3Heat a large skillet or wok over high heat until it's genuinely hot — like, wait-an-extra-minute hot. Add 2 tablespoons of oil. Working in two batches if needed so you don't crowd the pan, sear the beef strips for about 60 to 90 seconds per side. You want char on the edges, not a steam situation. Pull the beef out and set it aside.
  4. 4In the same hot pan, add the remaining tablespoon of oil. Toss in the red onion wedges and let them sit undisturbed for 30 seconds to get a little color, then toss. Add the garlic and cook for another 30 seconds, stirring constantly so it doesn't burn.
  5. 5Add the tomato wedges to the pan. Toss everything together and cook for just about 1 minute — you want the tomatoes softened but still holding their shape, not dissolving into sauce.
  6. 6Return the beef to the pan and pour the soy-vinegar sauce over everything. Toss aggressively over high heat for about 60 seconds until the sauce coats everything and smells incredible. Taste and adjust salt if needed.
  7. 7Pull the pan off heat and fold in most of the cilantro. Serve immediately over white rice with the hot fries tucked right alongside or tumbled on top. Finish with the remaining cilantro and a squeeze of lime.
💡 Improv Tip

The two biggest secrets to lomo saltado are high heat and not crowding your pan. If you pile all the beef in at once on a home stove, it steams instead of sears and you lose those caramelized edges that make this dish. Cook the beef in batches, get that char, and the whole thing will taste like a restaurant made it. Also — don't skip the lime at the end. It wakes everything up.
