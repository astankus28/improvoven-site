Easy Caldo Tlalpeño Chicken Soup Recipe

Published July 7, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy caldo tlalpeño chicken soup recipe is one of those Mexican classics that sounds fancy but comes together with stuff you probably already have sitting around. It's smoky, a little spicy, hearty from the chickpeas, and deeply satisfying in a way that makes you feel like someone's abuela loves you. Perfect for a weeknight dinner or anytime your soul needs a warm hug in a bowl.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
35 mins
Total
45 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs bone-in chicken thighs (or boneless — whatever you've got)
  • 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 2 chipotle peppers in adobo sauce, minced (plus 1 tbsp of the sauce from the can)
  • 1 can (14 oz) diced fire-roasted tomatoes
  • 6 cups chicken broth (or water with 2 bouillon cubes)
  • 1 medium white onion, roughly chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 2 medium carrots, sliced into coins
  • 2 stalks celery, sliced
  • 1 dried or fresh epazote sprig (optional but authentic — swap with a little fresh cilantro if you can't find it)
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp dried oregano (Mexican oregano if you have it)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Fresh cilantro, sliced avocado, and lime wedges for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Heat a large pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat with a drizzle of oil. Season your chicken thighs generously with salt, pepper, and cumin. Sear them for about 3 minutes per side until they've got some golden color on them — you're building flavor here, so don't skip this part.
  2. 2Remove the chicken and set it aside. In the same pot, add the onion and garlic and sauté for 2 to 3 minutes until softened and fragrant. Scrape up any brown bits from the bottom — that's gold.
  3. 3Add the minced chipotle peppers, the tablespoon of adobo sauce, and the fire-roasted tomatoes. Stir everything together and let it cook for 2 minutes so the chipotle blooms and the flavors start to come together.
  4. 4Pour in the chicken broth and bring it up to a boil. Nestle the seared chicken thighs back into the pot along with the carrots, celery, oregano, and epazote or cilantro if using.
  5. 5Reduce heat to medium-low, cover, and simmer for 20 minutes until the chicken is fully cooked through and tender. Pull the chicken out and shred it with two forks — it should fall apart easily.
  6. 6Return the shredded chicken to the pot and stir in the drained chickpeas. Simmer uncovered for another 5 minutes so the chickpeas soak up all that smoky broth.
  7. 7Squeeze in the lime juice and taste for salt — adjust as needed. Ladle into bowls and top with fresh cilantro, sliced avocado, and an extra lime wedge on the side. Serve immediately and accept all compliments graciously.
💡 Improv Tip

The canned chipotles in adobo are doing the heavy lifting here — one can goes a long way. After you use two peppers, freeze the rest in a small zip-lock bag or ice cube tray. Future you will be very grateful when you need that smoky kick for tacos, rice, or marinades without buying a whole new can.
