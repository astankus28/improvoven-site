Quick Salmon Recipe Under 20 Minutes: Honey Soy Pan-Seared Salmon

Published July 15, 2026 · Improv Oven

This quick salmon recipe under 20 minutes is the weeknight dinner your future self will thank you for — four ingredients, one pan, zero stress. The glaze hits that perfect sweet-salty balance, the skin gets crispy, and the whole thing comes together faster than delivery ever could. Serve it over rice, noodles, or a pile of greens and you've got a real dinner on the table before you've even changed out of your work clothes.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
12 mins
Total
17 mins
Serves
2

Ingredients

  • 2 salmon fillets (about 6 oz each, skin-on if possible)
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, grated (or a pinch of ground ginger from the pantry)
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil (vegetable, canola, or avocado)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Optional garnish: sliced scallions, sesame seeds, a squeeze of lime

Instructions

  1. 1Pat your salmon fillets completely dry with a paper towel — this is not optional. Dry fish = crispy crust. Season lightly with salt and pepper on both sides.
  2. 2In a small bowl, whisk together the soy sauce, honey, sesame oil, garlic, and ginger. Taste it — it should be punchy and a little sweet. Set it aside.
  3. 3Heat a skillet (stainless, cast iron, or nonstick) over medium-high heat until it's genuinely hot — about 2 minutes. Add your neutral oil and swirl to coat.
  4. 4Place the salmon skin-side UP in the pan. Don't touch it. Let it cook for 4 minutes until a golden crust forms on the bottom. Resist the urge to peek or press — just let the heat do its thing.
  5. 5Flip the salmon so it's skin-side down. Pour the honey soy glaze over the fillets and let it bubble into the pan. Cook for another 3 to 4 minutes, occasionally spooning the pan glaze back over the top of the fish, until the salmon is just cooked through and the glaze has thickened and gone glossy.
  6. 6Pull the pan off the heat. The salmon should feel firm at the edges and just barely opaque in the thickest part of the center — that's exactly right. Let it rest in the pan for one minute, then plate it up with whatever you've got: steamed rice, soba noodles, cucumber slices, or a handful of baby spinach wilted in the hot pan after the salmon comes out.
  7. 7Finish with sliced scallions, a few sesame seeds, and a squeeze of lime if you have it. Eat immediately.
💡 Improv Tip

The number one killer of good salmon is overcooking it — pull it off the heat when the center still looks just slightly underdone, because it'll keep cooking from residual heat while it rests. If your fillets are thick (over an inch), give them an extra minute per side. Thin? Check early. The glaze burns fast once it's in the pan, so don't walk away during that last step.
