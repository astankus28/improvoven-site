Homemade Fresh Salsa Recipe (Better Than Anything in a Jar)

Published May 28, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade salsa recipe with fresh tomatoes is the kind of thing that makes people ask you what restaurant you ordered from — and you get to smile and say you made it yourself. It comes together in about 10 minutes, costs almost nothing, and works on eggs, tacos, rice bowls, tortilla chips, or honestly just a spoon. Once you make it fresh, you will never go back to that little glass jar again.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
10 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 4 medium ripe roma tomatoes, roughly chopped
  • 1/2 white onion, roughly chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 1-2 jalapeños, seeds removed if you want less heat
  • 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, stems and all
  • juice of 2 limes
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • pinch of sugar (optional, balances acidity)

Instructions

  1. 1Roughly chop your tomatoes, onion, jalapeño, and garlic — nothing needs to be perfect here because it's all going in the blender or food processor anyway. You just want the pieces small enough to blend evenly.
  2. 2Add the tomatoes, onion, garlic, and jalapeño to your blender or food processor first. Pulse about 4 to 5 times — you want chunky, not smooth. If you blend it too long you'll end up with tomato juice, so take it easy.
  3. 3Add the cilantro, lime juice, salt, and cumin. Pulse another 3 to 4 times until everything is combined but still has texture and body. Taste it right here — this is the moment.
  4. 4Adjust your seasoning. Need more salt? Add it. Too sharp from the lime? Add that pinch of sugar. Want more heat? Toss in the jalapeño seeds you saved. This is your salsa, make it yours.
  5. 5Pour into a bowl and let it sit for at least 5 minutes before serving. That little rest lets all the flavors introduce themselves to each other. Serve immediately or refrigerate in a sealed container for up to 5 days — though it never lasts that long.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret move here is using roma tomatoes instead of regular round tomatoes. Romas have less water and more flesh, which means your salsa stays thick and scoopable instead of turning into a puddle at the bottom of the bowl. If your tomatoes are a little bland (we've all been there), a tiny pinch of sugar and an extra squeeze of lime will wake them right up.
