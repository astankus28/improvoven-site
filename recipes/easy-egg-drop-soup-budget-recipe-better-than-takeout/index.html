Easy Egg Drop Soup Budget Recipe (Better Than Takeout)

Published October 7, 2026 · Improv Oven

This egg drop soup budget recipe proves you don't need to order out to get that silky, restaurant-style bowl — just a handful of pantry staples and about 15 minutes on the stove. We're talking ribbony eggs in a golden, ginger-kissed broth that tastes way more complex than it has any right to. It's light enough for lunch, comforting enough for dinner, and cheap enough to make twice this week.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
10 mins
Total
15 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 cups low-sodium chicken broth (or vegetable broth)
  • 3 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons cornstarch
  • 3 tablespoons cold water
  • 1/2 teaspoon fresh grated ginger (or 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger)
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1/4 teaspoon white pepper (or black pepper)
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric (for that golden color — optional but worth it)
  • 2 green onions, thinly sliced
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

  1. 1Pour the chicken broth into a medium saucepan and bring it to a gentle simmer over medium heat. Stir in the soy sauce, garlic powder, ginger, white pepper, sesame oil, and turmeric if you're using it. Let that simmer for about 2 minutes so the flavors can bloom and the broth picks up that beautiful golden color.
  2. 2In a small bowl, whisk together the cornstarch and cold water until completely smooth with no lumps — this is your slurry. Pour it slowly into the simmering broth while stirring constantly. You'll see the broth thicken up slightly within about 30 seconds. That light body is what makes egg drop soup feel silky instead of watery.
  3. 3In a separate small bowl or measuring cup, beat the three eggs well until the yolks and whites are fully combined. Reduce the heat to medium-low — you want the broth simmering gently, not boiling hard.
  4. 4Here's the fun part: hold a fork or chopstick over the pot and slowly pour the beaten eggs through the tines of the fork in a thin, steady stream while stirring the broth in slow circles with your other hand. The eggs will cook almost instantly into delicate ribbons as they hit the hot broth. If you pour too fast, you get egg chunks; too slow and you get lacy wisps — both are still delicious, so don't stress it.
  5. 5Taste the broth and adjust seasoning with salt or a little more soy sauce. Ladle into bowls, scatter sliced green onions over the top, and serve immediately. A few drops of extra sesame oil on top right before serving is never a bad idea.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to those gorgeous silky egg ribbons is broth temperature — too hot and the eggs seize up into rubbery chunks, too cool and they just kind of float around sad. You want a gentle simmer, not a rolling boil. And don't skip the cornstarch slurry — it's what gives the broth that restaurant-quality body that makes the whole bowl feel luxurious instead of like sad watered-down broth.
