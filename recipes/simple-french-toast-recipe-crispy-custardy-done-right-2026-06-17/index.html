Simple French Toast Recipe (Crispy, Custardy, Done Right)

Published June 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple French toast recipe is the kind of thing you can pull off on a Tuesday morning with stuff you already have sitting around — eggs, bread, milk, done. It hits that perfect sweet spot between crispy on the outside and soft and custardy in the middle, and honestly it slaps harder than anything you'd pay $18 for at brunch.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
20 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 thick slices bread (Texas toast, brioche, or whatever you've got)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/4 cup whole milk (or any milk you have)
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon butter (for the pan)
  • Pinch of salt
  • Maple syrup or powdered sugar for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Crack your eggs into a wide shallow bowl — a pasta bowl or a pie dish works great here. Add the milk, vanilla, cinnamon, sugar, and pinch of salt. Whisk it all together until it looks smooth and the cinnamon is evenly mixed in, about 30 seconds.
  2. 2Set a skillet or griddle over medium heat. You want it properly hot before you start — give it about 2 minutes. Drop in the butter and let it melt and get a little foamy but not brown.
  3. 3Dip one slice of bread into your egg mixture, let it soak for about 15 to 20 seconds per side. You want it coated but not so soaked it falls apart. Thick bread is your friend here.
  4. 4Lay the soaked slice right into the buttered pan. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes without touching it — let that golden crust form. Flip it and cook another 2 minutes on the other side until both sides are deep golden brown.
  5. 5Repeat with the remaining slices, adding a little more butter between batches as needed. Keep finished slices warm in a 200°F oven on a baking sheet while you cook the rest.
  6. 6Serve hot with maple syrup, a dusting of powdered sugar, or fresh fruit if you've got it. A little squeeze of lime on top is very Miami and very good — try it.
💡 Improv Tip

Stale bread is actually better than fresh here — it soaks up the custard without getting mushy and gives you that crispy outside you want. If your bread is fresh, pop the slices in the toaster for 30 seconds first to dry them out a little. Game changer.
