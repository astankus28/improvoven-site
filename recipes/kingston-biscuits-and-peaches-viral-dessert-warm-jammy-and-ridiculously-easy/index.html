Kingston Biscuits and Peaches Viral Dessert (Warm, Jammy, and Ridiculously Easy)

Published August 14, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you've been seeing the Kingston biscuits peaches viral dessert all over your feed and wondering if it's actually worth making — yes, it absolutely is, and it comes together in under 30 minutes with ingredients you probably already have. Buttery, flaky biscuits get baked right on top of caramelized cinnamon peaches spiked with a little rum and brown sugar, giving you something that tastes like a cobbler and a shortcake had a gorgeous baby with a Caribbean passport. This is the kind of lazy-genius dessert that makes people think you spent way more effort than you did.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
22 mins
Total
32 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1 can (29 oz) sliced peaches in juice or syrup, drained (or 3 cups fresh or frozen sliced peaches)
  • 3 tablespoons brown sugar, divided
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg
  • 2 tablespoons dark rum or coconut rum (optional but highly encouraged)
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 can (16 oz) store-bought refrigerator biscuits (8-count, any brand)
  • 1 tablespoon melted butter for brushing
  • Pinch of salt
  • Whipped cream or vanilla ice cream for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 375°F. Pull out a 9-inch cast iron skillet or any oven-safe skillet — this is your whole cooking vessel, so make it a good one.
  2. 2Set the skillet over medium heat and melt 1 tablespoon of butter. Add the drained peaches, 2 tablespoons of brown sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, and a pinch of salt. Stir gently and let the peaches cook for about 4–5 minutes until they start to soften and the sugar turns into a glossy, bubbling sauce.
  3. 3If you're using rum, pour it in now and let it cook for about 1 minute so the alcohol cooks off slightly but the flavor stays. Stir in the vanilla extract. Remove the skillet from heat.
  4. 4Open your biscuit can and arrange the biscuits on top of the peach mixture in a single layer — they don't need to cover every inch, the gaps are part of the charm and let the peachy sauce bubble up beautifully.
  5. 5Brush the tops of the biscuits with the melted butter and sprinkle the remaining 1 tablespoon of brown sugar evenly over them.
  6. 6Slide the skillet into the preheated oven and bake for 15–18 minutes, until the biscuits are deeply golden on top and cooked through. If you poke one with a toothpick and it comes out clean, you're done.
  7. 7Let it cool for just 3–4 minutes — enough so you don't burn your mouth, but you definitely want to serve this warm. Spoon into bowls, making sure everyone gets a biscuit and a generous scoop of those caramelized peaches. Top with whipped cream or a scoop of vanilla ice cream and watch it disappear.
💡 Improv Tip

Canned peaches in syrup will make this sweeter, so cut the brown sugar down to 1.5 tablespoons total if you go that route. Peaches in juice are the move if you want more control over the sweetness. And if you want to lean even harder into that Kingston vibe, swap the vanilla ice cream for a scoop of coconut ice cream — total game changer.
