Easy Flan Recipe (Silky, Classic, and So Much Easier Than You Think)

Published October 11, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy flan recipe is the one you've been putting off making because you thought it was complicated — it's not, I promise. With just five pantry staples and a water bath, you get that impossibly silky custard and glossy caramel sauce that tastes like it came from your abuela's kitchen. This is classic Cuban-style flan, the kind that wobbles on the plate in the most satisfying way possible.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
55 mins
Total
1 hr 10 mins (plus 2 hrs chilling)
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar (for the caramel)
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • pinch of salt

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 325°F and set a kettle of water on to boil — you'll need it for the water bath later. Get out a 9-inch round cake pan or a 1.5-quart baking dish and set it nearby.
  2. 2Make the caramel: Add the sugar and water to a small saucepan over medium heat. Don't stir — just swirl the pan gently if you need to. Let it cook until the sugar melts and turns a deep amber color, about 8 to 10 minutes. Watch it closely once it starts changing color because it goes from perfect to burnt fast. The moment it hits that rich golden-brown, immediately pour it into the bottom of your baking dish and tilt the pan to coat the bottom evenly. It will harden quickly — that's exactly what you want.
  3. 3Make the custard: In a blender (or a large bowl with a whisk), combine the eggs, sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, vanilla, and salt. Blend or whisk until completely smooth, about 30 seconds. Try not to create too many bubbles — if you're whisking by hand, go slow and steady.
  4. 4Pour the custard mixture gently over the hardened caramel in the baking dish. If you see bubbles on the surface, pop them with a toothpick or the back of a spoon — this keeps the top of your flan smooth.
  5. 5Set up the water bath: Place your flan dish inside a larger roasting pan or baking dish. Carefully pour the hot boiled water into the outer pan until it comes about halfway up the sides of the flan dish. This gentle, even heat is what gives flan its signature silky texture.
  6. 6Carefully transfer the whole setup to the oven and bake for 50 to 55 minutes. The flan is done when the edges are set and the center still has a slight jiggle — like Jell-O, not like liquid. It will firm up more as it cools.
  7. 7Remove the flan dish from the water bath and let it cool at room temperature for about 30 minutes. Then cover it tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, or overnight. Cold flan unmolds much more cleanly, so patience pays off here.
  8. 8To unmold: Run a thin knife or offset spatula around the edges of the flan to loosen it. Place a rimmed serving plate (you want those sides to catch the caramel sauce) upside down over the dish. Hold both together and flip in one confident motion. Give it a moment, and you'll hear it release. Lift the dish and watch all that gorgeous caramel pour down over the top. Slice and serve cold.
💡 Improv Tip

The caramel is the only part that requires your full attention — once the sugar starts turning color, don't walk away, don't check your phone, don't do anything. Thirty seconds is the difference between golden perfection and a bitter, burnt pan. If your caramel seizes up before you coat the bottom of the pan, don't panic — it'll melt back into liquid sauce when the flan bakes. Also: overnight chilling is genuinely worth it. A flan chilled for 8 hours unmolds cleaner and tastes richer than one rushed out after 2.
