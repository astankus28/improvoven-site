Savory French Onion Soup with Buttered Bread — A $20 Budget Recipe for Four

Published October 6, 2026 · Improv Oven

This classic French onion soup with buttered bread is the kind of $20 budget recipe that genuinely feels like you splurged. Deeply caramelized onions, a rich savory broth, and slices of buttery toasted bread drowning under melted cheese — it's old-world comfort food that stretches pantry staples into something extraordinary. If you've ever stared at a pile of onions and a forgotten loaf and wondered what to do, this is your answer.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
35 mins
Total
45 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
  • 4 cups beef broth (or vegetable broth)
  • 2 cups water
  • 4 thick slices crusty bread (French bread, sourdough, or any sturdy loaf)
  • 2 tablespoons butter, softened (for spreading on bread)
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded Gruyère, Swiss, or mozzarella cheese

Instructions

  1. 1Set a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the 3 tablespoons of butter and the olive oil and let it melt together — the oil keeps the butter from burning.
  2. 2Add all your sliced onions at once. They'll look like way too many but don't worry, they cook down dramatically. Stir them to coat in the fat, then let them sit mostly undisturbed for about 5 minutes before stirring again.
  3. 3Sprinkle in the sugar, salt, and black pepper. Keep cooking the onions over medium to medium-low heat, stirring every few minutes, for about 25 minutes total. You want them deeply golden and jammy — not just translucent. This is where the flavor lives, so don't rush it.
  4. 4Add the minced garlic and dried thyme and stir for about 1 minute until fragrant.
  5. 5Pour in the Worcestershire sauce and stir to scrape up any browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pot — that's pure flavor. Then pour in the broth and water. Bring everything to a gentle simmer and let it cook for 10 minutes so the flavors meld.
  6. 6While the soup simmers, butter your bread slices generously on one side. Toast them butter-side down in a dry skillet over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes until golden and crispy. Set aside.
  7. 7Preheat your oven broiler to high. Ladle the hot soup into oven-safe bowls or ramekins. Place one slice of buttered toast on top of each bowl — buttered side up is fine, it doesn't matter much here. Pile a generous mound of shredded cheese over each piece of bread.
  8. 8Place the bowls on a baking sheet and slide them under the broiler for 2 to 4 minutes, watching closely, until the cheese is bubbling, browned in spots, and gloriously melted. Use oven mitts — the bowls will be very hot. Serve immediately.
💡 Improv Tip

The caramelization step is everything here — if your onions look pale and sad at the 10-minute mark, that's normal. Keep going. The moment they turn that deep amber color and smell almost sweet, you've unlocked the whole dish. No Gruyère? Swiss works beautifully, and mozzarella gives you that epic cheese pull. Don't skip buttering the bread before toasting — it's what keeps it from turning into a soggy raft in the soup.
