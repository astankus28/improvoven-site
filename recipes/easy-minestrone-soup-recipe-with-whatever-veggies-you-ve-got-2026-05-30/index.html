Easy Minestrone Soup Recipe (With Whatever Veggies You've Got)

Published May 30, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy minestrone soup recipe is basically the cure for that 'nothing to eat' feeling when your fridge is half-empty and your pantry is doing the most. It's hearty, loaded with vegetables, and comes together in about 30 minutes — no fancy ingredients, no stress. Think of it as Italian comfort food with a little Miami improvisation energy.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 small onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 medium carrots, sliced into coins
  • 2 stalks celery, chopped
  • 1 zucchini, diced (or whatever vegetable you have around)
  • 1 can (15 oz) diced tomatoes
  • 1 can (15 oz) cannellini beans or kidney beans, drained and rinsed
  • 4 cups vegetable broth (or chicken broth — use what you've got)
  • 1 cup small pasta like ditalini, elbows, or broken spaghetti
  • 1 teaspoon dried Italian seasoning
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 1 cup fresh or frozen spinach or kale
  • Parmesan rind (optional but highly recommended if you have one)
  • Grated Parmesan for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion, carrots, and celery and cook for about 4-5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion softens and everything smells amazing.
  2. 2Add the garlic and cook for another 60 seconds — just until fragrant. Don't let it burn or you'll be starting over, mija.
  3. 3Stir in the zucchini, Italian seasoning, and smoked paprika. Let that cook together for 2 minutes so the spices bloom and the zucchini gets a little color.
  4. 4Pour in the diced tomatoes (with all the juice from the can), the drained beans, and the broth. If you've got a Parmesan rind hanging out in your fridge, throw it in now — it adds a depth of flavor that'll make people think you cooked this for hours.
  5. 5Bring the soup to a boil, then add the pasta. Reduce the heat to a steady simmer and cook for 8-10 minutes, or until the pasta is tender but not mushy.
  6. 6Stir in the spinach or kale and let it wilt into the soup for about 1-2 minutes. Taste and adjust salt and pepper to your liking.
  7. 7Fish out the Parmesan rind if you used one. Ladle the soup into bowls and finish with a generous shower of grated Parmesan. Serve with crusty bread if you've got it, or crackers — no judgment here.
💡 Improv Tip

Minestrone is a refrigerator blindness CHAMPION — swap in green beans, potatoes, corn, bell pepper, or leftover cooked chicken. The only rule is broth plus beans plus pasta equals happiness. Also, if you're making this ahead, cook the pasta separately and add it per bowl so it doesn't turn to mush in the leftovers. You're welcome.
