Simple Ricotta Lemon Pancakes for Breakfast (Fluffy, Light, and Ready in 30 Minutes)

Published August 26, 2026 · Improv Oven

These simple ricotta lemon pancakes make the kind of breakfast that feels fancy but comes together with stuff you probably already have in your fridge. The ricotta melts into the batter and gives you this impossibly light, almost creamy texture — and the lemon zest wakes the whole thing up without being fussy. No buttermilk, no fancy equipment, just a bowl, a whisk, and a hot pan.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup whole-milk or part-skim ricotta cheese
  • 2 large eggs, separated
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice (about half a lemon)
  • 1 teaspoon lemon zest (from the same lemon)
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup whole milk (or whatever milk you have)
  • Butter or neutral oil for the pan
  • Powdered sugar, maple syrup, or fresh berries for serving (optional but encouraged)

Instructions

  1. 1Separate your eggs into two bowls — yolks in a large bowl, whites in a medium bowl. This is the one slightly fancy step and it is absolutely worth it. Set the whites aside for now.
  2. 2Add the ricotta, lemon juice, lemon zest, sugar, and vanilla to the yolks. Whisk everything together until smooth and combined. It should look like a pale, lemony cream. Take a little sniff — that's the smell of a good morning.
  3. 3Add the flour, baking powder, and salt right into the ricotta mixture. Pour in the milk. Stir gently until just combined — a few small lumps are totally fine. Overmixing is the enemy of fluffy pancakes, so put the whisk down early.
  4. 4Using a clean whisk or hand mixer, beat the egg whites until they form soft peaks — when you lift the whisk, the peak should curl over gently, not stand straight up. This takes about 2 minutes by hand or 60 seconds with a mixer. Don't skip this step; it's what makes these pancakes float.
  5. 5Fold the beaten egg whites into the batter in two additions. Use a spatula and a gentle hand — fold from the bottom up, rotating the bowl. You want to keep as much air in there as possible. A few white streaks left in the batter are perfectly okay.
  6. 6Heat a non-stick skillet or griddle over medium-low heat. Add a small pat of butter or a drizzle of oil and let it melt. The pan is ready when a drop of water flicked onto the surface dances and evaporates.
  7. 7Pour about 1/4 cup of batter per pancake onto the pan. Cook until the edges look set and a few bubbles appear on the surface, about 2 to 3 minutes. Flip gently — ricotta pancakes are more delicate than standard pancakes — and cook another 1 to 2 minutes until golden on the bottom.
  8. 8Transfer finished pancakes to a warm oven (200°F) while you work through the rest of the batter. Serve stacked with a dusting of powdered sugar, a drizzle of maple syrup, or a handful of fresh berries if you've got them. A little honey works great too.
💡 Improv Tip

If your ricotta looks watery when you open the container, drain it through a fine mesh strainer for 5 minutes before mixing — excess moisture can make the batter too loose. Also, medium-low heat is your friend here. Ricotta pancakes brown faster than regular ones, so resist the urge to crank the heat or you'll get dark outsides and a gooey middle. Low and slow wins the race.
