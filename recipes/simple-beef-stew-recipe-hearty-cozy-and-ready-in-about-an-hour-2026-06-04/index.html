Simple Beef Stew Recipe (Hearty, Cozy, and Ready in About an Hour)

Published June 4, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple beef stew recipe is the kind of thing your abuela would have simmering on the back burner all Sunday — rich, thick, and loaded with tender beef and vegetables that taste like they took way more effort than they did. We're keeping it budget-friendly and pantry-smart, using humble ingredients that turn into something seriously comforting. If you've got a pot and a little patience, you've got dinner.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
1 hr 15 mins
Total
1 hr 30 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs beef chuck, cut into 1.5-inch cubes
  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 medium yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 3 medium carrots, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 3 medium Yukon Gold potatoes, cut into 1.5-inch chunks
  • 2 celery stalks, sliced
  • 3 cups beef broth (or water with 2 bouillon cubes)
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tablespoon cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons cold water (optional, for thickening)
  • Fresh parsley or cilantro for garnish

Instructions

  1. 1Pat your beef chunks completely dry with paper towels — this is the move that gets you that beautiful brown crust instead of gray, sad meat. Season all over with salt, pepper, garlic powder, and cumin.
  2. 2Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a large heavy pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat until it shimmers. Working in batches so you don't crowd the pan, sear the beef on all sides until deeply browned, about 3-4 minutes per side. Don't rush this — the brown bits on the bottom of that pot are pure flavor gold. Remove the beef and set aside.
  3. 3Add the remaining tablespoon of oil to the same pot and drop the heat to medium. Toss in your onion and celery and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and starting to turn golden, about 4-5 minutes. Add the minced garlic and cook for another minute until fragrant.
  4. 4Stir in the tomato paste and let it cook with the vegetables for about 2 minutes, stirring constantly. It'll deepen in color and smell incredible — that's exactly what you want.
  5. 5Pour in the beef broth and water, scraping up all those browned bits from the bottom of the pot with a wooden spoon. Those bits are basically free seasoning — don't leave them behind.
  6. 6Return the seared beef to the pot along with the bay leaf and Worcestershire sauce. Bring everything to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover, and let it simmer for 45 minutes.
  7. 7After 45 minutes, add your carrots and potatoes. Give it a stir, cover again, and continue simmering for another 25-30 minutes until the vegetables are fork-tender and the beef practically falls apart when you look at it.
  8. 8If you want a thicker stew, stir in the cornstarch slurry now and let it cook uncovered for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the broth tightens up into a glossy, rich gravy. Taste and adjust salt as needed.
  9. 9Fish out the bay leaf, ladle into bowls, and hit it with fresh parsley or cilantro. Serve as-is or over white rice if you want to stretch it further — either way, nobody's leaving the table hungry.
💡 Improv Tip

Chuck is your best friend for stew — don't let anyone talk you into a leaner cut. The fat and connective tissue in chuck breaks down during that low-and-slow simmer and turns into the silky, rich texture that makes a good stew taste like it simmered all day. Leaner cuts will just go tough on you. And honestly, if you want to make this even better the next day, let it cool and stick it in the fridge overnight — stew always tastes more alive after the flavors have had time to get to know each other.
