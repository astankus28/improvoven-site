Quick Nicaraguan Gallo Pinto Breakfast (Ready in 30 Minutes)

Published June 6, 2026 · Improv Oven

This quick Nicaraguan gallo pinto breakfast is the kind of morning meal that makes you feel like someone's abuela is taking care of you — even if it's just you and a skillet at 7am. Gallo pinto is Nicaragua's national dish for a reason: rice and beans cooked together with aromatics until they're deeply savory and satisfying, and it costs almost nothing to make. Pair it with eggs and you've got a full, energizing breakfast that'll keep you going until way past lunch.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked white rice (day-old rice works best)
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained — reserve the liquid
  • 1/4 cup reserved black bean liquid
  • 1/2 medium white onion, finely diced
  • 1/2 green bell pepper, finely diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil or any neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon Salsa Lizano (or Worcestershire sauce as a swap)
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Small handful of fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • 4 large eggs (fried, scrambled, or however you like them)
  • Optional: sour cream or crema, sliced avocado, or fried plantains to serve

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the oil in a large skillet or sauté pan over medium heat. Add the diced onion and green pepper and cook for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and just starting to turn golden at the edges.
  2. 2Add the minced garlic and cook for 1 more minute, stirring constantly so it doesn't burn. Your kitchen should smell incredible right now — that's the sign you're doing it right.
  3. 3Add the drained black beans to the pan along with the 1/4 cup of reserved bean liquid, the Salsa Lizano (or Worcestershire), and the cumin. Stir everything together and let it cook for about 3 minutes so the beans absorb all those flavors.
  4. 4Add the cooked rice and fold it gently into the bean mixture. You want the rice and beans fully combined and the whole thing to look like one unified, fragrant mass — not two separate things sharing a pan. Cook for another 5 to 7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the mixture is heated through and slightly crispy in spots. Those crispy bits are gold, don't avoid them.
  5. 5Taste and season with salt and black pepper. Stir in the fresh cilantro, then remove from heat.
  6. 6In a separate pan, cook your eggs however you like them. Fried eggs with a runny yolk are traditional and honestly perfect here — the yolk becomes a little sauce when it breaks over the gallo pinto.
  7. 7Plate the gallo pinto and top with the eggs. Add sliced avocado, a spoonful of crema, or fried plantains on the side if you have them. Serve immediately and enjoy every bite.
💡 Improv Tip

The real secret to gallo pinto is using day-old rice — freshly cooked rice is too moist and will make everything clump together and steam instead of fry. If you only have fresh rice, spread it on a baking sheet and let it cool uncovered in the fridge for 20 minutes while you prep everything else. Also, Salsa Lizano is the ingredient that makes this taste authentically Nicaraguan — it's a tangy, slightly sweet Central American condiment and you can find it at most Latin grocery stores or online. If you're in Miami, check any Nicaraguan or Costa Rican market and you'll find it no problem. Worcestershire works in a pinch but if you try it once with Lizano, you'll always keep a bottle in your pantry.
