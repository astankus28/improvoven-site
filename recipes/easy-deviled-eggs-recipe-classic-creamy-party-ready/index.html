Easy Deviled Eggs Recipe (Classic, Creamy, Party-Ready)

Published November 28, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy deviled eggs recipe is the one you'll come back to every single time — simple ingredients, big flavor, and ready in under 30 minutes. The filling is smooth, tangy, and just a little mustardy in the best way. Whether you're making these for a holiday spread, a potluck, or honestly just a Tuesday snack, they disappear faster than anything else on the table.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
12 mins
Total
27 mins
Serves
12 deviled egg halves (6 whole eggs)

Ingredients

  • 6 large eggs
  • 3 tablespoons mayonnaise
  • 1 teaspoon yellow mustard (or Dijon for a sharper bite)
  • 1 teaspoon white vinegar or apple cider vinegar
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • Paprika, for dusting on top
  • Optional: 1 teaspoon sweet pickle relish for a little sweetness
  • Optional: fresh chives or parsley, finely chopped, for garnish

Instructions

  1. 1Place your 6 eggs in a single layer in a saucepan and cover with cold water by about an inch. Bring to a full boil over medium-high heat, then immediately remove from heat, cover the pan, and let the eggs sit for exactly 11 minutes. This gives you fully cooked yolks with no gray ring.
  2. 2While the eggs cook, prepare an ice bath — fill a large bowl with cold water and a generous handful of ice cubes. When the 11 minutes are up, transfer the eggs straight into the ice bath and let them chill for at least 5 minutes. This stops the cooking and makes peeling so much easier.
  3. 3Peel the eggs gently under a thin stream of cold running water — the water gets under the membrane and helps the shell slide off. Slice each egg in half lengthwise with a sharp knife wiped clean between cuts for neater edges.
  4. 4Pop the yolks out into a medium bowl. Set the whites on a plate or serving platter, hollow side up.
  5. 5Add the mayonnaise, mustard, vinegar, salt, and pepper to the yolks. If you're using pickle relish, add it now. Mash everything together with a fork first to break up the yolks, then switch to a whisk or stir vigorously until the filling is completely smooth and creamy with no lumps. Taste and adjust — more mustard for tang, more mayo for creaminess, a pinch more salt if it needs it.
  6. 6Spoon the filling into the egg white halves. You can use a small spoon for a rustic look, or transfer the filling to a zip-top bag, snip a small corner off, and pipe it in for a neater, slightly fancier result.
  7. 7Dust each egg lightly with paprika — don't skip this, it's not just decorative, it adds a gentle smoky note. Garnish with chives or parsley if you have them. Serve immediately or refrigerate uncovered for up to 2 hours, then cover loosely with plastic wrap until ready to serve.
💡 Improv Tip

Cold eggs from the fridge actually peel better after boiling than room-temperature eggs do — the temperature shock helps the membrane separate from the shell. And if your filling feels a little stiff or the yolks were on the dry side, just add mayo half a teaspoon at a time until it loosens up to that silky, pipeable texture you're going for.
