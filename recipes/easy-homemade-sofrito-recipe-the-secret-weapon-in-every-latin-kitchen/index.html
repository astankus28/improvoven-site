Easy Homemade Sofrito Recipe (The Secret Weapon in Every Latin Kitchen)

Published November 12, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy homemade sofrito recipe is the flavor base behind so many of the dishes we love — stews, rice, beans, soups, chicken, you name it. Think of it as a fresh, blended aromatic sauce made from peppers, onion, garlic, cilantro, and tomato that you can make once and use all week long. If you've ever wondered why your abuela's food hits different, this is probably why.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
10 mins
Serves
16 tablespoons (about 1 cup)

Ingredients

  • 1 medium green bell pepper, roughly chopped
  • 1 medium red bell pepper, roughly chopped
  • 1 medium yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 8 cloves garlic, peeled
  • 1 cup fresh cilantro (leaves and tender stems), loosely packed
  • 2 roma tomatoes, roughly chopped
  • 4 ají dulce peppers, stems removed (or substitute 1 additional mini sweet pepper — see tips)
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar or lime juice

Instructions

  1. 1Roughly chop your bell peppers, onion, and tomatoes into large chunks — no need to be precise here, it's all going in the blender.
  2. 2Add the bell peppers, onion, garlic cloves, cilantro, tomatoes, and ají dulce peppers to a blender or food processor.
  3. 3Add the olive oil, dried oregano, cumin, salt, and apple cider vinegar or lime juice on top.
  4. 4Blend or pulse until you get a chunky-smooth consistency — think salsa texture, not a totally smooth purée. You want some texture left in there, not baby food.
  5. 5Taste and adjust salt or acid as needed. If it tastes flat, it needs more salt. If it needs brightness, add a little more lime juice.
  6. 6Transfer to a glass jar or airtight container. Use immediately or refrigerate for up to 1 week. For longer storage, freeze in ice cube trays and pop out sofrito cubes whenever a recipe calls for it — each cube is roughly 1 tablespoon.
💡 Improv Tip

Can't find ají dulce? Don't stress — they're the small, sweet, fragrant peppers common in Caribbean cooking (no heat, tons of flavor). Most Latin grocery stores carry them, but if yours doesn't, a mini sweet pepper or even a small piece of red bell pepper gets you close. The ají dulce adds a floral, almost fruity note that makes the sofrito feel distinctly Caribbean rather than purely Mexican-style. Both are delicious, just different vibes.
