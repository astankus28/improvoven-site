Spaghetti Aglio e Olio (Recipes Using Pantry Staples Only)

Published August 1, 2026 · Improv Oven

When you need recipes using pantry staples only, this is the one to reach for first — spaghetti aglio e olio is a classic Italian dish built entirely from pasta, olive oil, garlic, and a pinch of red pepper flakes. It sounds almost too simple, but the result is silky, garlicky, and genuinely restaurant-worthy. This is the dish Roman cooks make at midnight when the fridge is empty, and once you know it, you will too.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb spaghetti
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • 8 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes (or more to taste)
  • 1 teaspoon salt (plus more for pasta water)
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 cup pasta cooking water (reserved before draining)
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan or Pecorino (optional but highly recommended if you have it)
  • Small handful fresh parsley, chopped (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Salt it generously — it should taste like the sea. Add the spaghetti and cook until just barely al dente, about 1 to 2 minutes less than the package says. Before you drain it, scoop out at least 1 cup of the starchy cooking water and set it aside. That water is liquid gold here.
  2. 2While the pasta cooks, add the olive oil and sliced garlic to a large skillet over medium-low heat. You want the garlic to slowly turn golden and fragrant, not brown — this takes about 5 to 7 minutes. Keep the heat low and stay close. The moment the garlic turns deep golden, pull the pan off the heat briefly to stop it from burning.
  3. 3Add the red pepper flakes to the garlic oil and let them sizzle for about 30 seconds. The oil will smell incredible.
  4. 4Add the drained spaghetti directly to the skillet. Pour in about 1/2 cup of the reserved pasta water. Toss everything together over medium heat using tongs, letting the starchy water emulsify with the oil into a light, glossy sauce that coats every strand. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if it looks dry — you want it silky, not soupy.
  5. 5Season with salt and black pepper. Toss in the Parmesan and parsley if you have them, toss once more, and serve immediately in warm bowls.
💡 Improv Tip

The garlic is everything in this dish — slice it thin and keep the heat low. Burnt garlic turns bitter and there's nowhere to hide in a recipe this simple. If yours starts to go too dark too fast, just lift the pan off the burner for a minute. Low and slow is the whole move.
