Simple Shepherd's Pie Recipe (Weeknight Comfort, Miami-Style)

Published July 28, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple shepherd's pie recipe is the kind of dinner that makes everyone at the table go quiet in the best way — fork through buttery mashed potatoes, into a savory, spiced meat filling that's got a little Cuban soul in it. We're talking ground beef (or lamb if you're feeling fancy), a sofrito-seasoned filling with warm spices, and a golden mashed potato top that comes together in about an hour with stuff you probably already have. No fancy equipment, no hard-to-find ingredients — just real, satisfying food on a budget.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
45 mins
Total
1 hour
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs russet potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 4 tablespoons butter, divided
  • 1/2 cup whole milk or heavy cream
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 1 lb ground beef (80/20) or ground lamb
  • 1 small yellow onion, finely diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, finely diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 cup frozen peas and carrots
  • 1 cup beef broth (or 1 cup water + 1 beef bouillon cube)
  • 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tablespoon all-purpose flour
  • Optional: shredded cheddar cheese for topping

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 400°F. Get a large pot of salted water going and add your peeled, chunked potatoes. Bring to a boil and cook until totally fork-tender, about 15 to 18 minutes. Drain well.
  2. 2While the potatoes cook, heat a large oven-safe skillet or cast iron pan over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef and break it up with a spoon. Cook until browned, about 6 to 7 minutes. If there's a lot of fat in the pan, drain off most of it but leave a thin layer — that's flavor.
  3. 3Push the meat to one side and add 1 tablespoon of butter to the empty side of the pan. Toss in the diced onion and green bell pepper. Cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and starting to get a little golden, about 4 to 5 minutes. Add the garlic and cook one more minute.
  4. 4Stir in the tomato paste and cook it for about 2 minutes, stirring so it toasts slightly and turns a deeper red. This step is worth it — it concentrates the flavor big time.
  5. 5Sprinkle the flour over the meat mixture and stir everything together. Pour in the beef broth and Worcestershire sauce, then add the cumin, smoked paprika, and oregano. Stir well and bring to a gentle simmer. Let it cook for 5 to 6 minutes until the sauce thickens and coats the meat. Fold in the frozen peas and carrots. Taste and season with salt and pepper.
  6. 6Back to those potatoes — mash them with the remaining 3 tablespoons of butter and the milk until smooth and creamy. Season generously with salt and pepper. Don't be shy here; the mashed potato layer needs its own flavor or the whole dish falls flat.
  7. 7Spread the mashed potatoes evenly over the meat filling in the skillet, starting from the edges and working inward. Use a fork to drag the surface into peaks and ridges — those little peaks get golden and slightly crispy in the oven, which is exactly what you want. If you're doing cheese, sprinkle it over the top now.
  8. 8Bake uncovered at 400°F for 20 to 25 minutes, until the top is golden and the edges are bubbling. Let it rest for 5 minutes before scooping — this gives the filling a chance to settle so it doesn't run everywhere on the plate.
💡 Improv Tip

The green bell pepper and cumin in the filling are your Miami spin — they give the whole dish this warmth that makes people ask what's different about it. But if you want to go full classic British, skip those and just use a carrot and celery instead of the pepper. Either way works. The real secret is not rushing the tomato paste step — let it actually sizzle and darken before you add the liquid. That's what makes the filling taste deep and restaurant-good instead of just fine.
