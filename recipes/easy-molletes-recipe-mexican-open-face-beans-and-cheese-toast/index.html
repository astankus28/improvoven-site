Easy Molletes Recipe: Mexican Open-Face Beans and Cheese Toast

Published August 10, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy molletes recipe — the Mexican open-face beans and cheese toast that's basically a perfect weeknight dinner — is one of those dishes that sounds too simple to be this satisfying. You toast a bolillo or sturdy sub roll, slather it with creamy refried beans, pile on shredded cheese, and broil it until everything is melty and golden. Finish with fresh pico de gallo and you've got a meal that tastes like way more effort than it took.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 bolillo rolls (or 2 sturdy sub rolls or hoagie rolls, split lengthwise)
  • 1 can (16 oz) refried beans
  • 1 tablespoon butter or olive oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 1.5 cups shredded Oaxacan cheese, Chihuahua cheese, or Monterey Jack
  • 1/2 cup shredded mozzarella (optional stretch booster)
  • 2 Roma tomatoes, finely diced
  • 1/4 white onion, finely diced
  • 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced
  • 1/4 cup fresh cilantro, chopped
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Pinch of salt for pico
  • Hot sauce for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your broiler to high and set a rack about 6 inches from the heat source. Line a baking sheet with foil — this makes cleanup way easier.
  2. 2Make your quick pico de gallo first so it has time to sit and get happy: combine the diced tomatoes, white onion, jalapeño, cilantro, lime juice, and a pinch of salt in a small bowl. Stir, taste, adjust, and set aside.
  3. 3In a small saucepan over medium heat, melt the butter or warm the olive oil. Add the minced garlic and cook for about 60 seconds until fragrant but not browned. Add the refried beans straight from the can and stir to combine. Season with cumin, chili powder, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring often, until the beans are warmed through and slightly loosened. If they look very thick, add a splash of water — you want them spreadable, not pasty.
  4. 4Split your bolillo rolls lengthwise and place them cut-side up on the lined baking sheet. If your rolls are very soft, pop them under the broiler for just 1 to 2 minutes first to get a little structure going — you want the bread to hold up under all those toppings.
  5. 5Spoon a generous layer of the seasoned beans onto each roll half and spread it all the way to the edges. Don't be shy here — the beans are the foundation and you want full coverage.
  6. 6Top each roll generously with the shredded cheese. If you're using both Oaxacan and mozzarella, mix them together first so you get that melty pull with a little more flavor.
  7. 7Slide the baking sheet under the broiler and broil for 3 to 5 minutes, watching closely the whole time. You want the cheese fully melted, bubbling, and starting to get golden brown spots on top. Pull them the second they hit that sweet spot — broilers go from golden to scorched fast.
  8. 8Let the molletes rest for one minute (the cheese is molten lava right now), then top each one with a big spoonful of the fresh pico de gallo. Serve immediately with hot sauce on the side and extra lime wedges if you've got them.
💡 Improv Tip

The bread choice really matters here — you want something sturdy enough that it doesn't go soggy the second the beans hit it. Bolillo rolls are the classic and totally worth hunting down at a Latin bakery or the bread section of your grocery store, but a good hoagie roll or even a thick slice of French bread works beautifully. Avoid soft sandwich bread or anything too airy — it'll collapse under the weight and turn into a delicious but structural disaster.
