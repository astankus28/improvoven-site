Tequila Spritz Easy Drink-Inspired Citrus Glazed Salmon

Published July 11, 2026 · Improv Oven

If a tequila spritz easy drink could somehow become dinner, this citrus-glazed salmon is exactly what that would taste like — bright, bubbly, a little smoky, and totally refreshing on a warm evening. We're talking a quick pan-seared salmon fillet with a tequila-lime reduction that's got all the vibes of your favorite happy hour sipper, minus the hangover. Miami energy, weeknight ease, done in under 30 minutes.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
18 mins
Total
28 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 salmon fillets (about 6 oz each, skin-on if possible)
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/4 cup tequila (blanco or reposado — nothing fancy)
  • 1/2 cup fresh orange juice (about 2 oranges)
  • 3 tablespoons fresh lime juice (about 2 limes)
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 small jalapeño, thinly sliced (seeds in or out — your call)
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup sparkling water or club soda (the secret spritz move)
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • Fresh cilantro, for serving
  • Lime wedges, for serving
  • Cooked white rice or warm tortillas, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Pat your salmon fillets completely dry with paper towels — this is non-negotiable if you want that beautiful sear instead of steaming. Season all over with salt, pepper, smoked paprika, and garlic powder.
  2. 2In a small bowl or measuring cup, whisk together the tequila, orange juice, lime juice, honey, and soy sauce. Set it aside — this is your spritz glaze.
  3. 3Heat a large skillet (cast iron is ideal) over medium-high heat. Add the olive oil and let it get shimmery and hot, about 90 seconds.
  4. 4Place the salmon skin-side up in the pan. Don't touch it. Let it cook undisturbed for 4 minutes until a golden crust forms, then flip. Cook another 3-4 minutes depending on thickness. You want it just barely opaque through the center. Transfer salmon to a plate and tent loosely with foil.
  5. 5In the same pan, lower heat to medium. Add the minced garlic and jalapeño slices and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant — just enough to bloom them in those leftover salmon bits.
  6. 6Carefully pour in the tequila-citrus mixture. Stand back slightly — it will bubble up aggressively and smell incredible. Let it simmer and reduce for 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it's thickened slightly and syrupy.
  7. 7Here's the spritz move: pull the pan off heat and pour in the sparkling water. Stir it in — it lightens the glaze just enough, gives it a brightness, and keeps it from being too heavy. Then swirl in the butter until melted and glossy.
  8. 8Nestle the salmon back into the pan and spoon that glaze over each fillet generously. Let it sit in the sauce for one minute just to warm back through and soak it all up.
  9. 9Serve over fluffy white rice or with warm tortillas, with plenty of fresh cilantro and lime wedges alongside. Spoon extra glaze from the pan right over the top — don't waste a drop of that.
💡 Improv Tip

The sparkling water trick is the whole move here — it sounds weird but adding a splash of club soda to pan sauces right at the end gives you that bright, lifted finish that keeps the glaze from feeling heavy or cloying. Same reason a spritz cocktail feels lighter than a straight pour. Budget tip: frozen salmon fillets work beautifully here, just thaw overnight in the fridge and dry them really well before you season.
