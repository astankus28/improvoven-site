Simple Stuffing Recipe for Thanksgiving (Homemade, Buttery, and Better Than the Box)

Published November 22, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple stuffing recipe is the homemade Thanksgiving side dish that earns the most compliments at the table — and it's shockingly easy to pull off. We're talking crusty bread, real butter, celery, onion, and a handful of herbs you probably already have. No special equipment, no fussy technique — just the kind of golden, savory stuffing that makes people go back for a second scoop.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
45 mins
Total
1 hour
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 1 loaf (about 14 oz) day-old white or sourdough bread, cut into 3/4-inch cubes (about 10 cups)
  • 6 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1 large yellow onion, diced
  • 4 stalks celery, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons dried sage
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried rosemary
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 to 2 1/2 cups low-sodium chicken broth (vegetable broth works too)
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 2 tablespoons fresh parsley, chopped (or 1 teaspoon dried)

Instructions

  1. 1Dry out your bread first — this is the most important step. If your bread isn't already stale, spread the cubes on a baking sheet and bake at 275°F for about 20 minutes, tossing once halfway through, until they're dry and lightly toasty but not browned. Set aside to cool. (Doing this the night before is even better.)
  2. 2Preheat your oven to 375°F and butter a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  3. 3Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and celery and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they're soft and translucent. Add the garlic and cook for another minute until fragrant.
  4. 4Stir in the sage, thyme, rosemary, salt, and pepper. Let it cook for 30 seconds so the spices bloom in the butter — your kitchen will smell incredible. Remove from heat.
  5. 5In a large mixing bowl, combine the dried bread cubes and the cooked vegetable mixture. Toss everything together gently so the butter and herbs coat the bread.
  6. 6In a small bowl or measuring cup, whisk together the eggs and 2 cups of broth. Pour this over the bread mixture and toss again. You want the bread to be moist and well-coated but not soggy — if it looks too dry, add the remaining 1/2 cup of broth a splash at a time. Fold in the parsley.
  7. 7Transfer the stuffing to your prepared baking dish. Spread it out evenly and press it down lightly. For crispier stuffing, leave it uncovered the whole time. For a softer, more custardy center, cover with foil for the first 20 minutes, then uncover.
  8. 8Bake at 375°F for 40 to 45 minutes, until the top is deeply golden and crispy at the edges. Let it rest for 5 minutes before serving.
💡 Improv Tip

Day-old bread is non-negotiable for good stuffing — fresh bread turns to mush. If you're in a rush, the oven-drying method in step one works perfectly. And if you want to make this ahead, assemble the whole thing the night before, cover it, and refrigerate. Just add about 10 extra minutes to the bake time straight from the fridge.
