Simple Pernil Recipe (Easy Slow-Roasted Puerto Rican Pork)

Published September 4, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple pernil recipe is the easy, low-effort version of the iconic Puerto Rican slow-roasted pork shoulder — the kind that fills your whole house with garlic and oregano and has everyone hovering around the oven. You don't need a special occasion, a huge budget, or a culinary degree — just a bone-in pork shoulder, a handful of pantry staples, and a little patience while the oven does all the heavy lifting. This is the dish that turns a cheap cut into something absolutely legendary.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
5 hours
Total
5 hours 20 mins (plus overnight marinade if possible)
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 5–7 lbs bone-in pork shoulder (also sold as pork butt — ask your butcher for the skin-on cut if you want crispy cuero)
  • 8 cloves garlic, minced or smashed into a paste
  • 2 tablespoons dried oregano
  • 1 tablespoon ground cumin
  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • 1 tablespoon adobo seasoning (store-bought is perfectly fine here)
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 2 tablespoons white vinegar or sour orange juice if you can find it
  • 1 teaspoon sazon seasoning packet (optional but adds beautiful color and depth)
  • 1 cup water or chicken broth (for the roasting pan)

Instructions

  1. 1Make your adobo paste: In a small bowl, combine the minced garlic, oregano, cumin, smoked paprika, adobo seasoning, salt, black pepper, onion powder, olive oil, and vinegar. Stir it all together into a thick, fragrant paste. This is the soul of the dish — taste it and adjust the salt if needed.
  2. 2Score the pork: Pat your pork shoulder dry with paper towels. Using a sharp knife, cut deep slits all over the meat — about 1 inch deep, spacing them a couple of inches apart. If you have skin-on pork, score the skin too in a crosshatch pattern. These pockets are what carry all that garlic-herb flavor straight into the center of the meat.
  3. 3Marinate the pork: Rub the adobo paste all over the pork shoulder, pressing it into every slit and crevice with your fingers. Don't be shy — get it everywhere. For best results, cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate overnight or at least 4 hours. If you're cooking same-day, even 30 minutes at room temperature helps.
  4. 4Prep your roasting setup: When you're ready to cook, preheat your oven to 325°F. Place the pork shoulder skin-side up in a deep roasting pan or a large Dutch oven. Pour 1 cup of water or broth into the bottom of the pan — this keeps things moist and gives you the beginning of a killer pan dripping to spoon over rice later.
  5. 5Slow roast covered: Cover the pan tightly with a lid or two layers of aluminum foil. Roast at 325°F for about 1 hour per pound — so a 6-pound shoulder goes in for roughly 6 hours. The goal is pork that's falling apart tender and registers at least 195°F internally. Low and slow is non-negotiable here.
  6. 6Blast the heat for crispy skin: If you have skin-on pork and want that glorious crispy cuero, uncover the pan in the last 30–45 minutes and crank the heat to 425°F. Watch it closely — the skin will bubble and blister into crackling perfection. If you don't have skin-on pork, skip this step and just uncover for the last 20 minutes to get some color on top.
  7. 7Rest before shredding: Pull the pork from the oven and let it rest uncovered for at least 20 minutes. This step matters — it lets the juices redistribute so nothing runs out the moment you cut into it. Then use two forks or your hands to pull the meat apart. Spoon those pan drippings over the top before serving.
  8. 8Serve it up: Pernil is traditionally served with arroz con gandules (rice with pigeon peas) and tostones, but honestly it's incredible stuffed into a crusty roll, piled over white rice, or eaten straight from the pan with a fork at midnight. No judgment here.
💡 Improv Tip

The biggest secret to pernil that actually tastes like something is making those deep slits and pressing the paste all the way in — surface seasoning alone won't cut it on a big roast. And if your grocery store only has boneless pork shoulder, that works too, just reduce the cook time slightly and check the internal temp earlier. Leftover pernil reheated in a pan with a splash of orange juice is honestly better than the original.
