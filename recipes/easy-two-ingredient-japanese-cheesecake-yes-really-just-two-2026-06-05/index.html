Easy Two Ingredient Japanese Cheesecake (Yes, Really Just Two)

Published June 5, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy two ingredient Japanese cheesecake is one of those recipes that feels like a magic trick the first time you make it — just cream cheese and eggs, and somehow you get this cloud-like, jiggly little cake that's equal parts impressive and adorable. No fancy equipment, no long ingredient list, just two things you probably already have sitting in your fridge right now. It's light, slightly sweet, and honestly way easier than it has any right to be.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 oz cream cheese, softened (half a standard block)
  • 3 large eggs, separated and at room temperature

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 325°F and place a rack in the center. Line a 6-inch round cake pan or oven-safe ramekins with parchment paper and lightly grease the sides — this cake is delicate and you want it to release cleanly.
  2. 2Separate your eggs, putting the yolks in one bowl and the whites in a clean, completely dry bowl. This is important — even a tiny drop of yolk in your whites will stop them from whipping up properly, so take your time here.
  3. 3Microwave the softened cream cheese in 15-second intervals, stirring between each, until it's completely smooth and almost pourable. You can also do this over a double boiler if you prefer. Once smooth, whisk in the egg yolks one at a time until the mixture is silky and uniform. Set aside.
  4. 4Using a hand mixer or stand mixer, beat the egg whites on medium speed until they reach stiff peaks — they should look glossy and hold their shape when you lift the beaters. This is the magic that makes the cake rise and get that signature jiggle, so don't rush it. It takes about 3 to 4 minutes.
  5. 5Gently fold one-third of the whipped egg whites into the cream cheese mixture to loosen it up. Then carefully fold in the remaining whites in two additions, using a rubber spatula with slow, sweeping motions from the bottom of the bowl up and over. You want to keep as much air in there as possible — that's your lift.
  6. 6Pour the batter into your prepared pan and smooth the top. Place the pan inside a larger baking dish and add about an inch of hot water to create a water bath. This gentle, humid heat is what gives Japanese cheesecake its soft, custardy texture without cracking.
  7. 7Bake for 25 minutes, then turn off the oven and crack the door open slightly. Let the cake sit in the warm oven for another 10 minutes before removing — this prevents it from deflating too fast. Let it cool completely at room temperature before slicing, or refrigerate for an hour for cleaner cuts.
  8. 8Dust with powdered sugar if you have it, or serve as-is. Either way, watch everyone's face when it jiggles on the plate. That's your moment.
💡 Improv Tip

Room temperature everything is non-negotiable here, mami. Cold cream cheese won't smooth out properly and cold egg whites won't whip to stiff peaks the way they need to. Pull both out of the fridge at least 30 minutes before you start and your life will be so much easier. Also, that water bath might feel like extra work but please don't skip it — it's the whole reason this cake comes out soft and creamy instead of rubbery.
