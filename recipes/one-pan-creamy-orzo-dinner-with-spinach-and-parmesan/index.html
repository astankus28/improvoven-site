One Pan Creamy Orzo Dinner with Spinach and Parmesan

Published July 12, 2026 · Improv Oven

This one pan creamy orzo dinner is the kind of weeknight meal that makes you feel like you actually have your life together — rich, silky, and done in about 30 minutes with ingredients you probably already have. The orzo cooks right in the broth and soaks up all that flavor, then a splash of cream and a fistful of Parmesan turn it into something that tastes genuinely fancy. It's cozy Italian-American comfort food at its absolute best, and the cleanup is basically nothing.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb boneless skinless chicken breasts or thighs, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 1 1/2 cups dry orzo pasta
  • 3 cups low-sodium chicken broth
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream (or half-and-half works)
  • 3 oz fresh baby spinach (about 2 big handfuls)
  • 1/2 cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese, plus more for serving
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 small yellow onion, diced
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried Italian seasoning
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon crushed red pepper flakes (optional but recommended)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • Fresh parsley or basil for garnish (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Season your chicken pieces generously with salt, pepper, and garlic powder. Heat olive oil in a large, deep skillet or wide sauté pan over medium-high heat until it shimmers.
  2. 2Add the chicken in a single layer and let it sear undisturbed for 3 to 4 minutes until golden brown on the bottom. Flip and cook another 2 to 3 minutes. The chicken doesn't need to be fully cooked through yet — it's going back in. Remove it to a plate and set aside.
  3. 3Reduce heat to medium. Add the diced onion to the same pan with all those good browned bits still in there. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and translucent. Add the minced garlic and Italian seasoning and cook for another 60 seconds until fragrant.
  4. 4Pour in the dry orzo and stir to coat it in the oil and aromatics. Let it toast for about 1 minute — you'll smell it turn slightly nutty. This step is worth it, don't skip it.
  5. 5Pour in the chicken broth and bring everything to a gentle boil, stirring to scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Nestle the seared chicken back in, reduce heat to medium-low, and cook uncovered for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring every couple of minutes to keep the orzo from sticking. Cook until the orzo is tender and most of the broth has been absorbed but it still looks a little saucy.
  6. 6Reduce heat to low and stir in the heavy cream. Let it simmer gently for 2 minutes, then add the spinach in big handfuls, stirring until it wilts completely into the pasta. This takes about 1 to 2 minutes.
  7. 7Take the pan off the heat and stir in the Parmesan cheese and lemon zest. Taste and adjust salt and pepper. The lemon zest is the secret — it cuts through the richness and makes everything pop. Add the red pepper flakes now if you want a little heat.
  8. 8Serve straight from the pan with extra Parmesan on top and a handful of fresh parsley or torn basil if you have it. It thickens as it sits, so eat it while it's hot and creamy.
💡 Improv Tip

Orzo is a clingy little pasta and it will keep absorbing liquid as it sits — if it tightens up too much before you serve it, just splash in a bit more warm broth or even a little pasta water and stir it back to life. Also, resist the urge to crank the heat once the broth goes in; a steady medium-low simmer gives you creamy and saucy, while too-high heat gives you gluey and stuck-to-the-pan. Low and slow wins this one.
