Simple Potato Soup Recipe (Creamy, Cozy, and Ready in 30 Minutes)

Published September 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple potato soup recipe is the kind of thing you make when you've got a bag of potatoes, half an onion, and absolutely zero patience for a complicated dinner — or lunch. It's thick, creamy, and deeply satisfying without requiring cream of anything from a can. One pot, pantry staples, done.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs russet or Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and diced into 1-inch chunks
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons butter or olive oil
  • 3 cups chicken or vegetable broth
  • 1 cup milk (whole, 2%, or even oat milk works)
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • Optional toppings: shredded cheddar, sour cream, sliced scallions, crumbled bacon or crispy chickpeas

Instructions

  1. 1Heat butter or olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook for about 4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and translucent. Add the minced garlic and cook another 30 seconds until fragrant — don't let it burn.
  2. 2Add the diced potatoes to the pot along with the broth, garlic powder, onion powder, salt, and pepper. Stir everything together. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to medium-low and let it simmer uncovered for 15 minutes, or until the potatoes are completely tender and break easily when poked with a fork.
  3. 3Use a potato masher or the back of a wooden spoon to mash roughly half the potatoes directly in the pot. This is your secret weapon — it thickens the soup without any flour or cream of anything. You want some chunks and some creaminess, not a smooth puree.
  4. 4Stir in the milk and let the soup cook on low for another 2 to 3 minutes until heated through. Taste and adjust salt and pepper as needed. If the soup feels too thick, add a splash more broth or milk until it's exactly the consistency you want.
  5. 5Ladle into bowls and load up with your toppings. Cheddar and scallions are the classic move. Sour cream and bacon if you're feeling extra. A drizzle of olive oil and some smoked paprika if you're keeping it simple and vegetarian. All of it is correct.
💡 Improv Tip

Yukon Golds make a naturally creamier, butterier soup — russets give you more starch and a slightly fluffier texture. Either works great, so just use whatever's rolling around in your pantry. The real trick is that mashing step: don't skip it and don't over-do it. Half-mashed is the sweet spot between brothy and gluey.
