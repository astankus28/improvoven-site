Simple Ropa Vieja Recipe — Turned Into a Caramel Flan Dessert

Published October 21, 2026 · Improv Oven

Okay, hear me out — this simple ropa vieja recipe inspiration led us somewhere wild and delicious. We took the slow-cooked, shredded magic of ropa vieja and turned it into a dessert concept: a silky, Cuban-style caramel flan with shredded coconut 'strands' woven through the custard, mimicking those iconic pulled beef ribbons in the most unexpected, sweet way. It's a Miami dessert through and through — rich, golden, wobbly, and totally achievable with pantry staples you probably already have.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
50 mins
Total
1 hr 5 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar (for caramel)
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/8 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 cup sweetened shredded coconut, loosely packed
  • 1/2 teaspoon orange zest (optional, but so Miami)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F and get a 9-inch round cake pan or a set of 6 ramekins ready. Also set up a larger baking dish that your pan or ramekins can sit inside — you're doing a water bath, which sounds fancy but just means a dish of hot water. Trust the process.
  2. 2Make the caramel: Add the sugar and water to a small saucepan over medium heat. Don't stir — just swirl the pan gently every 30 seconds or so. Watch it carefully. It'll bubble, then slowly turn amber and golden, about 8 to 10 minutes. The moment it hits a deep golden honey color, immediately pour it into the bottom of your cake pan (or divide among ramekins). Tilt the pan so the caramel coats the bottom evenly, then set it aside to harden. It will sizzle and set fast — that's exactly what you want.
  3. 3Make the custard: In a blender (or a big bowl with a whisk), combine the eggs, sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, vanilla, cinnamon, salt, and orange zest if using. Blend or whisk until totally smooth — about 30 seconds in a blender, or a solid minute by hand. Don't rush this step; you want it silky.
  4. 4Fold in the coconut: Stir the shredded coconut gently into the custard mixture with a spoon. This is your ropa vieja moment — those long coconut strands are your 'shredded' element, and they'll weave through the flan as it bakes, giving every bite a little chew and a lot of tropical flavor.
  5. 5Pour the custard mixture over the hardened caramel in your pan or ramekins. Place your pan inside the larger baking dish, then carefully pour hot water into the outer dish until it comes about halfway up the sides of the flan pan. This water bath keeps the heat gentle so your flan comes out smooth and creamy, not rubbery.
  6. 6Bake at 350°F for 45 to 50 minutes for a full pan (30 to 35 minutes for ramekins). The flan is done when the edges are set but the center still has a slight jiggle — like Jell-O, not liquid. Pull it out carefully (hot water, be careful!) and let it cool at room temp for 30 minutes.
  7. 7Transfer the flan to the refrigerator and chill for at least 2 hours, or overnight if you can wait. To unmold: run a thin knife around the edge of the pan, place a plate on top, and flip it quickly and confidently. The caramel will cascade down like a beautiful golden waterfall. Slice, serve, and watch people lose their minds.
💡 Improv Tip

The caramel is the only part that can go sideways — pull it off the heat the second it turns deep amber. It keeps cooking even after you pour it, so don't wait for it to be dark brown on the stove or it'll turn bitter. When in doubt, go slightly lighter — it deepens in color as it cools.
