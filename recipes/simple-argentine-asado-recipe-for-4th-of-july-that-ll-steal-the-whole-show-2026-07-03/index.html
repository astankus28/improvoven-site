Simple Argentine Asado Recipe for 4th of July That'll Steal the Whole Show

Published July 3, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you want a simple Argentine asado recipe for 4th of July that goes way beyond the usual burgers and dogs, this is your move — we're talking chimichurri-drenched beef short ribs slow-cooked over indirect heat the way Buenos Aires does it, no fancy equipment required. The whole secret is patience and salt, and I promise your neighbors are going to be asking what that smell is from two backyards over. This is the kind of cookout that gets talked about in the group chat for weeks.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
2 hours
Total
2 hours 20 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 4 lbs beef short ribs or flanken-style ribs (ask the butcher for asado de tira if they have it)
  • 2 tablespoons coarse kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon garlic powder
  • 1 cup fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup olive oil
  • 3 tablespoons red wine vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt for chimichurri
  • 1 lemon, sliced (for serving)

Instructions

  1. 1Pull the ribs out of the fridge at least 45 minutes before cooking — this is non-negotiable in Argentine culture and it makes a real difference. Cold meat straight to the grill is how you end up with tough, uneven results. Let them come up to room temp on the counter while you get everything else ready.
  2. 2Season the ribs generously on all sides with the coarse salt, black pepper, and garlic powder. Don't be shy — asado is all about that salty crust. Pat the seasoning in with your hands so it sticks.
  3. 3Set up your grill for indirect heat: if you have charcoal, push the coals to one side. If you have a gas grill, light only one side on medium. You want the grill temperature to hover around 300°F — low and slow is the Argentine way, not screaming hot.
  4. 4Place the ribs bone-side down on the indirect heat side of the grill. Close the lid and let them cook undisturbed for 1 hour. Resist the urge to flip them every five minutes — asado is a vibe, not a race.
  5. 5While the ribs cook, make your chimichurri: combine the chopped parsley, minced garlic, olive oil, red wine vinegar, oregano, red pepper flakes, and salt in a bowl. Stir well and let it sit at room temperature so the flavors marry together. Taste it and adjust — more vinegar for tang, more salt if it needs it.
  6. 6After 1 hour, flip the ribs and move them closer to the direct heat side. Cook for another 45 minutes to 1 hour, turning occasionally, until the exterior is deeply browned and caramelized and the meat pulls back from the bone. You're looking for bark, not char.
  7. 7Pull the ribs off the grill and let them rest on a cutting board for 10 minutes — I know, it's hard to wait, but resting keeps all those juices inside the meat. Cut between the bones into individual ribs.
  8. 8Pile the ribs on a big platter, spoon chimichurri generously over the top, and serve the rest of the chimichurri on the side with lemon slices. Put it in the middle of the table and watch it disappear.
💡 Improv Tip

The biggest mistake people make with asado is rushing the fire — Argentine grill masters use wood or hardwood charcoal and let the coals turn fully white before they even think about putting meat on. If you can get your hands on some hardwood lump charcoal at the hardware store instead of the standard briquettes, do it. The smoke flavor it gives those ribs is on a completely different level, and it usually costs the same or less.
