Budget Argentine Milanesa Chicken Recipe (Crispy, Golden, and So Worth It)

Published June 19, 2026 · Improv Oven

This budget Argentine milanesa chicken recipe is straight-up pantry magic — thin-pounded chicken cutlets coated in seasoned breadcrumbs and pan-fried until shatteringly crispy, just like they do it in Buenos Aires. It's one of those meals that feels way fancier than the price tag, and once you make it, you'll wonder why you ever bought frozen chicken tenders. Serve it with a squeeze of lemon and some chimichurri if you're feeling extra, or just eat it straight out of the pan — no judgment.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 boneless skinless chicken breasts (about 2 lbs)
  • 1 cup plain breadcrumbs (or crush up stale crackers if that's what you've got)
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons milk
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon sweet paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/3 cup neutral oil for frying (vegetable or canola)
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges for serving
  • Fresh parsley, chopped, for garnish (optional but pretty)

Instructions

  1. 1Pound the chicken: Place each chicken breast inside a zip-lock bag or between two sheets of plastic wrap. Use a meat mallet, rolling pin, or the bottom of a heavy pan to pound them to about 1/4-inch thickness. This is the move that makes milanesa what it is — thin, even, and quick-cooking. Don't skip it.
  2. 2Set up your dredging station: Grab three shallow dishes. In the first, add the flour with a pinch of salt and pepper. In the second, whisk together the eggs and milk. In the third, mix the breadcrumbs with garlic powder, onion powder, oregano, paprika, cumin, salt, and black pepper. Give that breadcrumb mix a taste — it should smell amazing already.
  3. 3Coat the chicken: Working one piece at a time, dredge the chicken in flour and shake off the excess. Dip it into the egg wash, letting the extra drip off. Then press it firmly into the seasoned breadcrumbs on both sides, making sure you get full coverage. Set the coated pieces on a plate or rack while you finish the rest.
  4. 4Heat your oil: Pour the oil into a large skillet (cast iron is ideal but any heavy pan works) and heat it over medium-high heat. You want about 1/4 inch of oil in the pan. To test if it's ready, drop a pinch of breadcrumbs in — if they sizzle right away, you're good to go.
  5. 5Fry the milanesas: Add one or two chicken cutlets at a time — don't crowd the pan or they'll steam instead of crisp. Cook for about 3 to 4 minutes per side until deep golden brown and cooked through. The internal temp should hit 165°F. Transfer to a paper towel-lined plate and hit them with a little extra salt while they're still hot.
  6. 6Serve it up: Plate your milanesas with lemon wedges on the side, a scatter of fresh parsley, and whatever else you're working with — rice, a simple salad, fried plantains, mashed potatoes, or chimichurri if you have five more minutes. This is one of those meals that goes with everything.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to keeping that crust from falling off is letting the coated cutlets rest for about 5 minutes before they hit the pan — it helps the breading grip the chicken. Also, resist the urge to move them around while they're frying. Set it, leave it alone for a few minutes, and you'll get that gorgeous even crust. Trust the process.
