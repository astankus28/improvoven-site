Homemade Chimichurri Sauce Recipe (The Only One You'll Ever Need)

Published September 4, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade chimichurri sauce recipe is the kind of thing that makes people think you spent way more time in the kitchen than you actually did — it comes together in about 10 minutes with ingredients you probably already have. Bright, garlicky, a little tangy, with that grassy freshness that makes everything it touches taste like a backyard asado in Argentina by way of Miami. Keep a jar in your fridge and suddenly every piece of grilled chicken, roasted veggie, or crusty bread becomes a whole situation.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
10 mins
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 1 cup fresh flat-leaf parsley, tightly packed (stems are fine — use them)
  • 4 cloves garlic, roughly chopped
  • 1/4 cup fresh oregano leaves (or 1 tablespoon dried oregano if that's what you've got)
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes (add more if you like heat)
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon freshly cracked black pepper
  • 3 tablespoons red wine vinegar
  • 1/2 cup good olive oil
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice (optional but really nice)

Instructions

  1. 1Wash and dry your parsley thoroughly — wet herbs will make your chimichurri watery and sad, so give it a good shake and pat it dry with a paper towel or spin it in a salad spinner if you have one.
  2. 2Add the parsley, garlic, oregano, red pepper flakes, salt, and black pepper to a food processor and pulse about 8 to 10 times until everything is roughly chopped and combined. You want texture here, not a smooth paste — stop before it looks like baby food.
  3. 3Transfer the chopped herb mixture to a bowl or jar. Pour in the red wine vinegar and lemon juice if using, then stir to combine.
  4. 4Drizzle in the olive oil slowly while stirring, or just add it all at once and mix well. The chimichurri should look vibrant green and loose, almost like a chunky vinaigrette — not thick, not oily puddles.
  5. 5Taste and adjust. More salt? More vinegar for tang? Another pinch of red pepper flakes? This is your sauce, make it yours. Let it rest at room temperature for at least 15 to 20 minutes before serving so the flavors can get friendly with each other.
  6. 6Serve immediately over grilled steak, chicken thighs, roasted vegetables, eggs, crusty bread — honestly anything that needs a little life. Store leftovers in a sealed jar in the fridge for up to one week. The color will darken slightly but the flavor just gets better.
💡 Improv Tip

Don't have a food processor? No problem — chimichurri was originally made by hand. Just mince everything finely with a good knife and mix it in a bowl. Some people actually prefer the hand-chopped version because the texture is chunkier and more rustic. Also, if your parsley is on its last legs and looking a little sad, chimichurri is the perfect rescue mission — it was basically invented to use up herbs before they go. Classic Improv Oven energy right there.
