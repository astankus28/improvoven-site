Simple Pambazo Sandwich Recipe for Weeknight Lunches

Published June 3, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple pambazo sandwich recipe is exactly what your weeknight lunch routine has been missing — a Mexican street food classic that's secretly easy to pull off at home with stuff you probably already have. A bolillo roll gets soaked in a smoky guajillo chile sauce, then pan-fried until crispy, stuffed with a savory potato and chorizo filling, and loaded with all the good toppings. Think of it like if a torta and a tostada had a delicious baby.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 bolillo rolls (or sub hoagie rolls in a pinch)
  • 3 dried guajillo chiles, stems and seeds removed
  • 2 dried ancho chiles, stems and seeds removed
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup warm water (for blending the sauce)
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil, divided
  • 3 medium Yukon gold potatoes, peeled and diced small
  • 8 oz Mexican chorizo, casing removed
  • 1/2 medium white onion, diced
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 cup shredded lettuce
  • 1/2 cup Mexican crema or sour cream
  • 1/2 cup crumbled queso fresco
  • 1 avocado, sliced
  • Pickled jalapeños for topping (optional but highly recommended)

Instructions

  1. 1Make the guajillo sauce first: Tear the dried guajillo and ancho chiles into pieces and drop them in a dry skillet over medium heat. Toast them for about 60 to 90 seconds per side — you want them fragrant and slightly darkened but not burnt. Transfer to a blender with the garlic, oregano, cumin, salt, and warm water. Blend until completely smooth. If it feels too thick, add a splash more water. Set aside.
  2. 2Bring a medium pot of salted water to a boil and cook the diced potatoes until fork-tender, about 8 to 10 minutes. Drain and set aside.
  3. 3In a large skillet over medium-high heat, cook the chorizo for about 5 minutes, breaking it up as it goes. Once it starts releasing its fat and getting a little crispy, add the diced onion and cook another 3 minutes until softened. Toss in the cooked potatoes, season with salt and pepper, and stir everything together so the potatoes soak up all that chorizo goodness. Keep warm on low heat.
  4. 4Now for the magic step — the chile dip. Pour your guajillo sauce into a wide shallow bowl or dish. Slice the bolillo rolls in half and dip both cut sides into the sauce, pressing gently so they really absorb it. You want them well-coated but not falling apart.
  5. 5Heat a tablespoon of oil in a large non-stick skillet or griddle over medium heat. Place the sauce-soaked rolls cut-side down and cook for 2 to 3 minutes until the sauce caramelizes and the bread gets a gorgeous crispy crust. Flip and cook the outside for another 1 to 2 minutes. Work in batches so you don't crowd the pan.
  6. 6Assemble your pambazos: Load the bottom half of each roll with a generous scoop of the potato-chorizo filling. Add sliced avocado, a handful of shredded lettuce, a drizzle of crema, a shower of crumbled queso fresco, and pickled jalapeños if you're about that life. Cap with the top half and serve immediately while that crust is still crackling.
💡 Improv Tip

The single biggest mistake people make with pambazos is being shy with the chile sauce dip — really press those rolls in and let them drink it up. The sauce is what gives the bread that iconic deep red color and that smoky, slightly spicy crust. If your guajillo chiles have been sitting in the pantry for a while and seem stiff, soak them in hot water for 10 minutes before blending instead of toasting — they'll still give you a beautiful sauce.
