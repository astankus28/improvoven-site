One Pot Vegetable Curry Recipe (Big Flavor, Pantry Ingredients)

Published November 13, 2026 · Improv Oven

This one pot vegetable curry recipe is the kind of weeknight dinner that makes you feel like you actually have your life together — and it costs less than $8 to feed four people. We're building deep, warming flavor with spices you probably already have, coconut milk from the can, and whatever vegetables are hanging out in your fridge looking sad. Miami twist: a squeeze of lime and a pinch of scotch bonnet heat at the end that ties the whole thing together.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
40 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or coconut oil
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, grated (or 1 teaspoon ground ginger)
  • 1 jalapeño or small scotch bonnet pepper, minced (seeds removed for less heat)
  • 2 tablespoons curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional, for extra heat)
  • 1 can (15 oz) diced tomatoes with their juices
  • 1 can (13.5 oz) full-fat coconut milk
  • 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 2 cups cauliflower florets (fresh or frozen)
  • 2 cups baby spinach or chopped kale
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Fresh cilantro, for serving
  • Cooked rice or warm flatbread, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the oil in a large, deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook for 4–5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and just starting to turn golden at the edges.
  2. 2Add the garlic, ginger, and minced jalapeño or scotch bonnet. Stir everything together and cook for about 1 minute until fragrant — your kitchen should smell incredible right about now.
  3. 3Add the curry powder, cumin, smoked paprika, turmeric, and cayenne if using. Stir the spices into the onion mixture and let them toast in the oil for 60 seconds. This is called blooming the spices and it's the step most people skip — don't skip it.
  4. 4Pour in the diced tomatoes and their juices. Stir to combine and scrape up any bits stuck to the bottom of the pot. Let this cook for 2 minutes so the tomatoes start to break down and meld with the spices.
  5. 5Add the coconut milk, chickpeas, and cubed potatoes. Stir everything together, bring to a gentle boil, then reduce the heat to medium-low. Cover the pot and let it simmer for 15 minutes, until the potatoes are just about fork-tender.
  6. 6Add the cauliflower florets and stir them in. Cover again and cook for another 8–10 minutes, until the cauliflower is tender but not mushy.
  7. 7Remove the lid and stir in the baby spinach or kale. It will wilt down in about 1–2 minutes. Squeeze in the lime juice, taste, and adjust salt as needed.
  8. 8Serve hot over rice or with warm flatbread, topped with plenty of fresh cilantro. A drizzle of coconut milk over the top right before serving looks beautiful and tastes even better.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to a curry that tastes like it simmered all day is not rushing the spice-blooming step — that 60 seconds of dry-toasting in the oil before you add any liquid is where the magic happens. And if your curry sauce looks too thick, splash in a little water or vegetable broth and stir. Too thin? Let it bubble uncovered for 5 extra minutes and it'll tighten right up.
