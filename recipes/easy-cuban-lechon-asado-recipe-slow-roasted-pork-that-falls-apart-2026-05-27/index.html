Easy Cuban Lechon Asado Recipe (Slow-Roasted Pork That Falls Apart)

Published May 27, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Cuban lechon asado recipe is the one my neighbor Marta basically threw at me through the fence because she was tired of watching me make sad pork. We're talking deeply marinated, mojo-soaked pork shoulder that roasts low and slow until it's falling apart and smelling like every good memory you have. No fancy equipment, no hard-to-find ingredients — just real Cuban flavor on a real budget.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
4 hrs
Total
4 hrs 20 mins (plus overnight marinating if you can)
Serves
6-8

Ingredients

  • 4-5 lbs bone-in pork shoulder (Boston butt works perfectly)
  • 10 cloves garlic, roughly chopped
  • 1/2 cup sour orange juice (or 1/4 cup orange juice plus 1/4 cup lime juice)
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 1 tablespoon dried oregano
  • 2 teaspoons cumin
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt (plus more for the outside)
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 large white onion, sliced into rings
  • 1 cup water or chicken broth (for the roasting pan)

Instructions

  1. 1Make your mojo: In a blender or using a mortar and pestle, combine the garlic, sour orange juice, olive oil, oregano, cumin, salt, and pepper. Blend until you have a loose, fragrant paste. This is the soul of the whole dish — taste it. It should be punchy, garlicky, and bright.
  2. 2Score and stuff the pork: Using a sharp knife, cut deep slits all over the pork shoulder — about 1 inch deep, all around. You want as many pockets as possible. Pour about half the mojo into those slits and rub it in with your fingers. Then coat the entire outside of the pork with the remaining mojo. Season the outside generously with extra salt.
  3. 3Marinate: Place the pork in a large zip-lock bag or a covered dish. Let it marinate in the fridge for at least 2 hours, but overnight is where the magic really happens. If you're doing it same-day, even 30 minutes is better than nothing — no judgment here.
  4. 4Prep the roasting pan: When you're ready to cook, preheat your oven to 325°F. Scatter the sliced onion rings on the bottom of a roasting pan or a deep baking dish. Pour in the water or broth. Place the pork on top of the onions, fat-side up — those onions are going to get jammy and delicious and they'll keep the pork from sticking.
  5. 5Roast low and slow: Cover the pan tightly with foil and roast at 325°F for about 3 to 3.5 hours, until the pork is very tender and pulling away from the bone. The internal temp should hit at least 190°F — you want it high enough to break down all that collagen and get it truly fall-apart tender.
  6. 6Crisp it up: Remove the foil, crank the oven to 425°F, and roast uncovered for another 20-30 minutes until the outside is deeply golden and caramelized in spots. That crispy outside against the juicy inside is what lechon asado is all about. Let it rest 15 minutes before shredding or slicing.
  7. 7Serve it up: Pull the pork apart with two forks or slice it thick. Spoon those jammy onions and pan juices right over the top. Classic pairings are white rice, black beans, and tostones — but honestly, it's incredible in a sandwich with pickles and mustard the next day too.
💡 Improv Tip

Sour orange — naranja agria — is the real key to authentic lechon asado flavor, and if you're in Miami you can grab a bottle at any Latin grocery for under two dollars. If you're not near one, the orange juice and lime juice combo works beautifully. Don't skip the overnight marinade if you have time — that's not extra, that's the recipe.
