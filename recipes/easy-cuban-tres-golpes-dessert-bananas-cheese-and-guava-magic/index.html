Easy Cuban Tres Golpes Dessert (Bananas, Cheese, and Guava Magic)

Published September 18, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Cuban tres golpes dessert is one of those combinations that sounds weird until you try it — then you wonder why you haven't been eating it your whole life. Tres golpes means 'three hits,' and those three hits are sweet guava paste, salty white cheese, and soft ripe banana all in one glorious bite. It's a classic Cuban breakfast-meets-dessert that costs almost nothing and comes together in about five minutes flat.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
5 mins
Total
10 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 ripe bananas, peeled and halved lengthwise
  • 8 oz guava paste (pasta de guayaba), sliced into 1/4-inch rounds
  • 8 oz queso blanco or queso de mano, sliced (or mild white cheddar in a pinch)
  • 1 tablespoon butter or neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon sugar (optional, for caramelizing bananas)
  • Pinch of salt
  • Fresh mint or a dusting of cinnamon for garnish (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Slice your bananas in half lengthwise so you have long flat pieces that will sit nicely in the pan. If your bananas are very ripe, handle them gently — they'll be soft and sweet, which is exactly what you want here.
  2. 2Heat a non-stick skillet or cast iron pan over medium heat and add the butter. Once it's melted and just starting to foam, lay the banana halves cut-side down in the pan. Sprinkle with the optional sugar and a tiny pinch of salt.
  3. 3Cook the bananas undisturbed for about 2 to 3 minutes until the cut side is golden and just starting to caramelize. You're not trying to cook them through — you just want that beautiful golden color and a little warmth. Flip gently and cook 1 minute more, then transfer to your serving plates.
  4. 4In the same warm pan (turn off the heat), add your guava paste slices and let them sit for about 30 to 60 seconds. The residual heat will soften them slightly and make them glossy and jammy. You can also just serve the guava paste at room temperature straight from the package — it doesn't need cooking.
  5. 5Plate each serving with two banana halves, two or three rounds of softened guava paste, and a generous slice or two of queso blanco. The classic presentation is side by side on a small plate — banana, guava, cheese — each one touching. That's the tres golpes: three hits, all at once.
  6. 6Finish with a light dusting of cinnamon or a sprig of fresh mint if you want to make it look fancy. Serve immediately while the banana is still warm and the guava is soft. Eat all three together in one bite and try not to make a sound.
💡 Improv Tip

The cheese is non-negotiable — that salty, milky contrast is what makes this dish work. Queso blanco is the classic choice and you'll find it at most Latin grocery stores or the international aisle for just a few dollars. If you can't find it, queso fresco or even a mild white cheddar will do the job. Just avoid anything too sharp or aged — you want creamy and mild so it doesn't fight the guava.
