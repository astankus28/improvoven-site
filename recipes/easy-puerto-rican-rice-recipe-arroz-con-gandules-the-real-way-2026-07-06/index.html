Easy Puerto Rican Rice Recipe (Arroz con Gandules the Real Way)

Published July 6, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Puerto Rican rice recipe is the one dish that'll make your whole kitchen smell like a Sunday at abuela's house — and yes, you can make it on a Tuesday with stuff you probably already have. We're talking arroz con gandules, the iconic Puerto Rican rice cooked with pigeon peas and sofrito that's been showing up at every good party, cookout, and holiday table for generations. Once you nail this, you'll never look at plain white rice the same way again.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 2 cups long-grain white rice
  • 1 can (15 oz) gandules (pigeon peas), drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (8 oz) tomato sauce
  • 3 cups water or chicken broth
  • 3 tablespoons sofrito (jarred is totally fine — Goya works great)
  • 2 oz smoked ham or ham hock, diced (optional but worth it)
  • 1 packet Sazón con culantro y achiote (Goya brand)
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder or 2 fresh garlic cloves, minced
  • Salt to taste
  • 4-5 pimiento-stuffed olives, sliced (optional but traditional)

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a medium-to-large caldero or heavy-bottomed pot over medium-high heat. If you have smoked ham, toss it in first and let it get a little golden and fragrant — about 2 minutes. That fat renders out and becomes the flavor base for everything.
  2. 2Add the sofrito directly to the pot and stir it around in that hot oil for about 1 to 2 minutes. You want it to sizzle and cook down slightly — this is where the magic starts. Your kitchen is about to smell incredible.
  3. 3Pour in the tomato sauce and stir everything together. Let it cook for another minute so the tomato sauce melds with the sofrito. Add your garlic, the whole packet of Sazón, and the sliced olives if you're using them. Stir to combine.
  4. 4Add the drained gandules and stir them into the sofrito-tomato base so every pea gets coated in that flavor.
  5. 5Pour in your water or chicken broth and bring everything to a boil. Taste the liquid right now — it should be seasoned and savory. If it tastes flat, add a little salt. You season the liquid, you season the rice.
  6. 6Once boiling, add the rice and stir it in. Let it cook uncovered on medium-high heat, stirring occasionally, until most of the liquid has been absorbed and you can see little holes forming on the surface of the rice — about 5 to 7 minutes.
  7. 7Once the liquid is mostly absorbed, reduce the heat to the lowest setting and cover the pot tightly with a lid or with aluminum foil first then the lid (the foil seal trick keeps all the steam in). Let it steam undisturbed for 20 minutes. Do not lift that lid.
  8. 8After 20 minutes, remove from heat and let it sit covered for 5 more minutes. Then fluff it gently with a fork from the bottom up. If there's a golden crispy layer at the bottom of the pot — that's the pegao, and in Puerto Rican cooking that crust is a prize, not a mistake.
💡 Improv Tip

The biggest mistake people make is lifting the lid while the rice steams — every time you peek, you're letting out the steam that's doing all the cooking. Set a timer, walk away, and trust the process. Also, if you can't find gandules at your regular grocery store, check the Latin foods aisle or any Hispanic market — they're usually less than two dollars a can and absolutely worth the trip.
