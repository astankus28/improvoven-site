Easy Puerto Rican Jibarito Lunch (Plantain Sandwich That Slaps)

Published June 2, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Puerto Rican jibarito lunch is what happens when someone replaces bread with smashed fried plantains and suddenly nobody cares about regular sandwiches anymore. It's savory, crispy, filling, and costs almost nothing to make — just the kind of move we love around here. Once you try it this way, a plain hoagie roll is going to feel like a serious downgrade.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 large green plantains, peeled and halved lengthwise
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil, for frying
  • 1 lb thin-sliced skirt steak or sandwich steak
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1 teaspoon sazon seasoning (store-bought or homemade)
  • 1/2 teaspoon adobo seasoning
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup shredded lettuce
  • 1 large tomato, sliced thin
  • 4 slices American or white cheddar cheese
  • 1/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tablespoon ketchup
  • 1 teaspoon hot sauce (optional but encouraged)

Instructions

  1. 1Peel your green plantains by scoring the skin lengthwise and pulling it off — green plantains can be stubborn, so don't be shy about using a knife to help. Cut each plantain in half crosswise so you end up with 4 thick chunks total, then slice each chunk in half lengthwise so you have flat planks. These are going to be your bread.
  2. 2Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Once shimmering, add the plantain planks and fry for about 3 to 4 minutes per side until they turn golden and are cooked through but not crispy yet. You want them pliable at this stage. Remove them and let them drain on a paper towel.
  3. 3While the plantains are still warm, place each plank between two pieces of parchment paper or inside a zip-lock bag and smash them flat with the bottom of a heavy pan, a cutting board, or whatever flat heavy thing is living on your counter. You want them thin like a wide flat piece of toast — about a quarter inch thick.
  4. 4Return the smashed plantains to the hot oil and fry them again for 2 to 3 minutes per side until they are golden brown and crispy on the outside. This second fry is what gives them that satisfying crunch. Drain again on paper towels and hit them with a little salt right away.
  5. 5Season your steak with the garlic powder, onion powder, sazon, adobo, and black pepper on both sides. Heat the olive oil in a skillet or grill pan over medium-high heat and cook the steak for about 2 to 3 minutes per side depending on thickness. You want good color on it. Let it rest for two minutes then slice it thin against the grain.
  6. 6In the same pan you cooked the steak, drop in the minced garlic over medium-low heat and let it soften and get fragrant in the leftover drippings for about 1 minute. Toss your sliced steak back in and mix it all together. This step is not optional — those garlicky drippings are everything.
  7. 7Make your jibarito sauce by mixing the mayonnaise, ketchup, and hot sauce together in a small bowl. This is your spread and it is simple but it works perfectly with everything else going on in this sandwich.
  8. 8Now build your jibarito. Spread the sauce on the inside of two crispy plantain planks. Layer on the garlicky steak, a slice of cheese, some tomato, and a handful of shredded lettuce. Press the top plank down gently and that is your sandwich. Serve immediately while the plantains are still warm and crispy.
💡 Improv Tip

Green plantains are non-negotiable here — ripe or yellow ones will be too sweet and too soft and they will fall apart when you smash them. If you can only find ripe ones at the store, save those for tostones maduro or just fry them sweet for a side dish and try the jibarito another day. Also if you want to keep the plantains crispy while you finish building all four sandwiches, lay them on a wire rack in a 200 degree oven instead of stacking them on a plate — stacking is how you lose the crunch.
