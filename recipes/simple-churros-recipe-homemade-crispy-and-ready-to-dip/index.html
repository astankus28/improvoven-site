Simple Churros Recipe (Homemade, Crispy, and Ready to Dip)

Published September 27, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple churros recipe homemade version is way easier than you think — just a basic dough, a star-tip piping bag, and hot oil standing between you and golden, cinnamon-sugared perfection. No fancy equipment, no obscure ingredients, just pantry staples doing their best work. Whip up a quick chocolate dipping sauce on the side and you've got a dessert that genuinely impresses people.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons granulated sugar (for the dough)
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil or butter (plus more for frying)
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar (for rolling)
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • Vegetable oil for frying (enough to fill a pot 2–3 inches deep)
  • For the chocolate dipping sauce:
  • 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips
  • 1/4 cup heavy cream or whole milk
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • Pinch of salt

Instructions

  1. 1Make your cinnamon sugar first: mix 1/2 cup granulated sugar with 1 teaspoon cinnamon in a wide shallow bowl and set it aside. You want this ready to go the second the churros come out of the oil — that's when it sticks best.
  2. 2In a medium saucepan over medium heat, combine the water, 2 tablespoons sugar, salt, and 2 tablespoons oil or butter. Stir and bring it to a light boil.
  3. 3Remove the pan from the heat and stir in the flour all at once. Mix vigorously with a wooden spoon until the dough comes together into a smooth ball and pulls away from the sides of the pan — about 1 minute of good stirring.
  4. 4Let the dough cool for 5 minutes so it's warm but not screaming hot. Then add the vanilla and mix in the eggs one at a time, stirring hard after each addition. The dough will look slippery and broken at first — keep going. It will come together into a thick, smooth, pipeable dough.
  5. 5Heat 2 to 3 inches of vegetable oil in a heavy-bottomed pot or deep skillet over medium-high heat until it reaches 350–375°F. If you don't have a thermometer, drop in a tiny piece of dough — it should sizzle and rise within a few seconds.
  6. 6Fit a piping bag with a large star tip (or use a zip-lock bag with a corner snipped off for a more rustic look) and fill it with the dough. Pipe 4 to 5 inch strips directly into the hot oil, using scissors or a knife to snip off each one. Fry in batches — don't crowd the pan.
  7. 7Fry each churro for about 2 to 3 minutes per side, turning once, until they're deep golden brown all over. Pale churros will be doughy inside — don't rush them.
  8. 8Use a slotted spoon or tongs to pull the churros out and immediately roll them in the cinnamon sugar while they're still hot and glistening. Set them on a wire rack or paper towels.
  9. 9While the last batch fries, make your chocolate sauce: in a small saucepan over low heat (or in the microwave in 30-second bursts), melt the chocolate chips with the cream and butter, stirring until smooth and glossy. Add a pinch of salt and stir. Done.
  10. 10Serve the churros right away with the warm chocolate sauce for dipping. These are best eaten fresh and hot — they're at their peak in the first 10 to 15 minutes.
💡 Improv Tip

The biggest churro mistake is pulling them out of the oil too soon. That gorgeous golden color isn't just about looks — it means the outside is set and the inside is cooked through. Go a shade darker than you think looks right, and you'll be rewarded with churros that are crispy outside and fluffy inside instead of greasy and soft. And if your dough feels too stiff to pipe, let it sit for another couple minutes — it loosens up slightly as it rests.
