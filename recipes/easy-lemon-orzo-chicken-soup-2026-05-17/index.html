Easy Lemon Orzo Chicken Soup

Published May 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy lemon orzo chicken soup recipe is the kind of thing you throw together on a Tuesday when the fridge looks sad but your pantry is holding it down. Bright, lemony broth, tender chicken, and little pasta pearls that make every spoonful feel like a hug — this is weekday magic, no drama required. It comes together in 30 minutes and tastes like you spent all afternoon on it.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb boneless skinless chicken breasts or thighs
  • 3/4 cup dry orzo pasta
  • 6 cups chicken broth (store-bought or homemade)
  • 2 lemons, juiced (about 1/4 cup) plus zest of 1 lemon
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 2 medium carrots, sliced into rounds
  • 2 stalks celery, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Fresh parsley or cilantro for garnish (optional but worth it)

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Once it shimmers, add your diced onion, carrots, and celery. Cook for about 4-5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion goes soft and translucent. Add the garlic and cook another 60 seconds until fragrant — your kitchen should smell incredible right now.
  2. 2Nestle the whole chicken breasts or thighs into the pot and pour in the chicken broth. Add the oregano, cumin, a generous pinch of salt, and some black pepper. Bring everything to a boil, then reduce the heat to a steady simmer. Cook the chicken for about 12-15 minutes until it's cooked through and no longer pink in the center.
  3. 3Fish the chicken out of the pot and set it on a cutting board. Drop the orzo directly into the simmering broth and let it cook for 8-9 minutes, stirring occasionally so it doesn't stick to the bottom.
  4. 4While the orzo cooks, use two forks to shred the chicken into bite-sized pieces — it should pull apart easily. Add the shredded chicken back into the pot.
  5. 5Turn the heat to low and stir in the fresh lemon juice and lemon zest. Taste the broth and adjust salt and pepper as needed. The lemon should be bright and forward — don't be shy with it. Ladle into bowls and top with fresh parsley or cilantro if you've got it.
  6. 6Serve hot with crusty bread on the side if you want to do it right.
💡 Improv Tip

Orzo keeps absorbing liquid as it sits, so if you have leftovers, add a splash of broth when reheating to bring it back to soup consistency instead of a thick stew. Also, don't skip the lemon zest — that's where the real brightness lives, and it costs you nothing extra.
