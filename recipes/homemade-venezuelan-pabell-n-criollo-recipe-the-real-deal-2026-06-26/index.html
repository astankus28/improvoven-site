Homemade Venezuelan Pabellón Criollo Recipe (The Real Deal)

Published June 26, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade Venezuelan pabellón criollo recipe is the kind of meal that turns a Tuesday night into a whole vibe — shredded beef, black beans, white rice, and sweet fried plantains all on one plate, each doing their own thing but better together. It's the national dish of Venezuela for a reason, and honestly it's more approachable than it looks. Once you make it, you'll understand why every Venezuelan is a little homesick without it.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
1 hour 15 mins
Total
1 hour 35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs flank steak or skirt steak
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1.5 cups long-grain white rice
  • 2 ripe plantains (the blacker the skin, the sweeter the plantain)
  • 1 medium yellow onion, divided
  • 1 medium green bell pepper, divided
  • 1 medium red bell pepper
  • 6 cloves garlic, minced and divided
  • 2 roma tomatoes, diced
  • 1/4 cup fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin, divided
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon paprika
  • 2 cups beef broth or water for boiling the meat
  • Oil for frying plantains (about 1/4 cup vegetable oil)

Instructions

  1. 1Start with the beef because it takes the longest. Place your flank steak in a pot, cover with water or beef broth, and add half the onion (roughly chopped), half the green bell pepper, 2 cloves of garlic, 1/2 teaspoon cumin, salt, and black pepper. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to medium-low, cover, and simmer for 45 to 55 minutes until the meat is fork-tender and shreds easily. Keep the cooking liquid — you'll use some of it.
  2. 2While the beef cooks, get your rice going. Rinse 1.5 cups of white rice under cold water until it runs clear. In a medium saucepan, heat 1 tablespoon of olive oil over medium heat, add 2 cloves of minced garlic and a pinch of salt, stir for about 30 seconds until fragrant, then add the rice and toast it for 1 to 2 minutes. Pour in 3 cups of water (or use some of that beef broth for extra flavor), bring to a boil, reduce to the lowest heat, cover, and cook for 18 minutes. Turn off the heat and let it sit covered for another 5 minutes. Do not lift the lid.
  3. 3Once the beef is tender, pull it out and let it rest for 5 minutes. Use two forks to shred it into long, thin strands — you want it rustic, not minced. Now make the carne mechada: dice the remaining half onion, the red bell pepper, and remaining green bell pepper. Heat 1 tablespoon of vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat, add the onion and peppers, and cook for 5 minutes until softened. Add the remaining 2 cloves of garlic, diced tomatoes, remaining 1/2 teaspoon cumin, paprika, salt, and pepper. Cook another 3 minutes. Add the shredded beef plus a splash (about 1/4 cup) of the reserved cooking liquid. Stir everything together and cook on medium until it's saucy but not soupy, about 5 to 7 minutes. Finish with fresh cilantro.
  4. 4For the caraotas negras (black beans), heat 1 tablespoon of vegetable oil in a small saucepan over medium heat. Sauté a small handful of the diced onion and one minced garlic clove for 2 minutes. Add your drained black beans, a pinch of cumin, salt, and a splash of water or broth. Mash about a quarter of the beans against the side of the pot with your spoon — this thickens everything up into that creamy, rich texture that makes Venezuelan black beans different from just beans out of a can. Cook on low for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  5. 5Peel your ripe plantains and slice them on a diagonal about half an inch thick. Heat about 1/4 cup of vegetable oil in a skillet over medium heat — you want it hot but not smoking. Fry the plantain slices in batches for about 2 minutes per side until they're deep golden and caramelized. They should have those beautiful dark edges. Drain on a paper towel and hit them with a tiny pinch of salt right away.
  6. 6To plate like a true pabellón: scoop a generous mound of white rice on one side, add the shredded beef carne mechada next to it, spoon the black beans on the other side, and lay the sweet fried plantains (tajadas) across the top or on the side. Each component should be visible — this is a dish that's meant to be seen. Serve immediately while everything is hot.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret weapon for your carne mechada is that reserved beef cooking broth — don't throw it out. It's already seasoned and loaded with flavor, and adding just a splash to your shredded beef while it simmers in the sofrito takes the whole thing from good to 'okay who taught you to cook like this.' You can also freeze leftover broth in an ice cube tray and use it the next time you make rice or beans.
