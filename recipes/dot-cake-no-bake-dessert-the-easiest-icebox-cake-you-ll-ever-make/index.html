Dot Cake No Bake Dessert (The Easiest Icebox Cake You'll Ever Make)

Published July 10, 2026 · Improv Oven

This dot cake no bake dessert is one of those magical fridge recipes that looks like you tried way harder than you did — we're talking layers of whipped cream and polka-dot wafer cookies that turn into a sliceable, dreamy cake overnight. No oven, no mixer required, just a little patience while it chills. It's the kind of dessert that clears out your pantry AND gets people asking for the recipe.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
4 hrs 20 mins (includes chilling)
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 2 (11 oz) packages chocolate-dotted vanilla wafer cookies (like Pepperidge Farm Bordeaux or any dot-style sandwich cookies, see tips)
  • 2 cups heavy whipping cream, cold
  • 3 tablespoons powdered sugar
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 8 oz cream cheese, softened to room temperature
  • 1 (3.4 oz) box instant vanilla pudding mix
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1/2 cup mini chocolate chips, for topping
  • optional: 2 tablespoons cocoa powder or 1/2 cup Nutella for a chocolate swirl layer

Instructions

  1. 1Make the cream cheese pudding base: In a large bowl, beat the softened cream cheese with a hand whisk or fork until smooth and fluffy — no lumps. Add the instant pudding mix and milk, and whisk vigorously for about 2 minutes until thick and combined. Set aside.
  2. 2Whip the cream: In a separate chilled bowl, pour in your cold heavy cream, powdered sugar, and vanilla extract. Whip by hand with a whisk (or use a hand mixer if you have one) until you get soft, pillowy peaks — it should hold its shape but not be grainy. This takes about 3–4 minutes by hand, less than 2 with a mixer.
  3. 3Fold together: Gently fold the whipped cream into the cream cheese pudding mixture using a spatula. Go slow, folding from the bottom up — you want to keep it light and airy. Taste it. It should be lightly sweet and creamy. Adjust with a pinch more powdered sugar if needed.
  4. 4Layer the cake — first layer: Grab a 9x13 inch baking dish. Spread a thin layer of the cream mixture on the bottom so the cookies don't slide. Lay your dot cookies in a single, snug layer across the bottom — flat side down, dots facing up. Slightly overlap them if needed to fill gaps.
  5. 5Layer the cream: Spread a generous layer of the cream mixture over the cookies — aim for about 1/3 of your total cream. Use a spatula to spread it evenly all the way to the edges.
  6. 6Repeat: Add a second layer of cookies, then another generous layer of cream. Repeat one more time until you've used all your cookies and cream, ending with a cream layer on top. You should get 3 cookie layers total.
  7. 7Finish the top: Scatter mini chocolate chips all over the top layer in an even layer. If you want extra drama, dust lightly with cocoa powder through a fine sieve for a tiramisu-style look.
  8. 8Chill — this is non-negotiable: Cover tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, but overnight is genuinely better. The cookies absorb the cream and transform into soft, cake-like layers. When you come back to it, it'll slice cleanly like a real cake.
  9. 9Serve: Use a sharp knife to cut into squares. A wide spatula helps you lift slices out cleanly. Serve cold straight from the fridge. Watch it disappear.
💡 Improv Tip

The 'dot cake' look comes from using cookies with chocolate polka dots on them — Pepperidge Farm Bordeaux cookies are the classic move, but Keebler Fudge Stripe minis, Chips Ahoy Thins, or even plain Nilla wafers with chocolate chips pressed into the cream between layers all work beautifully. The real secret is time: 4 hours is the minimum, but if you make this the night before, the texture is next-level — the cookies fully soften into something that genuinely tastes like sponge cake. Don't rush it.
