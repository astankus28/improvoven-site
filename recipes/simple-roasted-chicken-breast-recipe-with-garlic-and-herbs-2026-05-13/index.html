Simple Roasted Chicken Breast Recipe With Garlic and Herbs

Published May 13, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple roasted chicken breast recipe is the one you'll come back to every single week — juicy, golden, and seasoned just right with stuff you already have in your kitchen. No fancy techniques, no expensive cuts, just real food that tastes like somebody's abuela put love into it. We're keeping it budget-friendly and bold, because that's how we do things here.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
22 mins
Total
32 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts (about 6-8 oz each)
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • Juice of half a lemon (or lime — we're in Miami, limes are everywhere)
  • Fresh parsley for garnish, optional

Instructions

  1. 1Pull your chicken breasts out of the fridge about 15 minutes before cooking. This helps them cook evenly instead of being cold in the middle and overdone on the outside. While you wait, preheat your oven to 425°F. Yes, that high — we want golden and juicy, not pale and sad.
  2. 2Pat the chicken dry with paper towels. This step is non-negotiable, mi amor. Moisture is the enemy of a good sear and a beautiful roast. Dry chicken = better browning, better flavor.
  3. 3In a small bowl, mix together the olive oil, minced garlic, garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, oregano, cumin, salt, pepper, and lemon or lime juice. Stir it into a loose paste.
  4. 4Rub that seasoning mixture all over the chicken breasts — top, bottom, sides, get into every corner. Use your hands. This is cooking, not surgery.
  5. 5Place the chicken breasts in a baking dish or oven-safe skillet in a single layer. Don't crowd them or they'll steam instead of roast and you'll lose all that beautiful caramelization.
  6. 6Roast at 425°F for 20 to 22 minutes, depending on thickness. The internal temperature should read 165°F on a meat thermometer. If you don't have a thermometer, cut into the thickest part — juices should run clear and the center should not look translucent.
  7. 7Here's the move most people skip: let the chicken rest for 5 full minutes before cutting into it. Cover it loosely with foil and walk away. The juices redistribute and you get chicken that stays moist instead of running all over your cutting board.
  8. 8Slice, garnish with fresh parsley if you've got it, and serve. Goes amazing over rice, with roasted veggies, in a wrap, or honestly just on its own with some hot sauce on the side.
💡 Improv Tip

If your chicken breasts are really thick and uneven — like one end is twice as fat as the other — give them a quick pound with a heavy pan or rolling pin to even them out before seasoning. Uneven thickness means one part overcooks while the other is still catching up, and that's how you end up with dry chicken. Even thickness, even cooking, every time.
