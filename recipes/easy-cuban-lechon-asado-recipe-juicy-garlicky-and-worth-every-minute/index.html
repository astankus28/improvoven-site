Easy Cuban Lechon Asado Recipe (Juicy, Garlicky, and Worth Every Minute)

Published October 8, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Cuban lechon asado recipe brings all the magic of a whole roasted pig into a totally doable weeknight or weekend pork shoulder — no pit, no problem. The secret is a punchy mojo marinade made from pantry staples: lots of garlic, sour orange (or lime and orange juice), oregano, and cumin. Let the pork do its thing in the oven low and slow, and your whole house is going to smell absolutely incredible.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
4 hrs
Total
4 hrs 20 mins (plus overnight marinating if possible)
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 3–4 lbs bone-in pork shoulder (Boston butt), scored with a knife
  • 10 cloves garlic, minced or pressed
  • 1/2 cup fresh orange juice (or store-bought — totally fine)
  • 1/4 cup fresh lime juice (about 3 limes)
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 2 teaspoons dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional, for a little kick)
  • 1 medium yellow onion, sliced into rings
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Fresh cilantro or parsley, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Make your mojo marinade: In a bowl, whisk together the garlic, orange juice, lime juice, olive oil, oregano, cumin, onion powder, salt, black pepper, and red pepper flakes if using. Taste it — it should be bright, garlicky, and punchy. That's exactly what you want.
  2. 2Score and marinate the pork: Use a sharp knife to cut deep slits all over the pork shoulder, about an inch apart. This helps the mojo get all the way in. Place the pork in a zip-lock bag or a deep baking dish and pour the marinade over it. Rub it into every crevice you scored. Cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours — overnight is the move if you can plan ahead. It makes a real difference.
  3. 3When you're ready to cook, pull the pork out of the fridge and let it sit at room temperature for 30 minutes. Preheat your oven to 325°F.
  4. 4Set up your roasting pan: Lay the sliced onions and bay leaves in the bottom of a roasting pan or large oven-safe pot with a lid. Place the marinated pork on top, fat-side up. Pour any remaining marinade from the bag right over the top. Cover tightly with a lid or foil.
  5. 5Roast low and slow: Cook covered at 325°F for 3 to 3.5 hours, until the pork is fork-tender and pulling away from the bone. The internal temperature should reach at least 190°F — you want it collapse-tender, not just food-safe.
  6. 6Crisp the outside: Remove the lid or foil, crank the oven up to 425°F, and roast uncovered for another 20–30 minutes until the top is beautifully caramelized and crisped up. Watch it so it doesn't burn — you want golden and crunchy, not charred.
  7. 7Rest and shred: Pull the pork from the oven and let it rest for 10–15 minutes. Then use two forks to pull it apart — it should shred easily. Spoon some of those pan juices and softened onions back over the top. Finish with fresh cilantro if you have it.
  8. 8Serve with white rice, black beans, tostones, or warmed Cuban bread. Leftovers make insane sandwiches the next day — just saying.
💡 Improv Tip

No sour oranges? No stress. The classic Cuban mojo shortcut is equal parts fresh orange juice and fresh lime juice — together they hit that same tart, slightly sweet citrus note that makes lechon asado taste like lechon asado. If you have a bottle of Goya Mojo Criollo in your pantry, you can use about 3/4 cup of that in place of the citrus and garlic and it still turns out fantastic. This is improv cooking — work with what you've got.
