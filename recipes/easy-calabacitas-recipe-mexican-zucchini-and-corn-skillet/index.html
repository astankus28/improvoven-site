Easy Calabacitas Recipe: Mexican Zucchini and Corn Skillet

Published July 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy calabacitas recipe brings together tender Mexican zucchini, sweet corn, and melty cheese in one humble skillet that punches way above its weight. It's the kind of dish your abuela would make when the garden was talking — simple, vegetable-forward, and deeply satisfying as a full dinner. We're giving it a little Miami warmth with smoked paprika and a hit of lime, because good food always wants a reason to show up.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 1 medium white onion, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced (leave seeds in if you like heat)
  • 3 medium zucchini (about 1.5 lbs), cut into half-moons or small cubes
  • 1.5 cups fresh, frozen, or canned corn kernels (drained if canned)
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes, drained
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano (Mexican oregano if you have it)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 1 cup shredded Oaxaca, Monterey Jack, or mozzarella cheese
  • 1/4 cup fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Warm tortillas or crusty bread, for serving
  • Optional: 1 can (15 oz) black beans or pinto beans, drained — makes it even heartier

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the oil in a large skillet or cast iron pan over medium-high heat. Once shimmering, add the diced onion and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until soft and just starting to turn golden at the edges.
  2. 2Add the garlic and jalapeño and cook for another 60 seconds, stirring constantly so the garlic doesn't burn. Your kitchen should smell incredible right about now.
  3. 3Add the zucchini in a single layer as best you can and let it sit undisturbed for 2 minutes before stirring. You want some color on it, not just steaming. Toss and cook another 2 to 3 minutes until the zucchini is tender but still has a little backbone to it.
  4. 4Stir in the corn, drained diced tomatoes, cumin, smoked paprika, and oregano. If you're adding beans, now's the time. Season generously with salt and pepper. Cook everything together for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomatoes have cooked down a bit and everything is melded and fragrant.
  5. 5Taste it. Adjust salt, pepper, or cumin to your liking. Squeeze the lime juice over the whole pan and give it one final stir — that acid lift at the end makes everything taste brighter.
  6. 6Scatter the shredded cheese evenly over the top. Cover the skillet with a lid or a sheet of foil and let it sit on low heat for 2 to 3 minutes until the cheese is fully melted and gooey.
  7. 7Pull off the lid, shower with fresh cilantro, and bring the whole skillet straight to the table. Serve with warm tortillas for scooping or crusty bread if that's what you've got.
💡 Improv Tip

Zucchini is basically water in vegetable form, and if you crowd the pan it'll steam instead of sear — you'll end up with a soggy mess. Work in a large skillet and don't skip that initial undisturbed sear on the zucchini. If your skillet feels too full, pull out a bigger pan or cook the zucchini in two batches. That little bit of caramelization is what takes calabacitas from cafeteria vegetable side to dinner you actually want.
