Rice Minho Budget Recipe (Crispy, Garlicky, and Deeply Satisfying)

Published September 21, 2026 · Improv Oven

This rice minho budget recipe is your new secret weapon for turning a humble bag of thin rice noodles into something genuinely crave-worthy. Minho — those delicate, wiry Portuguese-style rice vermicelli noodles — fry up beautifully in a hot pan, soaking in garlic, soy, and a little sesame to create a dish that tastes way more expensive than it is. Think of it as pantry magic: fast, filling, and endlessly riffable.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 8 oz rice vermicelli (minho / rice noodles)
  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 eggs, lightly beaten
  • 1 cup shredded cabbage or coleslaw mix
  • 1 medium carrot, julienned or grated
  • 3 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon oyster sauce (or hoisin sauce)
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon crushed red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 3 scallions, sliced — for garnish
  • Lime wedges — for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Soak the rice vermicelli in a large bowl of hot (not boiling) water for 6 to 8 minutes until just pliable but still a little firm — they'll finish cooking in the pan. Drain well and toss with 1 teaspoon of vegetable oil so they don't stick together. Set aside.
  2. 2In a small bowl, stir together the soy sauce, oyster sauce, sesame oil, and black pepper. This is your flavor sauce — set it right next to the stove so you're ready.
  3. 3Heat a large skillet or wok over high heat until it's very hot. Add 1 tablespoon of vegetable oil and swirl to coat. Add the garlic and onion and stir-fry for 60 to 90 seconds until golden and fragrant, watching carefully so the garlic doesn't burn.
  4. 4Push the garlic and onion to the side of the pan. Add another splash of oil to the empty space and pour in the beaten eggs. Let them set for about 20 seconds, then scramble them gently and fold into the onion mixture before they're fully cooked.
  5. 5Add the cabbage and carrot to the pan. Stir-fry everything together for 1 to 2 minutes until the cabbage is just wilted but still has a little crunch.
  6. 6Add the drained noodles to the pan and pour the sauce over everything. Toss and stir-fry for 2 to 3 minutes, using tongs or chopsticks to separate the noodles and get them evenly coated. If anything starts sticking, add a tiny splash of water to loosen it up.
  7. 7Taste and adjust — a little more soy if you want more salt, a squeeze of lime if you want brightness, a pinch more chili if you want heat. Plate it up, scatter the scallions over the top, and serve with lime wedges on the side.
💡 Improv Tip

The single biggest mistake people make with rice vermicelli is oversoftening them in the soak — if they're already totally limp before they hit the pan, they'll turn mushy and clump. Pull them out of the water while they still feel just slightly too firm, almost like al dente pasta. The hot wok finishes the job and gives you that slightly chewy, slippery noodle texture that makes this dish sing.
