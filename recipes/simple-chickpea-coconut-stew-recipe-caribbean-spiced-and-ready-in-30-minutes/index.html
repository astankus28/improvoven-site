Simple Chickpea Coconut Stew Recipe (Caribbean-Spiced and Ready in 30 Minutes)

Published August 28, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple chickpea coconut stew recipe is the kind of pantry magic that makes you feel like a genius for not going to the grocery store — rich, creamy, and packed with warm spice from stuff you probably already have. It leans into that Miami-Caribbean soul with cumin, allspice, and a hit of scotch bonnet or hot sauce, all pulled together by a can of coconut milk that makes everything silky and beautiful. It's vegetarian, it's filling, and it costs next to nothing — which means it's already a permanent member of your weeknight rotation.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 (15 oz) cans chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 (13.5 oz) can full-fat coconut milk
  • 1 (14.5 oz) can diced tomatoes
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground allspice
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon hot sauce or a small pinch of cayenne (more if you like heat)
  • 1 cup vegetable broth or water
  • 2 cups fresh baby spinach or roughly chopped kale (optional but highly encouraged)
  • Juice of 1/2 lime
  • Fresh cilantro for serving (optional)
  • Cooked rice, flatbread, or crusty bread for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the oil in a large deep skillet or medium pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it's soft and just starting to turn golden at the edges. Don't rush this — those soft caramelized onion bits are laying the flavor foundation.
  2. 2Add the minced garlic and cook for another 60 seconds, stirring constantly so it doesn't burn. You'll smell when it's ready — fragrant, not sharp.
  3. 3Add the cumin, coriander, allspice, smoked paprika, turmeric, black pepper, and salt directly to the onion and garlic. Stir everything together and let the spices toast in the oil for about 30 seconds. This step is what separates a flat stew from one that smells like a Caribbean kitchen.
  4. 4Pour in the diced tomatoes with their juice and stir to combine. Let it simmer for 2 to 3 minutes so the tomatoes cook into the spice base and everything starts to meld together.
  5. 5Add the drained chickpeas, coconut milk, and vegetable broth. Stir well to combine. Bring the stew to a gentle boil, then reduce heat to medium-low and let it simmer uncovered for 15 minutes. The stew will thicken and the coconut milk will deepen in color as it reduces.
  6. 6If you're adding spinach or kale, stir it in during the last 2 to 3 minutes of cooking. Spinach will wilt almost instantly; kale needs a couple of minutes to soften. Either way, it disappears into the stew like it was always supposed to be there.
  7. 7Squeeze the lime juice in right at the end and give it a final taste. Adjust salt or hot sauce to your preference. The lime brightens everything and ties the coconut and spice together — don't skip it.
  8. 8Serve hot over rice or with flatbread on the side. Top with fresh cilantro if you've got it, and an extra drizzle of hot sauce if you're that kind of person (respect).
💡 Improv Tip

Here's the move that takes this stew from good to 'why does this taste like it simmered all day': after you add the chickpeas, use the back of a wooden spoon or a potato masher to smash about a quarter of them right in the pot. Those mashed chickpeas thicken the stew naturally and give it a heartier, almost creamy body ��� no cornstarch, no fuss, just chickpea alchemy.
