Simple Yuca Recipe for Thanksgiving: Herb Butter Mashed Yuca

Published November 14, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you're looking for a simple yuca recipe to shake up your Thanksgiving side dish spread, this herb butter mashed yuca is about to become your new tradition. It's creamy, rich, and deeply comforting — like mashed potatoes' more interesting cousin who shows up and steals the show. Best part? Yuca is cheap, filling, and works with whatever herbs and aromatics you've already got on hand.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
40 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs frozen yuca (or fresh, peeled and cut into chunks)
  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1/2 cup whole milk or heavy cream (warmed)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons fresh parsley, chopped (or 1 teaspoon dried)
  • 1 tablespoon fresh chives or green onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves (or 1/2 teaspoon dried)
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more for boiling water
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • Pinch of nutmeg
  • Extra butter and fresh herbs for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil. Add the yuca pieces (no need to thaw if using frozen). Boil for 20–25 minutes until completely tender and a fork slides through without any resistance — yuca should be soft all the way to the center. Don't rush this step; undercooked yuca is tough and waxy.
  2. 2Drain the yuca well. While it's still hot, use your hands or a fork to pull out and discard the thin fibrous string that runs through the center of each piece. It comes out easily once the yuca is cooked. This is the one step people skip that causes lumpy mash — don't skip it.
  3. 3In the same pot (or a small skillet), melt the butter over medium-low heat. Add the minced garlic and cook gently for 1–2 minutes until fragrant and just barely golden. Don't let it brown — you want that sweet, mellow garlic butter flavor.
  4. 4Return the drained yuca to the pot. Add the warm milk, garlic butter, salt, pepper, and nutmeg. Mash with a potato masher or a sturdy fork until smooth and creamy. Yuca gets beautifully silky — almost like a cross between mashed potatoes and polenta. Add a splash more milk if you like it looser.
  5. 5Fold in the parsley, chives, and thyme. Taste and adjust salt. Transfer to a serving bowl, top with an extra pat of butter and a shower of fresh herbs, and serve hot. This holds well covered in a warm oven (around 200°F) for up to 30 minutes while you finish the rest of your Thanksgiving spread.
💡 Improv Tip

Frozen yuca is your best friend here — it's already peeled, usually cheaper than fresh, and cooks beautifully. Look for it in the freezer aisle of any Latin, Caribbean, or international grocery store. If you're at a regular supermarket and only see fresh yuca, just make sure to peel it deeply (past the waxy outer skin AND the thin pink layer underneath) before boiling.
