Easy Argentine Beef Stew Recipe (Guiso de Carne on a Budget)

Published July 18, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Argentine beef stew recipe — called guiso de carne — is the kind of deeply satisfying, one-pot dinner that makes your whole kitchen smell incredible for hours. It's rustic, hearty, and built from humble ingredients like chuck beef, potatoes, and canned tomatoes, which means it's genuinely affordable any night of the week. Think of it as Argentina's answer to comfort food: smoky paprika, cumin, and a splash of red wine give it that unmistakable South American soul.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
1 hour 45 mins
Total
2 hours
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs beef chuck, cut into 1.5-inch cubes
  • 1.5 teaspoons kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 1 large yellow onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 teaspoons smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 cup dry red wine (a cheap Malbec is perfect, or skip and use extra broth)
  • 1 can (14 oz) crushed or diced tomatoes
  • 2 cups beef broth
  • 1 lb Yukon Gold or russet potatoes, cut into 1-inch chunks
  • 2 medium carrots, sliced into half-moons
  • 1 cup frozen peas
  • Fresh flat-leaf parsley, chopped, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Pat your beef cubes dry with paper towels — this is the move that actually gets you a good sear. Season them all over with 1.5 teaspoons salt and the black pepper.
  2. 2Heat the vegetable oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat until shimmering. Working in two batches so you don't crowd the pan, sear the beef cubes for about 3-4 minutes per side until deeply browned. Don't rush this — that golden crust is where all the flavor lives. Transfer the browned beef to a plate and set aside.
  3. 3Drop the heat to medium. Add the diced onion and green bell pepper to the same pot (don't wipe it out — those brown bits are gold). Cook, stirring occasionally, for about 5 minutes until softened and starting to turn translucent.
  4. 4Add the garlic, smoked paprika, cumin, and oregano. Stir everything together and cook for another minute until the spices are fragrant and coating the vegetables.
  5. 5Pour in the red wine to deglaze — scrape up all those brown bits stuck to the bottom of the pot with your wooden spoon. Let the wine bubble and reduce for about 2 minutes.
  6. 6Add the crushed tomatoes and beef broth, then return the seared beef (and any juices from the plate) back to the pot. Stir to combine. Bring everything to a gentle boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover with a lid slightly ajar, and simmer for 1 hour.
  7. 7After the first hour, add the potatoes and carrots. Stir them in, put the lid back on, and continue simmering for another 30-35 minutes until the potatoes are fork-tender and the beef is fall-apart soft.
  8. 8Stir in the frozen peas during the last 5 minutes of cooking — they just need to warm through. Taste and adjust salt as needed.
  9. 9Ladle into bowls and finish with a generous handful of fresh parsley. Serve with crusty bread for mopping, or a scoop of white rice on the side.
💡 Improv Tip

Chuck beef is your best friend for this stew — it's cheap, and the connective tissue melts into the broth over low and slow heat, giving you that silky, rich texture. Do NOT try to rush it with a higher heat; you'll end up with tough, chewy beef. Low and slow is the whole game here. And if you happen to have a leftover Malbec bottle from the weekend? That's your wine. Argentina's most famous grape in Argentina's most comforting stew — that's just poetic.
