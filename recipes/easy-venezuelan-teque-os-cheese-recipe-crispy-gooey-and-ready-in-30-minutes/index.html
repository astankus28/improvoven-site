Easy Venezuelan Tequeños Cheese Recipe (Crispy, Gooey, and Ready in 30 Minutes)

Published August 27, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Venezuelan tequeños cheese recipe is the one your party guests will talk about for weeks — golden, crispy on the outside, and impossibly stretchy and gooey on the inside. Tequeños are a beloved Venezuelan street snack and party staple, and once you learn the basic method, you'll be making them on repeat. The best part? They're made with just a handful of pantry and fridge staples, and they disappear faster than you can fry them.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
12 mins
Total
32 mins
Serves
24 tequeños (about 6 servings)

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 4 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into small cubes
  • 1 large egg
  • 3 to 4 tablespoons cold milk (or cold water), added one tablespoon at a time
  • 8 oz queso blanco or mozzarella, cut into finger-sized sticks (about 3 inches long, 1/2 inch wide)
  • Neutral oil for frying (vegetable or canola), enough to fill a small pot 2 to 3 inches deep

Instructions

  1. 1Cut your cheese first so it can chill while you make the dough. Slice your queso blanco or mozzarella into sticks roughly 3 inches long and half an inch wide — think short, thick cheese fingers. Pop them in the freezer for 10 minutes. Cold cheese holds its shape better when frying and gives you that dramatic stretchy pull without leaking everywhere.
  2. 2In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, salt, and sugar. Add the cold butter cubes and use your fingertips to press and rub the butter into the flour until the mixture looks like coarse, sandy crumbs with some pea-sized bits still visible. Cold butter is key — don't let it warm up or the dough will get greasy.
  3. 3Make a well in the center of the flour mixture. Crack in the egg and add 3 tablespoons of cold milk. Mix with a fork until the dough starts to come together, then use your hands to bring it into a smooth ball. If it feels dry or crumbly, add the last tablespoon of milk. The dough should be soft, pliable, and not sticky — like Play-Doh but tastier. Don't overwork it.
  4. 4Lightly flour your surface and roll the dough out to about 1/8-inch thickness. Use a knife or pizza cutter to cut strips roughly 1 inch wide and 5 to 6 inches long — you're going to wrap these around the cheese sticks in a spiral.
  5. 5Take a cheese stick from the freezer and place it at one end of a dough strip. Roll and wrap the dough around the cheese in a tight spiral, overlapping each wrap slightly and pressing the ends to seal. Pinch both ends firmly so the cheese is fully enclosed. Repeat with all your cheese sticks. Place finished tequeños on a plate and keep them cold in the fridge until your oil is ready.
  6. 6Pour 2 to 3 inches of neutral oil into a small heavy-bottomed pot or deep skillet. Heat over medium to medium-high heat until the oil reaches 350°F. If you don't have a thermometer, drop a tiny pinch of dough in — it should sizzle actively and float to the surface within a few seconds. Adjust heat as needed to maintain that temperature.
  7. 7Fry the tequeños in batches of 4 to 6 — don't crowd the pot or the temperature will drop and they'll turn greasy instead of crispy. Fry for about 2 to 3 minutes per batch, turning occasionally, until they're deeply golden all around. Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on a paper towel-lined plate.
  8. 8Eat them immediately. Seriously. Tequeños are a right-now food — that's the whole magic. Serve them plain, with a side of guasacaca (Venezuelan avocado sauce) if you want to go full Caracas, or with hot sauce, honey, or even a simple garlic aioli. Watch them vanish.
💡 Improv Tip

Mozzarella is your budget MVP here and honestly the most widely available option — it melts beautifully and gives you that iconic cheese pull. Queso de mano is the traditional Venezuelan choice if you can find it at a Latin market, and queso blanco works great too since it softens without making a mess. Whatever you use, keep it cold until the very last second before frying. Warm cheese starts melting before the dough is done and you end up with cheese leaking into your oil — which is sad and also a fire hazard. Frozen cheese, cold dough, hot oil. That's the trio.
