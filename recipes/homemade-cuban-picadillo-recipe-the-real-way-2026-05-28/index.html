Homemade Cuban Picadillo Recipe (The Real Way)

Published May 28, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade Cuban picadillo recipe is the kind of dish that makes your whole apartment smell like someone's abuela is in the kitchen — and honestly, that's the whole point. It's ground beef simmered low with olives, raisins, tomatoes, and sofrito until everything gets soft, savory, and a little sweet in that way only Cuban food can pull off. Serve it over white rice with some maduros on the side and you've got a full meal for under ten bucks that'll have everyone asking what restaurant you ordered from.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground beef (80/20 works great here)
  • 1/2 medium white onion, diced
  • 1/2 green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1 can (8 oz) tomato sauce
  • 1/4 cup dry white wine or water
  • 1/3 cup pimento-stuffed green olives, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons olive brine (from the jar — don't throw that away)
  • 3 tablespoons raisins
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Cooked white rice, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a large skillet or heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat. Add the diced onion and green pepper and cook for about 4-5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they're soft and the onion is starting to look a little translucent.
  2. 2Add the minced garlic and cook for another 60 seconds — just until it's fragrant. Don't walk away here, garlic burns fast and bitter garlic will ruin your whole vibe.
  3. 3Add the ground beef to the pan. Break it up with a wooden spoon or spatula and cook until it's browned all the way through, about 6-7 minutes. If there's a lot of excess fat pooling in the pan, carefully drain most of it off — a little is fine and adds flavor.
  4. 4Stir in the cumin, oregano, salt, and black pepper. Then add the tomato paste and stir everything together so the paste coats the meat. Let it cook for about a minute — this step deepens the flavor and gets rid of that raw tomato paste taste.
  5. 5Pour in the tomato sauce, white wine (or water), and the olive brine. Stir to combine, then add the olives, raisins, and bay leaves. Give it one good stir and bring everything up to a gentle simmer.
  6. 6Reduce the heat to medium-low and let the picadillo simmer uncovered for 10-12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce has thickened and the flavors have all come together. Taste it and adjust salt if needed. Fish out the bay leaves before serving.
  7. 7Serve hot over a big scoop of white rice. Maduros (sweet plantains) on the side if you've got them — that sweet-savory combo is next level.
💡 Improv Tip

The raisins are non-negotiable — I know it sounds wild if you've never had Cuban picadillo before, but that little pop of sweetness is what makes it taste authentic. If someone in your house is a raisin skeptic, just don't tell them until after they've had seconds. Works every time.
