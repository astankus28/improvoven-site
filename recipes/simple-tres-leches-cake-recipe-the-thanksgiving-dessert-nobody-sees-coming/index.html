Simple Tres Leches Cake Recipe — The Thanksgiving Dessert Nobody Sees Coming

Published November 2, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple tres leches cake recipe is the Thanksgiving dessert that'll quietly steal the show from every pie on the table — and honestly? Good. It's light, impossibly moist, and already made ahead so you're not stressing in the kitchen on turkey day. Think of it as your secret weapon: a little Miami on a very American holiday.

Prep
25 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
55 mins plus 4 hours chilling
Serves
12

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 5 large eggs, separated
  • 1 cup granulated sugar, divided
  • 1/3 cup whole milk
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk
  • 1 cup whole milk (for the soaking mixture)
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract (for the soaking mixture)
  • 1 teaspoon rum or dark rum extract (optional but recommended)
  • 2 cups heavy whipping cream
  • 3 tablespoons powdered sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract (for the whipped cream)
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon (for garnish)
  • Maraschino cherries or fresh strawberries for topping (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F. Grease a 9x13-inch baking dish with butter or nonstick spray and set it aside. This is the whole kitchen — you're making the whole cake in one pan, and that's exactly the energy we want.
  2. 2Whisk together the flour, baking powder, and salt in a medium bowl. Set it aside — that's your dry team.
  3. 3In a large bowl, beat the egg yolks with 3/4 cup of the sugar using a hand mixer or stand mixer on medium-high for about 3 to 4 minutes. You want it pale yellow, thick, and ribbony. Mix in the 1/3 cup whole milk and 1 teaspoon vanilla. Gently fold in the flour mixture until just combined — don't overwork it.
  4. 4In a separate clean bowl, beat the egg whites on medium speed until soft peaks form. Gradually add the remaining 1/4 cup sugar and keep beating until you get stiff, glossy peaks. This is what makes tres leches so light — it's basically a sponge cake in disguise.
  5. 5Fold the egg whites into the batter in three additions, being gentle so you don't knock all the air out. The batter will be light and airy — that's the goal. Pour it into the prepared baking dish and spread it evenly.
  6. 6Bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until the top is golden and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes.
  7. 7While the cake cools, whisk together the sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, 1 cup whole milk, 1 teaspoon vanilla, and rum or rum extract (if using) in a large measuring cup or bowl with a pour spout. This is your three-milk mixture — it's the whole soul of the dessert.
  8. 8Using a fork or wooden skewer, poke holes all over the surface of the warm cake — really go for it, every inch. You want those holes deep and all the way through so the milk soaks in evenly.
  9. 9Slowly pour the three-milk mixture over the entire surface of the cake, starting at the edges and working your way in. It's going to look like a lot of liquid — and it is. That's the point. Give it a minute to absorb, then pour any remaining liquid around the edges. Cover the pan with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or overnight. Overnight is better.
  10. 10When you're ready to serve, make your whipped cream: beat the heavy cream, powdered sugar, and vanilla together until soft, billowy peaks form. Spread it over the entire top of the cake like you're frosting a dream.
  11. 11Dust the top with a little ground cinnamon — this is that Miami touch, très Cuban-grandma energy — and add cherries or berries if you want something festive for the Thanksgiving table. Slice into squares and serve straight from the pan.
💡 Improv Tip

Make this the day before Thanksgiving and don't even think twice about it — tres leches is genuinely better after a full night in the fridge. The longer it sits, the more the cake drinks up that milk mixture and becomes this impossibly tender, custardy thing. The whipped cream goes on right before serving so it stays fluffy. If you don't have heavy cream, a can of coconut cream (well chilled) whips up beautifully and adds a subtle tropical note that nobody will be mad about.
