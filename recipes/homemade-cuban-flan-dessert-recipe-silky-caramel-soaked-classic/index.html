Homemade Cuban Flan Dessert Recipe (Silky, Caramel-Soaked Classic)

Published July 18, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade Cuban flan dessert recipe is the one your abuela would approve of — creamy, wobbling, drenched in that deep amber caramel that makes everyone at the table go quiet for a second. Cuban flan is simpler than it looks, made almost entirely from pantry staples you probably already have, and it's the kind of dessert that makes a Tuesday feel like a celebration. Make it the night before, flip it out the next day, and watch people think you've been cooking all week.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
55 mins
Total
1 hr 10 mins plus 4 hrs chilling
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 1 cup granulated sugar (for caramel)
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • Optional: 1 tablespoon cream cheese (the Cuban secret for extra richness)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 325°F. Set a kettle or pot of water on the stove to heat up — you'll need it for the water bath. Grab a 9-inch round cake pan or a standard loaf pan. No special flan mold required.
  2. 2Make the caramel: Pour the sugar and water into a small heavy-bottomed saucepan over medium heat. Don't stir — just swirl the pan gently as the sugar melts. Watch it closely. It'll go from clear to golden to a deep amber color in about 8 to 10 minutes. The moment it hits that rich amber (think iced tea color), pull it off the heat immediately and pour it into the bottom of your cake pan. Tilt the pan so the caramel coats the bottom evenly. It will set up fast — that's perfect.
  3. 3Make the custard: In a blender or large bowl, combine the sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, eggs, vanilla, and salt. If you're using the cream cheese, add it now — it blends in smoothly and gives the flan that extra velvety Cuban-style body. Blend or whisk until completely smooth, about 30 seconds in a blender or 1 full minute by hand.
  4. 4Pour the custard mixture slowly and gently over the hardened caramel in your pan. Go slow so you don't crack the caramel layer.
  5. 5Set up your water bath: Place your flan pan inside a larger roasting pan or baking dish. Carefully pour the hot water from your kettle into the outer pan until it comes about halfway up the sides of the flan pan. This gentle, humid heat is what gives flan its signature silky texture — don't skip it.
  6. 6Carefully transfer the whole setup to the oven. Bake at 325°F for 50 to 55 minutes. The flan is done when the edges are set but the center still has a gentle jiggle — like Jell-O, not like liquid. A knife inserted near the center should come out mostly clean.
  7. 7Remove the flan pan from the water bath and let it cool on the counter for 30 minutes. Then cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, but overnight is genuinely better. The wait is the hardest part.
  8. 8To unmold: Run a thin knife or offset spatula around the edges of the flan. Place a rimmed plate or platter on top of the pan — it needs a rim to catch all that caramel sauce. Flip it in one confident motion. Let the pan sit on top for a few seconds so all the caramel drizzles down. Lift the pan off and there it is — that wobbling, caramel-lacquered beauty. Slice and serve chilled.
💡 Improv Tip

The number one flan mistake is rushing the caramel. If it's too light (pale gold), your sauce will taste flat. You want it deep amber — almost a little scary — because that slight bitterness is what balances all the sweetness of the custard. Pull it off the heat the second it hits that color, because residual heat will keep cooking it for another few seconds in the pan. And if your caramel seizes up or hardens unevenly on the way into the pan, don't stress — it melts right back into gorgeous liquid sauce during baking.
