Homemade Tres Leches Pancakes Recipe (Fluffy, Soaked, and Ridiculously Good)

Published September 24, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade tres leches pancakes recipe takes everything you love about the classic Latin American cake — that dreamy, milky soak and pillowy texture — and turns it into a stack of pancakes you can make any morning or night. We're talking fluffy buttermilk pancakes drenched in a sweet three-milk syrup, topped with whipped cream and a little cinnamon, all from ingredients you probably already have. It's dessert for breakfast, or dessert for dessert — no judgment here.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup buttermilk (or regular milk with 1 tsp white vinegar, left to sit 5 minutes)
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter or neutral oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup evaporated milk
  • 1/4 cup sweetened condensed milk
  • 1/4 cup whole milk or heavy cream
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract (for the tres leches soak)
  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream
  • 2 tablespoons powdered sugar
  • Ground cinnamon, for dusting
  • Maraschino cherries or fresh strawberries, for garnish (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Make your tres leches soak first so it's ready to go: In a small saucepan over low heat, whisk together the evaporated milk, sweetened condensed milk, whole milk or heavy cream, and 1/2 teaspoon vanilla. Warm it for about 3 to 4 minutes until everything is combined and just barely steaming. Don't boil it — you just want it warm enough to soak in fast. Set aside.
  2. 2Make the pancake batter: In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. In a separate small bowl, whisk together the buttermilk, egg, melted butter, and vanilla extract. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry and stir gently until just combined — lumps are totally fine. Overmixing makes tough pancakes and nobody wants that.
  3. 3Heat a non-stick skillet or griddle over medium heat. Lightly grease it with butter or a little neutral oil. Once it's hot (a water drop should sizzle), ladle about 1/4 cup of batter per pancake onto the surface. Cook until bubbles form on the surface and the edges look set, about 2 to 3 minutes. Flip and cook another 1 to 2 minutes until golden. Repeat with remaining batter.
  4. 4While the pancakes are still warm, plate them in a stack of 3 to 4 and slowly pour 3 to 4 tablespoons of the tres leches soak over each serving. Let it sit for a minute — the pancakes will absorb that milky goodness like little sponges. That's the whole magic move.
  5. 5Make your whipped cream: In a cold bowl, beat the heavy cream and powdered sugar with a hand mixer or whisk until soft peaks form. This takes about 2 to 3 minutes with a mixer, a bit longer by hand. Don't overwhip — you want it cloud-like, not buttery.
  6. 6Top each soaked stack generously with whipped cream, a good dusting of ground cinnamon, and a cherry or a few strawberry slices if you've got them. Serve immediately and watch them disappear.
💡 Improv Tip

The tres leches soak is where the magic lives, so don't rush it — pour it slowly and let those warm pancakes drink it in for at least a full minute before you add the whipped cream. If your pancakes are too cool, they won't absorb as well, so try to time it fresh off the griddle. And if you somehow have leftover soak (lucky you), it's incredible stirred into coffee.
