Vegetarian Blessing Box Budget Recipe: Creamy Mushroom & White Bean Pot Pie Skillet

Published November 9, 2026 · Improv Oven

This vegetarian blessing box budget recipe is built entirely from pantry and freezer staples — the kind of stuff that actually shows up in blessing boxes — and turns them into something that feels genuinely special. Think all the cozy, creamy comfort of a pot pie without the fuss of actual pastry: a rich, herb-flecked filling of mushrooms, white beans, peas, and carrots finished with a golden drop biscuit topping baked right in the skillet. It's hearty, it's warming, and it costs just a few dollars to feed a hungry table.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
45 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or butter
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 8 oz cremini or white button mushrooms, sliced (or one 4 oz can drained mushrooms if that's what you have)
  • 2 medium carrots, peeled and diced small
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried rosemary, crumbled
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 cups vegetable broth (or water plus 1 vegetable bouillon cube)
  • 3/4 cup whole milk or any milk you have
  • 1 can (15 oz) white beans (cannellini or great northern), drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup frozen peas
  • 1 teaspoon soy sauce or Worcestershire (the soy sauce keeps it vegetarian)
  • Salt to taste
  • FOR THE DROP BISCUIT TOPPING:
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 3 tablespoons cold butter, cut into small pieces
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • Optional: 2 tablespoons shredded cheddar stirred into biscuit dough

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 425°F. If your skillet isn't oven-safe, now's the time to grab a 9-inch baking dish and have it nearby.
  2. 2Heat the olive oil or butter in a large oven-safe skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and carrots and cook, stirring occasionally, for about 5 minutes until the onion is soft and translucent.
  3. 3Add the garlic, mushrooms, thyme, rosemary, and black pepper. Stir everything together and cook another 4–5 minutes, until the mushrooms have released their liquid and started to turn golden. Don't rush this step — those browned mushrooms are where most of your flavor is coming from.
  4. 4Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir well to coat everything. Cook for 1 minute, stirring constantly, so the raw flour taste cooks out.
  5. 5Pour in the vegetable broth and milk, stirring as you go to prevent lumps. Add the soy sauce. Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer and let it cook for 3–4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens to a creamy, gravy-like consistency.
  6. 6Stir in the white beans and frozen peas. Taste the filling and add salt as needed — this is your moment to make it yours. Remove from heat.
  7. 7Make the drop biscuit topping: In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, salt, and garlic powder. Add the cold butter pieces and use your fingers or a fork to work the butter into the flour until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs with some pea-sized bits remaining. Stir in the milk (and cheese if using) just until a shaggy dough comes together. Don't overmix.
  8. 8Drop the biscuit dough by heaping spoonfuls over the top of the filling in the skillet — it doesn't need to cover every inch, rustic is the goal here.
  9. 9Transfer the skillet to the oven and bake for 18–22 minutes, until the biscuit topping is golden brown and cooked through. If you're not sure, a toothpick inserted into the center of a biscuit should come out clean.
  10. 10Let it rest for 5 minutes before serving — the filling will firm up slightly and you won't burn your mouth. Serve straight from the skillet.
💡 Improv Tip

Cold butter is non-negotiable for fluffy drop biscuits. If yours has been sitting out, pop it in the freezer for 10 minutes before you start. And if you're working with canned mushrooms instead of fresh, drain them really well and pat them dry before adding — otherwise your filling can get a little watery. One more thing: this reheats beautifully the next day with a splash of broth stirred into the filling.
