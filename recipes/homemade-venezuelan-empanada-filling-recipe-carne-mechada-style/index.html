Homemade Venezuelan Empanada Filling Recipe (Carne Mechada Style)

Published August 28, 2026 · Improv Oven

This homemade Venezuelan empanada filling recipe is built around carne mechada — that deeply savory, slow-pulled shredded beef that's basically the soul of Venezuelan street food. We're keeping it budget-friendly with a cheap cut of beef, a handful of pantry staples, and the kind of flavor that makes people ask what your secret is. Spoon this into your empanada shells, stuff it into arepas, or honestly just eat it over rice — it works for breakfast, lunch, or dinner.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
45 mins
Total
60 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs beef chuck or flank steak (cheap cut works great here)
  • 1 teaspoon salt (for boiling)
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 3 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 1 small white onion, finely diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, finely diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 roma tomatoes, diced (or one 14-oz can diced tomatoes, drained)
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon sweet paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt, or to taste
  • 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 tablespoons fresh cilantro, chopped (optional but highly encouraged)

Instructions

  1. 1Place the beef in a medium pot and cover with water. Add 1 teaspoon salt, 1 teaspoon black pepper, and the 3 smashed garlic cloves. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer. Cook uncovered for 35 to 40 minutes until the beef is completely tender and pulls apart easily with a fork. If you're using flank steak it'll go faster — chuck takes a little longer but gets silkier.
  2. 2Remove the beef from the pot and let it rest for 5 minutes so it's cool enough to handle. Reserve about half a cup of the cooking broth — don't pour it out, that stuff is gold. Using two forks or your hands, shred the beef into thin, rough strips. You want texture, not mush.
  3. 3While the beef rests, heat 2 tablespoons of vegetable oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the diced onion and green bell pepper. Cook for 5 to 6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and just starting to turn golden at the edges.
  4. 4Add the minced garlic to the skillet and cook for 1 minute, stirring constantly so it doesn't burn. Then add the diced tomatoes, cumin, paprika, oregano, salt, and black pepper. Stir everything together and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until the tomatoes break down and the mixture looks jammy and fragrant.
  5. 5Add the shredded beef to the skillet and toss it into the sofrito base. Pour in the Worcestershire sauce and a splash of the reserved cooking broth — about 3 to 4 tablespoons — to keep things juicy without making the filling watery. Stir well to combine.
  6. 6Cook the filling over medium heat for another 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until everything is deeply combined and the liquid has mostly absorbed. The filling should be moist but not wet — if you're spooning it onto an empanada shell, it shouldn't drip. Taste and adjust salt as needed.
  7. 7Remove from heat and stir in the fresh cilantro if using. Let the filling cool for at least 10 minutes before assembling your empanadas — this helps it hold together and prevents the dough from getting soggy. Use immediately or refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4 days.
💡 Improv Tip

The single biggest mistake people make with empanada filling is using it too hot and too wet — steam and moisture will blow out your seam and you'll have sad, leaky empanadas. Let the filling cool completely and err on the drier side when you're done cooking it. You can always add a tiny drizzle of broth back in if it tightens up too much in the fridge.
