Roast Pack Portioned Budget Recipe: Cinnamon Banana Bread Pudding with Vanilla Drizzle

Published October 27, 2026 · Improv Oven

This roast pack portioned budget recipe proves that a humble loaf of bread and a few spotty bananas can turn into something that tastes genuinely fancy. Bread pudding is one of those old-school desserts that stretches cheap ingredients into pure comfort — custardy in the middle, golden on top, and ready to feed a crowd without wrecking your wallet. Make it once and you'll start hoarding bananas on purpose.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
40 mins
Total
55 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 6 cups day-old bread, torn into 1-inch chunks (sandwich bread, French bread, or rolls all work)
  • 3 ripe bananas, mashed
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 1/2 cups whole milk (or any milk you have)
  • 1/3 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter (to grease the pan)
  • 1/2 cup powdered sugar (for drizzle)
  • 2 tablespoons milk (for drizzle)
  • 1/4 teaspoon vanilla extract (for drizzle)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F. Rub a 9x13-inch baking dish (or any similar-sized roasting pan you have) with the butter so nothing sticks.
  2. 2In a large bowl, mash your bananas until mostly smooth — a few lumps are totally fine and add great texture. Whisk in the eggs, milk, sugar, cinnamon, vanilla extract, and salt until everything is combined into a smooth custard.
  3. 3Add your torn bread chunks to the bowl and gently fold them into the custard. Press down with a spatula and let it sit for 5 to 10 minutes so the bread really soaks everything up. Don't skip this — it's what makes bread pudding custardy instead of dry.
  4. 4Pour the soaked bread mixture into your prepared baking dish and spread it out in an even layer. Press it down lightly with the back of a spoon so the top is relatively flat and compact.
  5. 5Bake uncovered for 38 to 42 minutes, until the top is deep golden brown and the center feels set when you press it gently — it should spring back slightly rather than jiggle. If your oven runs hot, check at 35 minutes.
  6. 6While the pudding bakes, make the vanilla drizzle: whisk together the powdered sugar, 2 tablespoons of milk, and vanilla extract in a small bowl until smooth. Add a tiny splash more milk if it feels too thick — you want it pourable but not watery.
  7. 7Let the bread pudding cool for at least 10 minutes before slicing. This helps it hold its shape when you portion it. Cut into 6 even squares, drizzle each serving with the vanilla glaze, and serve warm. It's also genuinely delicious at room temperature the next day.
💡 Improv Tip

Stale bread is your best friend here — fresh bread tends to get a little gummy because it can't absorb the custard as well. If all you have is fresh, spread the chunks on a baking sheet and pop them in the oven at 300°F for about 10 minutes to dry them out a bit before you start. And if your bananas aren't very ripe yet, mash them with a fork and microwave them for 30 seconds — it softens them right up and brings out the sweetness.
