Easy Deviled Eggs Recipe (With a Little Miami Kick)

Published September 10, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy deviled eggs recipe is the one you'll make on repeat — creamy, tangy, and just a little spicy thanks to a hit of hot sauce and a pinch of smoked paprika. We keep the filling simple with pantry staples, but sneak in a touch of sofrito-inspired flavor that makes people ask what your secret is. Perfect for brunch, parties, potlucks, or honestly just Tuesday afternoon snacking.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
12 mins
Total
27 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 6 large eggs
  • 3 tablespoons mayonnaise
  • 1 teaspoon yellow mustard
  • 1 teaspoon white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon hot sauce (Tabasco or Crystal work great)
  • 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon onion powder
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Smoked paprika for topping
  • 2 tablespoons finely minced dill pickles or pickle relish
  • Optional garnish: sliced green olives or a few capers

Instructions

  1. 1Place your eggs in a single layer in a saucepan and cover with cold water by about an inch. Bring to a full boil over medium-high heat, then immediately cover the pot, turn off the heat, and let the eggs sit for exactly 11 minutes. This gives you fully set yolks without that gray ring.
  2. 2While the eggs are sitting, fill a large bowl with ice water. When the timer goes off, transfer the eggs straight into the ice bath and let them chill for at least 5 minutes. Cold eggs peel way more easily — don't skip this.
  3. 3Peel the eggs gently under a thin stream of cold running water. Once peeled, slice each egg in half lengthwise and pop the yolks out into a medium mixing bowl. Arrange the whites on a plate or serving tray and set aside.
  4. 4Mash the yolks with a fork until they're fine and crumbly — no big chunks. Add the mayonnaise, mustard, white vinegar, hot sauce, garlic powder, onion powder, and minced pickles. Season with salt and pepper. Mix everything together until completely smooth and creamy. Taste it — it should be tangy, a little spicy, and savory. Adjust as needed.
  5. 5Spoon or pipe the filling into the egg white halves. A zip-lock bag with the corner snipped off works perfectly as a piping bag if you want them to look fancy. Fill them generously — don't be shy.
  6. 6Dust the tops with smoked paprika and add a sliced green olive or a caper to each egg if you're going that route. Refrigerate for at least 15 minutes before serving so the filling firms up slightly and the flavors come together. Serve cold.
💡 Improv Tip

The biggest deviled egg mistake is under-seasoning the filling before it goes into the whites. Yolk filling needs more salt and acid than you think — taste it on a plain cracker or a piece of bread to get a true read, and don't be afraid to add another splash of vinegar or hot sauce. That little extra punch is what makes them disappear off the plate.
