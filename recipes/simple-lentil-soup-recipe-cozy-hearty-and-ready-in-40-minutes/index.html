Simple Lentil Soup Recipe (Cozy, Hearty, and Ready in 40 Minutes)

Published September 16, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple lentil soup recipe is exactly what you need when the fridge looks sad and you want something genuinely warming and filling. It's built almost entirely from pantry staples — dried lentils, canned tomatoes, a few spices — and it comes together in one pot without any soaking, draining, or fussing. Earthy, a little smoky, and deeply satisfying, this is the kind of soup that makes you feel like you actually have your life together.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
35 mins
Total
45 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup green or brown lentils, rinsed
  • 1 (14.5 oz) can diced tomatoes
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 medium carrots, peeled and diced
  • 2 stalks celery, diced
  • 4 cups vegetable broth (or chicken broth)
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice (fresh or bottled)
  • Optional: crusty bread or pita for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Once it shimmers, add the diced onion, carrots, and celery. Cook for about 5 to 7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion is soft and translucent and the vegetables start to smell sweet and good.
  2. 2Add the garlic, cumin, smoked paprika, turmeric, and thyme. Stir everything together and let it cook for about 1 minute — you want those spices to bloom in the oil and get fragrant. This is a small step that makes a big flavor difference.
  3. 3Pour in the canned tomatoes (with their juice), the rinsed lentils, the broth, and the water. Stir it all together, scraping up any bits stuck to the bottom of the pot. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat.
  4. 4Once it's boiling, reduce the heat to medium-low and let the soup simmer uncovered for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils are completely tender and starting to break down slightly. The soup will naturally thicken as it cooks.
  5. 5Taste for seasoning and add more salt and pepper if needed. Stir in the lemon juice right at the end — this brightens the whole thing and makes the flavors pop in a way that's hard to explain until you try it.
  6. 6Serve hot with crusty bread, pita, or just a spoon. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 5 days and honestly taste even better the next day.
💡 Improv Tip

Don't skip the lemon juice at the end — it sounds like a small thing but it completely transforms the soup from 'good' to 'wait, did I make this?' Also, if your lentils are old (been sitting in the pantry for over a year), they may take longer to soften, so just keep simmering and trust the process.
