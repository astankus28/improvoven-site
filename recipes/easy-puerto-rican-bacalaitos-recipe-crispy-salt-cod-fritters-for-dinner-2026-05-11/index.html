Easy Puerto Rican Bacalaitos Recipe (Crispy Salt Cod Fritters for Dinner)

Published May 11, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Puerto Rican bacalaitos recipe turns a humble piece of salt cod into golden, crispy fritters that'll have everyone crowding the kitchen before they even hit the table. These are the real deal — salty, savory, lacy-edged, and deeply satisfying as a main course when you pair them with rice and beans or a simple salad. If you grew up eating these at a beach kiosko in Luquillo, this one's going to hit different.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
35 mins (plus 1 hour soaking time for the cod)
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb dried salt cod (bacalao)
  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/4 teaspoon oregano (dried)
  • 1 1/2 cups water (more as needed)
  • 3 tablespoons fresh cilantro, finely chopped
  • 2 tablespoons fresh culantro (recao), finely chopped — or use extra cilantro if you can't find it
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 small green bell pepper, finely diced
  • Vegetable oil for frying (about 2 cups)

Instructions

  1. 1Start by soaking your salt cod: Rinse the bacalao under cold water, then place it in a large bowl covered with cold water. Let it soak for at least 1 hour, changing the water once or twice. If you have more time, soak it overnight in the fridge — the longer it soaks, the less salty it'll be. This step is non-negotiable, mija.
  2. 2After soaking, place the cod in a pot, cover with fresh water, and bring it to a boil. Let it simmer for about 10 minutes until it's tender and flaky. Drain it, let it cool slightly, then use your hands or a fork to shred it into small pieces — remove any bones or skin you find while you're in there.
  3. 3In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, garlic powder, onion powder, black pepper, cumin, and oregano. Make a little well in the center.
  4. 4Pour in 1 1/2 cups of water and stir until you have a smooth, thick batter — think pancake batter consistency. If it feels too thick, add water a tablespoon at a time. If it's too loose, add a little more flour. You want it to coat the back of a spoon but still be pourable.
  5. 5Fold in the shredded bacalao, minced garlic, cilantro, culantro, and green bell pepper. Stir everything together until the cod is evenly distributed throughout the batter. Taste it — yes, taste the raw batter, you're checking seasoning. Add a tiny pinch of salt only if it needs it, since the cod carries a lot of salt on its own.
  6. 6Pour about 1 1/2 to 2 inches of vegetable oil into a deep skillet or heavy-bottomed pan and heat it over medium-high heat. You want the oil around 350°F — drop a tiny bit of batter in and if it sizzles and floats right up, you're good to go.
  7. 7Working in batches, drop heaping spoonfuls of batter into the hot oil — about 2 to 3 tablespoons per fritter. Don't crowd the pan or the oil temperature will drop and your bacalaitos will absorb oil instead of crisping up. Fry each batch for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until they're deep golden brown and the edges look lacy and crisp.
  8. 8Use a slotted spoon or spider strainer to transfer the finished bacalaitos to a plate lined with paper towels. Let them drain for just a minute — you want to eat these hot.
  9. 9Serve them as your main course alongside white rice and gandules (pigeon peas), a simple avocado salad, or some tostones on the side. A squeeze of fresh lime over everything doesn't hurt one bit.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to that gorgeous lacy edge on bacalaitos is batter consistency and oil temperature — both matter equally. If your fritters are coming out thick and doughy, thin your batter out a little more with water. If they're falling apart in the oil, it's either too loose or your oil isn't hot enough. Keep a little bowl of flour and a little cup of water next to you as you fry so you can adjust on the fly. This is improv cooking at its finest.
