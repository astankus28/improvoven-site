Budget Canned Corn Chowder Recipe (Creamy, Cozy, and Ready in 30 Minutes)

Published August 5, 2026 · Improv Oven

This budget canned corn chowder recipe is the kind of thing that makes you feel like a genius for cleaning out your pantry — creamy, sweet, smoky, and deeply satisfying without a single fancy ingredient. We're building real chowder flavor from canned corn, potatoes, and a handful of pantry staples, no fresh corn or heavy cream required. It's the kind of soup that tastes like you spent all afternoon on it, even though dinner is ready before your bread is done toasting.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 cans (15 oz each) whole kernel corn, drained (reserve the liquid from one can)
  • 1 can (15 oz) creamed corn
  • 2 medium russet or Yukon gold potatoes, peeled and diced small (about 1/2 inch)
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons butter or neutral oil
  • 2 cups chicken or vegetable broth
  • 1 cup whole milk or evaporated milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional, for a little heat)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 4 strips bacon, chopped (optional but highly recommended)
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • Sliced green onions or chives for garnish
  • Shredded cheddar cheese for topping (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1If using bacon, add the chopped strips to a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Cook until crispy, about 5 minutes, then remove with a slotted spoon and set aside on a paper towel. Leave about a tablespoon of bacon fat in the pot — that's your flavor base. If skipping bacon, melt the butter or heat oil in the pot instead.
  2. 2Add the diced onion to the pot and cook over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until softened and just starting to turn golden, about 4 to 5 minutes. Add the garlic and cook for one more minute until fragrant.
  3. 3Sprinkle the flour over the onion and garlic and stir constantly for about 1 minute. This cooks out the raw flour taste and gives your chowder that classic, slightly thick body. Don't skip this step — it's what separates chowder from corn soup.
  4. 4Pour in the broth and the reserved corn liquid from one can, stirring as you go to work out any flour lumps. Add the diced potatoes, smoked paprika, cayenne if using, and a good pinch of salt and pepper. Bring everything to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer.
  5. 5Cook uncovered for about 12 to 15 minutes, until the potatoes are completely tender when pierced with a fork. The broth will thicken slightly as the potatoes cook.
  6. 6Add all three cans of corn — the two drained whole kernel cans and the creamed corn. Stir in the milk or evaporated milk and let everything come back to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat. Do not boil at this stage or the milk can break. Taste and adjust salt, pepper, and paprika.
  7. 7For a thicker, creamier chowder, use an immersion blender to blend about a third of the soup right in the pot, then stir it back together. This gives you creamy texture without any added cream. Alternatively, scoop out two cups of soup, blend in a regular blender, and stir it back in.
  8. 8Ladle into bowls and top with the reserved crispy bacon, sliced green onions, and a handful of shredded cheddar if you've got it. Serve with crusty bread, oyster crackers, or buttered toast — whatever you've got.
💡 Improv Tip

Creamed corn is the secret weapon here — don't swap it out thinking two cans of whole kernel corn will do the same job. That thick, blended corn in a can adds body and sweetness that you'd otherwise have to cook for an hour to build. It's doing a lot of quiet, important work in this pot.
