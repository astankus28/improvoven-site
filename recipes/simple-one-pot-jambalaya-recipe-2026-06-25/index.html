Simple One Pot Jambalaya Recipe

Published June 25, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple one pot jambalaya recipe is exactly what you need when you want something hearty, smoky, and full of flavor without dirtying every dish in the kitchen. It's got that deep Southern soul with a little Miami heat thrown in — the kind of meal that makes people think you spent all day cooking. Thirty minutes, one pot, done.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb smoked sausage or kielbasa, sliced into rounds
  • 1 lb raw shrimp, peeled and deveined (fresh or frozen and thawed)
  • 1 cup long grain white rice, uncooked
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes with juices
  • 2 cups chicken broth
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 3 stalks celery, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 2 teaspoons Cajun or Creole seasoning
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional, for extra heat)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 2 green onions, sliced (for topping)
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (optional, for topping)

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the oil in a large deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the sliced sausage and cook for about 3-4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it gets a little golden and caramelized on the edges. That browning is flavor — don't skip it. Remove the sausage with a slotted spoon and set aside.
  2. 2In the same pot with all those beautiful sausage drippings, add the diced onion, bell pepper, and celery — that's your holy trinity right there. Cook for about 4-5 minutes, stirring here and there, until everything softens up and the onion goes translucent.
  3. 3Add the garlic, Cajun seasoning, smoked paprika, oregano, and cayenne if you're using it. Stir it all together and let it cook for about 60 seconds until your kitchen smells absolutely incredible.
  4. 4Add the uncooked rice and stir it into the veggie mixture so every grain gets coated in those seasonings. Let it toast for about a minute — this gives the jambalaya a nuttier, richer base.
  5. 5Pour in the diced tomatoes with all their juices and the chicken broth. Stir everything together and scrape up any bits stuck to the bottom of the pot. Nestle the browned sausage back in. Bring it to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover with a tight lid, and let it simmer for 18-20 minutes until the rice has absorbed the liquid and is cooked through.
  6. 6Remove the lid and gently stir in the shrimp, pushing them down into the rice. Re-cover and cook for another 3-5 minutes just until the shrimp are pink and curled. Don't overdo it — overcooked shrimp are a crime.
  7. 7Taste it, adjust salt and pepper if needed, then top with sliced green onions and fresh parsley. Serve straight from the pot because that's the whole point.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret weapon here is letting that sausage properly brown before anything else goes in the pot — those caramelized bits that stick to the bottom are pure gold. When you add the broth, you're pulling all of that flavor back into the dish. That's free seasoning right there, and it's what separates a good jambalaya from a great one.
