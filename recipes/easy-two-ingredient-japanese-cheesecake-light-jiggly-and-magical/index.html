Easy Two Ingredient Japanese Cheesecake (Light, Jiggly, and Magical)

Published September 16, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy two ingredient Japanese cheesecake is exactly what it sounds like — just cream cheese and eggs, and somehow the result is a cloud-like, jiggly little cake that feels way fancier than anything this simple has a right to be. No flour, no sugar added, no water bath drama — just pure, pillowy magic from your pantry. If you've never tried making it before, get ready to feel like a baking genius.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 8 oz cream cheese (one block, softened to room temperature)
  • 3 large eggs (separated into yolks and whites)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 325°F (165°C). Line a small round cake pan or an 8-inch springform pan with parchment paper and lightly grease the sides. If you have a 6-inch pan, even better — the cake comes out taller and more dramatic.
  2. 2Microwave the softened cream cheese in a large microwave-safe bowl for about 20–30 seconds, just until it's very soft and easy to stir. Beat it with a fork or hand mixer until completely smooth with no lumps at all — this step matters, so take your time.
  3. 3Add the 3 egg yolks to the cream cheese and mix until fully incorporated and silky. Set this bowl aside.
  4. 4In a separate clean bowl, beat the 3 egg whites with a hand mixer (or a stand mixer) on medium-high speed until stiff peaks form — this means when you lift the beaters, the whites hold a firm, glossy peak that doesn't flop over. This takes about 3–4 minutes. Don't rush it; the whipped whites are what make this cake jiggly and light.
  5. 5Add about one-third of the whipped egg whites to the cream cheese mixture and gently fold it in using a spatula — don't stir aggressively. This first addition loosens the batter. Then fold in the remaining whites in two more additions, using slow, sweeping motions to keep as much air in the batter as possible. Stop when just combined; a few streaks are fine.
  6. 6Pour the batter into your prepared pan — it will look almost like a thick mousse. Smooth the top gently with your spatula.
  7. 7Place the pan in the oven and bake for 20–25 minutes, until the top is lightly golden and the cake jiggles like Jell-O when you gently shake the pan — that jiggle is the goal, not a sign it's underdone. If the top is browning too fast, loosely tent it with foil.
  8. 8Turn the oven off, crack the door open slightly, and let the cake cool inside the oven for 10–15 minutes. This slow cool-down helps prevent cracking. Then move it to the counter to cool completely before removing from the pan. It will deflate a little — that's totally normal and part of its charm.
  9. 9Serve at room temperature or chilled from the fridge. Dust with a tiny bit of powdered sugar if you have it, or eat it plain — it's beautiful either way.
💡 Improv Tip

The number one thing that tanks this cake is overmixing the egg whites into the batter. Think of each fold like you're tucking in a blanket — slow, gentle, and deliberate. Also, room temperature cream cheese is non-negotiable here. Cold cream cheese will give you lumps, and lumps won't fold smoothly into the whites. Pull it out of the fridge at least 30 minutes before you start.
