Simple Banana Bread Recipe (Easy, Moist, One Bowl)

Published July 26, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple banana bread recipe is the easy, no-fuss version you actually want to make on a weeknight — one bowl, no mixer, just those sad overripe bananas finally getting their moment. It's tender, warmly spiced, and comes together in about 10 minutes of real effort before the oven does the rest. If you've got three brown bananas on the counter, you're already halfway there.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
60 mins
Total
70 mins
Serves
10 slices

Ingredients

  • 3 very ripe bananas (the blacker the peel, the sweeter the bread)
  • 1/3 cup melted butter (salted or unsalted both work)
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar (or brown sugar for deeper flavor)
  • 1 large egg, beaten
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • Optional: 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or chocolate chips

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a standard 9x5-inch loaf pan with butter or cooking spray, then dust it lightly with flour — this keeps the bread from sticking and gives the crust a little texture.
  2. 2In a large mixing bowl, mash your bananas well with a fork until almost completely smooth. A few small lumps are totally fine — they bake into little pockets of banana flavor. The riper the bananas, the easier they mash and the sweeter your bread will be.
  3. 3Pour the melted butter into the mashed bananas and stir to combine. Add the sugar, beaten egg, and vanilla extract, and mix until everything is incorporated.
  4. 4Sprinkle the baking soda, salt, and cinnamon directly over the wet mixture and stir them in. This shortcut saves you a bowl — the baking soda will mix evenly as long as you stir it in before the flour goes in.
  5. 5Add the flour all at once and fold it in with a spatula or wooden spoon. Mix only until no dry streaks remain — overmixing develops gluten and makes the bread tough and dense instead of tender. If you're adding walnuts or chocolate chips, fold them in now.
  6. 6Pour the batter into your prepared loaf pan and spread it evenly. If you want a bakery-style split down the center, run a thin line of butter along the top of the batter with your finger or a spoon — the steam will crack it open beautifully as it bakes.
  7. 7Bake for 55 to 65 minutes. At the 55-minute mark, insert a toothpick or thin knife into the center of the loaf. If it comes out clean or with just a couple of dry crumbs, it's done. If it's wet with batter, give it another 5 to 10 minutes. Every oven is a little different.
  8. 8Let the bread cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn it out onto a wire rack. Try to wait at least 20 more minutes before slicing — it firms up as it cools and the slices will be much cleaner. (Easier said than done, we know.)
💡 Improv Tip

The number one banana bread mistake is using bananas that aren't ripe enough. Yellow bananas with just a few spots will give you bland, dry bread. You want the peel to be mostly brown or fully black — those bananas are sweeter, softer, and packed with way more flavor. If you forgot to let your bananas ripen, you can fake it: put unpeeled bananas on a baking sheet and roast them at 300°F for about 20 minutes until the skins turn black. They'll be soft, sweet, and perfect for this recipe.
