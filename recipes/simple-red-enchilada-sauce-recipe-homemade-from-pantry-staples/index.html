Simple Red Enchilada Sauce Recipe (Homemade, From Pantry Staples)

Published August 25, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple red enchilada sauce recipe is the kind of homemade thing that makes you wonder why you ever bought the canned stuff — it comes together in about 20 minutes with spices you probably already have sitting in the back of your cabinet. We're doing it Miami-style with a little extra cumin and a whisper of oregano that gives it that deep, earthy backbone you'd find in a good Cuban sofrito-rubbed roast. Pour it over chicken enchiladas, use it to braise eggs, or just keep a jar in the fridge for the week — this sauce works hard.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil or lard
  • 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 3 tablespoons chili powder (mild or hot — your call)
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 can (15 oz) tomato sauce
  • 1 1/2 cups chicken broth or vegetable broth
  • 1 teaspoon apple cider vinegar
  • Pinch of sugar (optional, to balance acidity)
  • 8 medium flour or corn tortillas
  • 2 cups shredded rotisserie chicken or canned chicken, drained
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded Monterey Jack or cheddar cheese, divided
  • 1/2 cup diced white onion
  • Fresh cilantro, sour cream, and sliced jalapeño for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Make the red enchilada sauce: Heat the oil in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Once it shimmers, whisk in the flour and cook for 1 to 2 minutes, stirring constantly — you're building a little roux here, and it should smell nutty and toasty, not raw. This step is what makes your sauce rich instead of thin and sad.
  2. 2Add the chili powder, cumin, garlic powder, oregano, smoked paprika, onion powder, salt, and pepper directly into the roux. Stir everything together and let the spices bloom in the oil for about 30 seconds. Your kitchen is going to smell incredible right now — that's the sign you're doing it right.
  3. 3Pour in the tomato sauce and broth slowly, whisking as you go to keep things smooth. Bring the sauce up to a gentle simmer over medium heat, then reduce to medium-low. Cook uncovered for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon. Stir in the apple cider vinegar and taste — add a pinch of sugar if it tastes too sharp, and adjust salt as needed. Set aside.
  4. 4Preheat your oven to 375°F. Spread about 1/2 cup of the red enchilada sauce across the bottom of a 9x13 baking dish so the enchiladas don't stick and don't dry out on the bottom.
  5. 5Build your enchiladas: In a bowl, mix the shredded chicken with the diced onion and about 1/2 cup of the cheese. Warm your tortillas for 20 to 30 seconds in the microwave so they're pliable and won't crack when you roll them. Dip each tortilla briefly into the remaining enchilada sauce to coat both sides — this is the move that gives you that deeply flavored, slightly soft enchilada wrapper instead of a dry one.
  6. 6Spoon about 1/4 cup of the chicken and cheese filling down the center of each tortilla. Roll tightly and place seam-side down in the sauced baking dish. Repeat with all 8 tortillas, fitting them snugly next to each other.
  7. 7Pour the remaining enchilada sauce evenly over the top of all the rolled enchiladas, making sure to get the ends covered so they don't dry out. Sprinkle the remaining 1 cup of shredded cheese over the top.
  8. 8Bake uncovered at 375°F for 20 to 25 minutes, until the cheese is melted, bubbly, and just starting to get golden at the edges. Let the dish rest for 5 minutes before serving — this helps everything set up so the enchiladas hold together when you plate them.
  9. 9Serve two enchiladas per person topped with sour cream, fresh cilantro, and sliced jalapeño if you like a little heat. This sauce has enough depth that it carries the whole dish — your dinner table is going to be quiet in the best way.
💡 Improv Tip

If your chili powder is old and smells like dust, your sauce is going to taste like dust — this is the one moment in budget cooking where fresh spices genuinely matter. A new $2 bottle of chili powder will transform this recipe. Also, the sauce keeps beautifully in a jar in the fridge for up to a week, so make the full batch even if you're only doing a smaller dinner. Future-you will drizzle it over eggs on a Tuesday and feel like a genius.
