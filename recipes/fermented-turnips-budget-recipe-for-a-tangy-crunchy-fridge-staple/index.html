Fermented Turnips (Budget Recipe for a Tangy, Crunchy Fridge Staple)

Published August 3, 2026 · Improv Oven

This fermented turnips budget recipe costs almost nothing to make and turns a humble root vegetable into something genuinely addictive — bright pink, crunchy, tangy, and alive. All you need is turnips, a beet for color, garlic, salt, and water, and about five minutes of hands-on time. Think of it as the Miami version of a Middle Eastern staple: a jar of pickled magic you'll start putting on everything from rice bowls to sandwiches to scrambled eggs.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
15 mins + 3–5 days fermentation
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs turnips (about 3–4 medium), peeled and cut into sticks or half-moons
  • 1 small beet, peeled and thinly sliced (just for color — totally optional but worth it)
  • 4 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 1 tablespoon kosher salt or non-iodized sea salt (do NOT use iodized — it kills the fermentation)
  • 2 cups filtered or non-chlorinated water (tap water left uncovered overnight also works)
  • Optional: 1 small jalapeño or habanero, sliced — for a Caribbean kick
  • Optional: 1 teaspoon whole peppercorns or a bay leaf

Instructions

  1. 1Make your brine first: dissolve 1 tablespoon of kosher salt in 2 cups of room-temperature water. Stir until completely dissolved. If it's not dissolving easily, let it sit for a few minutes and stir again. Set aside.
  2. 2Peel your turnips and cut them into sticks (like thick French fries) or half-moons — either works great. Peel and thinly slice the beet if using. The beet is what turns everything that gorgeous hot-pink color. It's basically food magic.
  3. 3Pack everything into a clean quart-sized mason jar: turnips first, then tuck in the beet slices, garlic, jalapeño if using, and any spices. Pack it as tightly as you can without smashing everything — you want the jar full.
  4. 4Pour the salt brine over everything until the turnips are completely submerged. Leave about an inch of headspace at the top. If any turnips are floating above the brine, weigh them down with a smaller zip-lock bag filled with a little extra brine, or just use a clean folded cabbage leaf as a weight.
  5. 5Loosely cover the jar — a lid set on top without screwing it down works perfectly, or use a cloth held with a rubber band. You want air to escape but no dust or bugs to get in. Do NOT seal it airtight or pressure can build up.
  6. 6Leave the jar at room temperature, away from direct sunlight, for 3 to 5 days. Start tasting on day 3. You're looking for a pleasantly sour, tangy flavor. In a warm Miami kitchen, fermentation moves fast — check daily. In a cooler kitchen, give it the full 5 days.
  7. 7Once they taste tangy and delicious to you, screw the lid on tight and move the jar to the fridge. Fermentation slows way down at cold temperatures. They'll keep refrigerated for up to 2 months — if they last that long, which they won't.
  8. 8Serve cold, straight from the jar. Pile them on sandwiches, chop them into salsas, serve alongside grilled chicken or pork, stir into rice, or eat them straight with a fork like a snack. They're especially good with anything fried or fatty — the acidity cuts right through.
💡 Improv Tip

The single most common mistake with fermented vegetables is using iodized table salt — it actually inhibits the good bacteria you need for fermentation and your veggies just go mushy instead of tangy. Grab kosher salt or look for a bag labeled 'pickling salt' or 'sea salt' — it's usually a dollar or two at any grocery store and worth every penny. Also, don't stress if you see a few tiny bubbles in the jar after day 2 — that's a good sign, not a bad one. That's your ferment saying hello.
