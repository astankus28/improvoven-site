Easy Tres Leches Cake Recipe (Soft, Soaked, and Absolutely Dreamy)

Published September 26, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy tres leches cake recipe is the one you'll make for every birthday, potluck, and 'I just need cake tonight' moment — and it never, ever disappoints. The magic is in the soak: a sweet bath of three milks that turns a simple sponge into something impossibly tender and custardy. Miami vibes optional, but highly recommended alongside a cafecito.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
3 hrs (includes soaking time)
Serves
12

Ingredients

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 5 large eggs, separated
  • 1 cup granulated sugar, divided
  • 1/3 cup whole milk
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk
  • 1 cup whole milk (for the soak)
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon (for the soak)
  • 1 1/2 cups heavy whipping cream
  • 3 tablespoons powdered sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract (for the whipped cream)
  • Ground cinnamon or maraschino cherries, for garnish (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F. Grease a 9x13-inch baking dish and set it aside. In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, and salt — just a quick 20 seconds to combine everything evenly.
  2. 2Separate your eggs. In a large mixing bowl, beat the egg yolks with 3/4 cup of the granulated sugar on medium-high speed until the mixture is pale yellow and ribbony, about 3 to 4 minutes. Mix in the 1/3 cup of whole milk and the teaspoon of vanilla extract until combined.
  3. 3In a separate clean bowl (this part matters — any grease will wreck your egg whites), beat the egg whites on medium speed until foamy. Gradually add the remaining 1/4 cup of sugar and beat on high until stiff, glossy peaks form, about 2 to 3 minutes.
  4. 4Gently fold the flour mixture into the egg yolk batter in two additions, stirring until just combined — don't overwork it. Then fold in the beaten egg whites in thirds, using a light hand so you keep all that air in there. The batter will look fluffy and a little cloud-like.
  5. 5Pour the batter into your prepared baking dish and smooth the top. Bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until the cake is golden on top and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes.
  6. 6While the cake cools, make your three-milk soak: whisk together the sweetened condensed milk, evaporated milk, whole milk, and the 1/2 teaspoon of cinnamon in a pitcher or large measuring cup. That cinnamon is a small move that makes a big difference — trust.
  7. 7Use a fork or skewer to poke holes all over the top of the warm cake — go ahead and go a little wild with it, the more holes the better. Slowly pour the three-milk mixture evenly over the entire cake, letting it absorb as you go. It will look like a lot of liquid. That's correct. That's the point.
  8. 8Cover the cake with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, but overnight is genuinely better. The soak needs time to work its magic and turn the whole thing into that signature custardy texture.
  9. 9When you're ready to serve, make the whipped cream topping: beat the heavy cream, powdered sugar, and vanilla together on high speed until soft, billowy peaks form — about 2 to 3 minutes. Spread it over the chilled cake in big swoops.
  10. 10Dust the top with a little ground cinnamon or add a few maraschino cherries if you're feeling festive. Slice into squares and serve cold. Watch how fast it disappears.
💡 Improv Tip

Cold cake, warm welcome — tres leches is actually one of the best make-ahead desserts out there. Make it the night before your gathering and let it soak overnight in the fridge. The texture the next day is dramatically better than after just two hours. Add the whipped cream topping right before serving so it stays fresh and fluffy. If you want to push the Miami angle, swap the whole milk in the soak for coconut milk — it adds a subtle tropical sweetness that makes people stop mid-bite and ask what your secret is.
