Kung Pao Chicken Budget Recipe (Better Than Takeout, Seriously)

Published October 25, 2026 · Improv Oven

This kung pao chicken budget recipe proves you don't need to spend $18 on delivery to get that perfect sweet-spicy-savory thing happening in a wok. With a handful of pantry staples — soy sauce, rice vinegar, a little honey, and whatever dried chilies you've got — you can pull off a legit version on a weeknight for a fraction of the cost. Serve it over rice and you've got dinner for four that tastes like you actually know what you're doing.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce (divided)
  • 1 tablespoon cornstarch
  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil (divided)
  • 6 to 8 dried red chilies (or 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes — use what you've got)
  • 1/3 cup roasted peanuts (dry-roasted are great, lightly salted works too)
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, grated (or 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger from the pantry)
  • 3 green onions, sliced — whites and greens separated
  • For the sauce: 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar (apple cider vinegar works in a pinch)
  • 1 tablespoon honey (or sugar)
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • Cooked white rice, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1In a medium bowl, toss the chicken pieces with 2 tablespoons soy sauce and 1 tablespoon cornstarch until coated. Let it sit while you prep everything else — even 10 minutes helps the cornstarch do its thing and gives you that slightly crispy exterior.
  2. 2Mix your sauce in a small bowl or measuring cup: 2 tablespoons soy sauce, rice vinegar, honey, sesame oil, 1 teaspoon cornstarch, and 2 tablespoons water. Stir until the cornstarch is dissolved. Set it right next to the stove — this dish moves fast.
  3. 3Heat a large skillet or wok over high heat until it's genuinely hot — a drop of water should evaporate immediately. Add 2 tablespoons of oil. Add the chicken in a single layer and let it sear undisturbed for 2 minutes. Flip and cook another 2 to 3 minutes until cooked through and golden in spots. Transfer to a plate.
  4. 4Lower the heat to medium-high and add the remaining tablespoon of oil to the same pan. Add the dried chilies (or red pepper flakes) and the white parts of the green onions. Stir-fry for about 30 seconds — the chilies will darken slightly and your kitchen will smell incredible.
  5. 5Add the garlic and ginger and stir constantly for 30 seconds. Don't let the garlic burn — this is the flavor base so treat it with a little respect.
  6. 6Return the chicken to the pan. Give your sauce a quick stir (the cornstarch settles), then pour it over everything. Toss and stir for 1 to 2 minutes until the sauce thickens and coats the chicken like a glossy, clingy dream.
  7. 7Remove from heat. Stir in the peanuts and the green parts of the green onions. Taste and adjust — more soy for salt, a tiny splash more vinegar for brightness, or a drizzle of honey if you want it slightly sweeter. Serve immediately over rice.
💡 Improv Tip

Chicken thighs are the budget MVP here — cheaper than breasts, way more forgiving, and they stay juicy even if you get distracted and cook them a little long. But honestly, this works with whatever protein you have: diced pork loin, shrimp (cut the cook time down), or even firm tofu pressed and cubed. The sauce is the soul of the dish — make extra and keep it in the fridge all week.
