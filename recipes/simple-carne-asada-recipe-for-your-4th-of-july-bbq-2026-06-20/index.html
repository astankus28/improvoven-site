Simple Carne Asada Recipe for Your 4th of July BBQ

Published June 20, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple carne asada recipe is exactly what your 4th of July BBQ has been missing — smoky, juicy, and packed with that citrus-garlic punch that makes the whole neighborhood show up uninvited. We're talking Miami-style backyard magic using flank or skirt steak and a marinade you can throw together with stuff already in your fridge. No fancy equipment, no culinary degree — just fire, flavor, and good vibes.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins (plus 2 hours marinating)
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs flank steak or skirt steak
  • 1/3 cup orange juice (fresh or from the carton, no judgment)
  • 3 tablespoons lime juice (about 2 limes)
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon oregano (dried is fine)
  • Optional: handful of fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • Optional: 1 jalapeño, sliced, for extra heat

Instructions

  1. 1Make your marinade: In a bowl or large zip-lock bag, mix together the orange juice, lime juice, minced garlic, soy sauce, oil, cumin, chili powder, smoked paprika, black pepper, salt, and oregano. Stir it all together until combined. Taste it — it should smell like a cookout is already happening.
  2. 2Score and marinate the steak: Take your flank or skirt steak and score it lightly with a knife in a crosshatch pattern on both sides — this helps the marinade get all the way in. Drop the steak into the marinade, making sure it's fully coated. Seal the bag or cover the bowl and refrigerate for at least 2 hours. Overnight is even better if you're planning ahead.
  3. 3Get your grill hot: Fire up your grill to high heat — you want it screaming hot for carne asada. If you're using charcoal, let those coals get good and ashed over. If gas, crank it up and let it preheat for at least 10 minutes. Oil the grates lightly so nothing sticks.
  4. 4Pull and dry the steak: Take the steak out of the marinade about 15-20 minutes before grilling so it comes closer to room temperature. Pat it lightly with a paper towel — a drier surface means a better sear and that gorgeous char we're going for.
  5. 5Grill the steak: Place the steak on the hottest part of the grill. For flank steak about 1 inch thick, grill 4-5 minutes per side for medium (adjust slightly for thinner skirt steak, which cooks faster — closer to 3-4 minutes per side). You want good char marks and a little caramelization on the edges. Resist the urge to move it around constantly — let the grill do its thing.
  6. 6Rest before you cut: This step is non-negotiable. Move the steak to a cutting board and let it rest for 5-8 minutes. If you cut it right away, all those juices run out and you end up with dry steak. Nobody wants that at a 4th of July cookout.
  7. 7Slice against the grain: Look at the steak and find the direction the muscle fibers run — then cut perpendicular to that. Slice thin, on a slight diagonal. This is what makes carne asada tender instead of chewy. Finish with a squeeze of fresh lime juice right over the top and scatter cilantro if you've got it.
  8. 8Serve it up: Pile the sliced carne asada on a platter and let people go wild. It's incredible in warm tortillas with grilled onions, on top of rice, next to grilled corn, or just straight off the board with nothing but hot sauce.
💡 Improv Tip

Skirt steak is usually cheaper than flank and honestly even more flavorful — it just cooks a little faster and can go from perfect to overdone quick, so keep your eyes on it. If your budget is tight, check the Latin or international section of your grocery store for the best price on either cut. And if you somehow have leftover carne asada the next day, chop it up and throw it in scrambled eggs or on top of black beans and rice — you'll forget it was leftovers.
