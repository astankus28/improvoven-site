Easy Homemade Hot Sauce Recipe (And a Killer Chicken to Put It On)

Published July 16, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy homemade hot sauce recipe is the kind of thing you make once and then put on absolutely everything — eggs, tacos, grilled cheese, your finger, whatever. We're making a simple ferment-free stovetop hot sauce with pantry chiles and vinegar, then slathering it over crispy pan-seared chicken thighs so you've got a full dinner that tastes like you've been cooking all day. It takes about 30 minutes and costs almost nothing.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
  • 1 tsp kosher salt (for the chicken)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (vegetable or canola)
  • FOR THE HOT SAUCE:
  • 6–8 dried chiles de arbol (or dried cayenne peppers), stems removed
  • 4 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 1/2 small yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/3 cup distilled white vinegar
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika (optional, adds depth)

Instructions

  1. 1Pat your chicken thighs completely dry with paper towels — this is the move for crispy skin, don't skip it. Season generously on both sides with salt, pepper, and garlic powder.
  2. 2Heat 1 tbsp oil in a large cast-iron or stainless skillet over medium-high heat. Place the chicken thighs skin-side down and don't touch them for 7–8 minutes. You want that skin deeply golden and releasing from the pan on its own. Flip and cook another 6–8 minutes until cooked through (internal temp 165°F). Set aside to rest.
  3. 3While the chicken cooks, start your hot sauce. In a small saucepan over medium heat, warm 1 tbsp oil. Add the dried chiles, smashed garlic, and onion. Cook, stirring occasionally, for 3–4 minutes until the onion softens and everything smells toasty and fragrant — you're blooming the chiles here, which wakes up their flavor.
  4. 4Pour in 1 cup of water and bring to a simmer. Cook for 8–10 minutes until the chiles have softened and rehydrated and the liquid has reduced by about a third.
  5. 5Carefully transfer everything to a blender. Add the white vinegar, salt, sugar, and smoked paprika if using. Blend on high for 60–90 seconds until completely smooth. Taste it — if it needs more heat, add another chile. More tang, splash more vinegar. More salt, you know what to do.
  6. 6Pour the sauce through a fine mesh strainer into a bowl or jar, pressing with a spoon to get every drop through. What you're left with is a glossy, pourable, genuinely excellent hot sauce.
  7. 7Serve the chicken thighs on a plate or cutting board with a generous drizzle of hot sauce over the top. Put the rest of the sauce in a jar on the table. Nobody will leave it alone.
💡 Improv Tip

Dried chiles de arbol are the move here because they're widely available, cheap, and give you that classic vinegary hot sauce heat — but this recipe is wide open for improvising. Got chipotles in adobo? Blend a couple in. Dried anchos? They'll make it smoky and a little sweet. Even a tablespoon of store-bought chili flakes simmered in that onion-garlic base will give you something worth bottling. The hot sauce keeps in a sealed jar in the fridge for up to 3 weeks.
