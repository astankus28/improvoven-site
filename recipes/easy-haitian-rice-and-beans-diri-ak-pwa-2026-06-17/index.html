Easy Haitian Rice and Beans (Diri ak Pwa)

Published June 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Haitian rice and beans recipe is the kind of dish that makes you understand why simple food cooked right is the greatest luxury there is. Diri ak pwa is Haitian comfort food at its core — creamy kidney beans simmered with aromatics, then cooked right into the rice so every grain soaks up that deep, savory flavor. It's pantry magic, it's budget-friendly, and once you make it this way, plain rice is gonna feel like a downgrade.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
35 mins
Total
45 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 can (15 oz) kidney beans, drained — reserve the liquid
  • 1 1/2 cups long grain white rice, rinsed
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 1/2 small onion, finely diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 green bell pepper, finely diced
  • 2 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1/2 teaspoon dried thyme)
  • 1 whole scotch bonnet or habanero pepper (keep it whole — don't cut it)
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 2 1/2 cups water (combined with reserved bean liquid to reach this amount)
  • 1 tablespoon butter (optional but recommended)

Instructions

  1. 1Pour your drained kidney beans into a bowl and set aside. Take that reserved bean liquid and add enough water to make 2 1/2 cups total liquid. This bean water is liquid gold — it gives the rice that gorgeous deep color and extra flavor, so don't you dare throw it out.
  2. 2Heat the oil in a medium heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the onion and green bell pepper and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until softened and starting to smell amazing.
  3. 3Add the garlic and cook for another 60 seconds, stirring so it doesn't burn. Then add the thyme, black pepper, and ground cloves — stir everything together and let those spices bloom in the oil for about 30 seconds.
  4. 4Add the drained kidney beans to the pot and stir to coat them in all that aromatic goodness. Let them cook with the sofrito base for 2 minutes.
  5. 5Pour in your 2 1/2 cups of bean-water mixture and bring everything to a gentle boil. Taste the liquid right now and adjust salt — it should taste well-seasoned because the rice is going to absorb all of it.
  6. 6Add the rinsed rice and stir once to distribute evenly. Nestle the whole scotch bonnet pepper on top — do not pierce or cut it. This is how you get that subtle heat and fruity flavor without turning this into a fire drill. Add salt now.
  7. 7Bring back to a boil, then reduce heat to the lowest setting, cover tightly, and cook for 18 to 20 minutes. No peeking — let that steam do its work.
  8. 8After 20 minutes, check the rice. If there's still liquid, cover and cook 3 to 5 more minutes. Once the rice is cooked through, remove the scotch bonnet pepper and thyme sprigs, add the butter if using, and fluff gently with a fork.
  9. 9Let it rest covered for 5 minutes off the heat before serving. That rest time matters — it finishes the texture and makes every grain separate perfectly. Serve as a main or alongside fried plantains, griot, or stewed chicken.
💡 Improv Tip

The whole scotch bonnet trick is non-negotiable — it perfumes the whole pot with this beautiful floral heat without making it spicy. But if your pepper has any cracks or soft spots, swap it for a fresh one or skip it entirely, because a busted scotch bonnet in a pot of rice is a different kind of adventure nobody asked for.
