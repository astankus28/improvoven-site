Simple Pecan Pie Recipe for Thanksgiving (Classic, Gooey, Never Fails)

Published November 21, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple pecan pie recipe for Thanksgiving is the one you'll make every year — rich, buttery filling, perfectly toasted pecans, and that beautiful glossy top that makes everyone think you've been baking for decades. No candy thermometer, no fancy technique, just pantry staples and about 15 minutes of hands-on work. It's the kind of pie that earns you serious table cred without stressing you out before the big meal.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
55 mins
Total
1 hr 10 mins
Serves
8

Ingredients

  • 1 unbaked 9-inch pie crust (store-bought is totally fine)
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 cup light corn syrup
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon cinnamon (optional but recommended)
  • 1 1/2 cups pecan halves

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F. If you're using a refrigerated store-bought crust, let it sit at room temperature for about 10 minutes so it doesn't crack when you unroll it. Fit it into your 9-inch pie dish, crimp the edges however you like, and set it aside — no need to pre-bake.
  2. 2In a large bowl, whisk together the eggs until they're fully combined and slightly frothy, about 30 seconds. This step matters — it keeps the filling smooth and prevents any streaky egg bits in your final pie.
  3. 3Add the corn syrup, granulated sugar, brown sugar, melted butter, vanilla extract, salt, and cinnamon (if using) to the eggs. Whisk everything together until the mixture is completely smooth and uniform, about 1 minute. It should look glossy and smell incredible.
  4. 4Scatter 1 cup of the pecan halves evenly across the bottom of your unbaked pie crust. Pour the filling slowly over the pecans — they'll start floating up, which is exactly what you want. Arrange the remaining 1/2 cup of pecan halves on top in whatever pattern makes you happy. A simple concentric circle looks stunning and takes 30 seconds.
  5. 5Place the pie on a baking sheet (this catches any drips and makes it easier to move in and out of the oven) and bake at 350°F for 50 to 60 minutes. The pie is done when the edges are set and puffed but the very center still has a slight jiggle — like Jell-O, not liquid. It will firm up as it cools.
  6. 6Here's the hardest part: let the pie cool on a wire rack for at least 2 hours before slicing. I know. But cutting into a hot pecan pie gives you a runny, messy slice. Cool it completely and you get those perfect, clean cuts with that gorgeous gooey-but-set filling. If you made it the day before Thanksgiving, congratulations — you're winning.
  7. 7Serve at room temperature or slightly warm with a big scoop of vanilla ice cream or a cloud of whipped cream. Store leftovers loosely covered at room temperature for up to 2 days, or refrigerate for up to 5 days.
💡 Improv Tip

If your pie crust edges are browning too fast before the filling sets, grab a sheet of aluminum foil, tear out the center so it's just a ring, and lay it over the edges for the last 20 minutes of baking. No pie shield? No problem — that foil trick has been saving Thanksgiving pies for generations and it costs literally nothing.
