Easy Argentine Humita Recipe (Creamy Corn Dumplings in the Husk)

Published June 3, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy humita recipe Argentine style is pure comfort food — think sweet corn masa steamed in its own husk until it's soft, creamy, and slightly sweet with a hint of spice. It's the kind of dish that makes your whole kitchen smell like abuela's house, and the best part? You probably have most of what you need already. No fancy tools, no stress — just real, soulful food.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
45 mins
Total
65 mins
Serves
8 humitas

Ingredients

  • 6 ears of fresh corn (husks and silk reserved)
  • 1/2 cup whole milk
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 1/2 medium white onion, finely diced
  • 1/2 red bell pepper, finely diced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon paprika or smoked paprika
  • Optional: 1/2 cup shredded mozzarella or white cheese

Instructions

  1. 1Carefully peel back the husks from each ear of corn without tearing them — you want those husks intact because they are your cooking vessel. Set the husks aside flat. Remove the silk and set it aside too (you'll use it to tie the humitas later).
  2. 2Cut the kernels off all 6 cobs directly into a blender or food processor. Add the milk and blend until you get a thick, slightly chunky paste — not completely smooth. You want some texture in there, that's what makes it feel homemade.
  3. 3In a medium skillet over medium heat, melt the butter. Add the diced onion and bell pepper and cook for about 5 minutes, stirring, until softened and fragrant. Season with salt, sugar, black pepper, and paprika.
  4. 4Pour the corn paste into the skillet with the softened vegetables. Stir everything together and cook over medium-low heat for about 8 minutes, stirring constantly, until the mixture thickens up and pulls away from the sides of the pan. It should look like a thick, glossy corn porridge. If you're adding cheese, stir it in now and let it melt right in.
  5. 5Now it's assembly time. Lay two or three corn husks flat, overlapping them slightly to make a little pocket. Spoon about 3 to 4 tablespoons of the corn filling into the center. Fold the sides of the husks over the filling, then fold the bottom end up. Tie each humita closed with a thin strip of corn silk or a piece of kitchen twine — don't overthink it, just get it snug.
  6. 6Place the tied humitas upright or on their side in a large pot fitted with a steamer basket. Add about 2 inches of water to the bottom of the pot, cover tightly with a lid, and steam over medium heat for 35 to 40 minutes. Check the water level halfway through and add more if needed.
  7. 7Pull them out and let them rest for 5 minutes before serving — they firm up beautifully as they cool just slightly. Unwrap at the table for the full experience and serve as-is or with a simple salsa criolla on the side.
💡 Improv Tip

If you can't find fresh corn with intact husks, grab dried corn husks from the Latin aisle at the grocery store — same ones used for tamales. Just soak them in warm water for 20 minutes to soften before using. Also, the filling mixture can be made a day ahead and refrigerated, which makes assembly super quick when you're ready to cook.
