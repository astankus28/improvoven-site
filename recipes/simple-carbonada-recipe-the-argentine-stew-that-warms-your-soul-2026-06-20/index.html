Simple Carbonada Recipe: The Argentine Stew That Warms Your Soul

Published June 20, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple carbonada recipe brings the cozy magic of Argentine stew right into your kitchen — no fancy butcher shop or specialty store required. It's a hearty, sweet-and-savory one-pot situation with beef, potatoes, corn, and a little dried fruit that makes the whole thing sing. Think of it like your abuela's locrio had a South American cousin — pure comfort in a bowl.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
45 mins
Total
1 hour
Serves
4-6

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs beef chuck or stew beef, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped (or one 14 oz can diced tomatoes)
  • 3 medium potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 2 ears of corn, cut into rounds (or 1 cup frozen corn)
  • 1 medium sweet potato, peeled and cubed
  • 1/3 cup dried peaches or dried apricots, roughly chopped
  • 3 cups beef broth or water with 1 bouillon cube
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon sweet paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Fresh parsley for garnish

Instructions

  1. 1Pat your beef cubes dry with a paper towel — this is your secret to getting a good sear. Season generously with salt, pepper, cumin, and paprika. Heat the vegetable oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium-high heat until it shimmers.
  2. 2Working in batches so you don't crowd the pot, sear the beef cubes for about 2-3 minutes per side until they get a nice deep brown crust. Don't rush this — that browning is pure flavor. Remove the browned beef and set it aside on a plate.
  3. 3In the same pot over medium heat, add your diced onion and green bell pepper. Scrape up those brown bits from the bottom of the pot as the vegetables soften — that's liquid gold right there. Cook for about 4-5 minutes until the onion turns translucent.
  4. 4Add the minced garlic and cook for another minute until fragrant. Then stir in the chopped tomatoes (or canned diced tomatoes) and oregano. Let that cook down for about 3 minutes until it starts to look like a loose sauce.
  5. 5Return the seared beef to the pot and pour in the beef broth. Stir everything together, bring it to a boil, then reduce the heat to low. Cover and let it simmer for 20 minutes so the beef starts getting tender and the flavors get to know each other.
  6. 6Add the cubed potatoes, sweet potato, corn rounds, and the dried peaches or apricots. Stir gently to combine. The dried fruit might feel a little wild to you, but trust — it's what makes this dish authentically Argentine and absolutely delicious. Cover and simmer for another 20-25 minutes until the potatoes are fork-tender and the beef is completely soft.
  7. 7Taste and adjust your salt and pepper. The stew should have a beautiful balance of savory, slightly sweet, and earthy. If it looks too thick, splash in a little more broth or water. If it's too thin, let it simmer uncovered for a few more minutes.
  8. 8Ladle into bowls, hit it with fresh chopped parsley, and serve with crusty bread or white rice. This is the kind of meal that makes people ask you for the recipe before they're even done eating.
💡 Improv Tip

Don't skip the dried fruit — I know it sounds unexpected but that's literally what makes carbonada CARBONADA. Dried peaches are traditional but dried apricots from the dollar store work perfectly and cost almost nothing. They plump up in the broth and give the whole stew this subtle sweetness that balances the savory beef. Once you try it you'll wonder why we don't put fruit in every stew.
