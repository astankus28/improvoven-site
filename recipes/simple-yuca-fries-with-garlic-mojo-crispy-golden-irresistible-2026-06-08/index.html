Simple Yuca Fries with Garlic Mojo (Crispy, Golden, Irresistible)

Published June 8, 2026 · Improv Oven

Simple yuca fries with garlic mojo are the kind of thing that make people forget french fries even exist — crispy outside, fluffy inside, and dunked in a bright, garlicky citrus sauce that hits every single time. Yuca is cheap, filling, and sitting in most Latin grocery stores for almost nothing, so this is one of those wins that feels fancy but costs almost nothing. Make these once and they're in your regular rotation forever.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs fresh or frozen yuca, peeled and cut into thick fry-shaped pieces
  • Water for boiling
  • 1 tbsp salt (for boiling water)
  • 3 to 4 cups neutral oil for frying (vegetable or canola)
  • Salt to taste after frying
  • 6 cloves garlic, minced or pressed
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • 3 tbsp fresh lime juice (about 2 limes)
  • 2 tbsp fresh orange juice
  • 1/2 tsp cumin
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro or parsley, roughly chopped
  • Pinch of red pepper flakes (optional but recommended)

Instructions

  1. 1If using fresh yuca, peel it by cutting off the ends, slicing down the sides to remove the thick brown skin and the pink layer underneath, then cut into 3-inch logs and halve or quarter them lengthwise into fry shapes. If using frozen yuca, it usually comes pre-cut — just thaw it out.
  2. 2Place your yuca pieces in a large pot, cover with cold water, add 1 tablespoon of salt, and bring to a boil. Cook for 15 to 20 minutes until the yuca is fork-tender but not falling apart. You want it cooked through but still holding its shape. Drain and let it steam dry on a rack or paper towels for at least 5 minutes — this step is everything for getting them crispy.
  3. 3While the yuca dries, make your garlic mojo. In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, warm the olive oil for about a minute, then add the minced garlic. Let it cook gently for 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant and just barely golden — do not let it burn or the whole thing turns bitter. Pull it off the heat and immediately stir in the lime juice, orange juice, cumin, and salt. It will sizzle dramatically and that is completely normal and very satisfying. Stir in the cilantro or parsley and set aside.
  4. 4Heat your frying oil in a deep skillet or heavy pot over medium-high heat until it reaches around 350°F. If you don't have a thermometer, drop a small piece of yuca in — if it sizzles right away and floats up, you're good to go.
  5. 5Fry the yuca in batches, don't crowd the pan. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes per side until deep golden and crispy on the outside. Use tongs or a slotted spoon to transfer them to a paper towel-lined plate and hit them with salt immediately while they're hot.
  6. 6Pile the fries on a platter and either drizzle the garlic mojo right over the top or serve it on the side for dipping. Either way, somebody at the table is going to ask you for the recipe before they even finish chewing.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to truly crispy yuca fries is letting them steam dry after boiling — if they go into the oil wet, they'll steam instead of fry and you'll end up with something sad and soggy. Give them at least 5 minutes on a rack, or even pat them dry with a paper towel if you're in a hurry. Also, that mojo is good on basically everything, so make extra and thank yourself later.
