Simple One Pan Salmon with Garlic Butter and Herbs

Published May 12, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple one pan salmon recipe is the kind of weeknight dinner that makes you look like you actually have your life together — ready in under 30 minutes with almost no cleanup. We're talking golden-seared salmon in a garlicky, herby butter sauce that tastes like it came from a nice restaurant but costs maybe $12 for the whole family. Classic Improv Oven energy — big flavor, minimal effort, nothing fancy required.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
18 mins
Total
28 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 salmon fillets (about 6 oz each, skin-on if possible)
  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional but recommended)
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 2 tablespoons fresh parsley, chopped (or 1 tsp dried in a pinch)
  • Lemon slices for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Pull your salmon out of the fridge about 10 minutes before cooking — room temperature fish sears way better than cold fish straight from the fridge. Pat the fillets completely dry with paper towels. This is non-negotiable if you want that beautiful golden crust instead of sad steamed salmon.
  2. 2Mix together the smoked paprika, oregano, cumin, salt, black pepper, and red pepper flakes in a small bowl. Sprinkle that seasoning blend evenly over the top of each salmon fillet and press it in lightly with your fingers.
  3. 3Heat a large skillet — cast iron is the move if you have it, but any heavy pan works — over medium-high heat. Add the olive oil and let it get hot but not smoking, about 1 minute.
  4. 4Place the salmon fillets in the pan skin-side up if skinless, or skin-side down if skin-on. Don't touch them. Let them sear undisturbed for 4 to 5 minutes until a golden crust forms on the bottom. You'll see the color start changing up the sides of the fillet — that's your cue it's almost time to flip.
  5. 5Flip the salmon carefully and immediately reduce the heat to medium. Cook for another 3 to 4 minutes depending on thickness. You're looking for the salmon to be just opaque in the thickest part — a little translucent in the very center is perfectly fine and actually ideal.
  6. 6Push the salmon to the sides of the pan and add the butter right in the middle. Once it melts, add the minced garlic and let it cook for about 60 seconds, stirring gently so it doesn't burn. The whole kitchen is going to smell incredible right now.
  7. 7Squeeze the lemon juice directly into the pan and tilt it slightly to swirl everything together into a quick pan sauce. Spoon that garlic butter over the salmon fillets a few times to coat them. This is called basting and it is absolutely the move.
  8. 8Sprinkle with fresh parsley, pull it off the heat, and serve immediately with lemon slices on the side. This goes beautifully over white rice, with roasted veggies, or honestly just with some crusty bread to soak up that sauce.
💡 Improv Tip

The number one mistake people make with salmon is overcooking it — and I get it, nobody wants raw fish. But salmon keeps cooking for a minute or two after you pull it off the heat, so take it off the pan when it still looks just slightly underdone in the center. It'll finish on the plate and stay buttery and flaky instead of turning into dry, chalky fish that nobody's excited about.
