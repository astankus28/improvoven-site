Budget Argentine Alfajores Cookies Recipe (With Pantry Staples!)

Published June 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This budget Argentine alfajores cookies recipe skips the fancy imports and uses what you probably already have sitting in your pantry right now. We're talking melt-in-your-mouth shortbread sandwiched with dulce de leche — the kind of cookie that makes people think you trained in Buenos Aires. Once you make these, you'll understand why Argentines are basically obsessed with them.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
12 mins
Total
32 mins plus 30 mins chill time
Serves
24 cookies (12 sandwiches)

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup cornstarch
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened (1 stick)
  • 1/3 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 tablespoon rum or orange juice (rum is the move, just saying)
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 1 can (13.4 oz) store-bought dulce de leche — or see tip below
  • 1/2 cup shredded sweetened coconut, for rolling the edges

Instructions

  1. 1In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, cornstarch, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. Set aside. The cornstarch is your secret weapon here — it's what gives alfajores that legendary tender, almost crumbly texture that falls apart in the best way.
  2. 2In a large bowl, beat the softened butter and powdered sugar together until pale and fluffy, about 2-3 minutes. You can use a hand mixer or just go in with a sturdy wooden spoon and some elbow grease — this is a low-equipment kitchen, always.
  3. 3Add the egg yolks one at a time, mixing well after each. Then add the vanilla, rum (or orange juice), and lemon zest. Give it a good stir until everything is combined and smells amazing.
  4. 4Gradually add the flour mixture to the butter mixture, stirring gently until a soft dough forms. Do not overmix — the moment it comes together, stop. Overworking the dough means tough cookies, and we are not about that life.
  5. 5Shape the dough into a disk, wrap it in plastic wrap or a reusable bag, and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes. This rest time is non-negotiable — it keeps the cookies from spreading and makes the dough easier to handle.
  6. 6Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Line two baking sheets with parchment paper.
  7. 7On a lightly floured surface, roll the dough out to about 1/4-inch thickness. Use a round cookie cutter or the rim of a glass to cut circles about 2 inches in diameter. Re-roll the scraps and cut more — don't waste anything.
  8. 8Place the rounds on your prepared baking sheets, spacing them about an inch apart. Bake for 10-12 minutes. Here's the key: you want them just barely golden on the bottom with no color on top. They should look almost underdone when you pull them — that's correct. They firm up as they cool.
  9. 9Let the cookies cool completely on the pan before you touch them. I know it's hard. Be strong. Moving them warm means they'll crack.
  10. 10Once fully cooled, flip half the cookies upside down. Spoon about a teaspoon of dulce de leche onto the flat side and spread it almost to the edge. Top with another cookie, flat side down, and press gently so the dulce de leche just peeks out from the sides.
  11. 11Pour the shredded coconut into a shallow bowl. Roll the exposed dulce de leche edges through the coconut so it sticks all around the sandwich. This is the classic finish and it makes them look like you bought them from a panadería in Palermo. Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 4 days — if they last that long.
💡 Improv Tip

No store-bought dulce de leche? No problem — this is Improv Oven, after all. Take an unopened can of sweetened condensed milk, remove the label, place it in a deep pot, cover completely with water by at least 2 inches, and simmer for 2.5 to 3 hours, adding water as needed to keep the can submerged. Let it cool completely before opening. Boom — homemade dulce de leche for about $1.50 a can. Just never let that pot run dry or you'll have a very dramatic evening.
