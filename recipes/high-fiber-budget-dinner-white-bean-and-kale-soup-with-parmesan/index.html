High Fiber Budget Dinner: White Bean and Kale Soup with Parmesan

Published July 14, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you're looking for an increase fiber budget recipe that actually tastes like something you'd choose to eat, this white bean and kale soup is your answer. It's built from pantry staples — canned beans, dried pasta, a bunch of kale, and a parmesan rind if you've got one lurking in the cheese drawer — and it comes together in one pot in about 35 minutes. Tuscan in spirit, completely weeknight in effort.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
40 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 stalks celery, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon dried rosemary (or thyme)
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  • 2 cans (15 oz each) cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes
  • 4 cups low-sodium chicken or vegetable broth
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 parmesan rind (optional but highly recommended)
  • 1 large bunch curly kale, stems removed, leaves roughly torn (about 4 cups packed)
  • 1/2 cup small pasta (ditalini, small elbow, or broken spaghetti)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Grated parmesan, for serving
  • Crusty bread or toast, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the onion and celery with a pinch of salt and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and translucent, about 6 to 8 minutes.
  2. 2Add the garlic, rosemary, and red pepper flakes. Stir and cook for another minute until fragrant — your kitchen should smell amazing right now.
  3. 3Add the drained cannellini beans. Here's a trick: scoop out about one cup of the beans and mash them roughly with a fork or the back of a spoon, then stir them back in. This thickens the broth naturally and makes the soup feel hearty and creamy without any cream.
  4. 4Pour in the diced tomatoes (with their juices), broth, and water. Drop in the parmesan rind if you have one — it melts into the background and adds a savory depth that's hard to explain but impossible to miss. Bring everything to a gentle boil.
  5. 5Add the pasta and stir. Cook uncovered for about 8 to 10 minutes, until the pasta is just shy of al dente — it will keep cooking off the heat.
  6. 6Stir in the kale in big handfuls. It looks like way too much but it wilts down significantly. Cook for 4 to 5 more minutes until the kale is tender but still has a little bite. Fish out and discard the parmesan rind.
  7. 7Taste and adjust salt and pepper. Ladle into bowls, finish with a generous shower of grated parmesan, a drizzle of olive oil if you're feeling fancy, and serve with crusty bread to mop up every last bit.
💡 Improv Tip

Don't skip mashing some of the beans — that one step is what separates a watery bean soup from something that feels like a real meal. And if you end up with leftovers, the pasta will absorb a lot of the broth overnight. Just add a splash of water or broth when reheating and it comes right back to life.
