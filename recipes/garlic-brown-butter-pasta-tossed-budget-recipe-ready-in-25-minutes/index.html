Garlic Brown Butter Pasta Tossed Budget Recipe (Ready in 25 Minutes)

Published August 22, 2026 · Improv Oven

This pasta tossed budget recipe is proof that the most satisfying dinners don't need a long ingredient list — just good technique and a hot pan. Brown butter, garlic, Parmesan, and a splash of pasta water transform a box of spaghetti into something that tastes genuinely restaurant-worthy. It's the kind of meal that makes you feel like a genius for spending about four dollars on dinner.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 12 oz spaghetti or linguine
  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 5 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional but recommended)
  • 1/2 cup finely grated Parmesan, plus more for serving
  • 1/2 cup reserved pasta water
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Small handful fresh parsley, chopped (or 1 teaspoon dried parsley)

Instructions

  1. 1Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil — it should taste like mild seawater. This is the only seasoning the pasta itself gets, so don't skip it.
  2. 2Cook your spaghetti or linguine according to package directions until just al dente, about 1 minute less than the box says. Before you drain it, scoop out at least 1 cup of pasta water with a mug and set it aside. Drain the pasta and set it aside.
  3. 3In a large skillet or wide pan, melt the butter over medium heat. Keep watching it — you want it to foam, then settle, then turn a toasty golden-brown with a nutty smell. This takes about 3 to 4 minutes. Don't walk away; it goes from perfect to burnt fast.
  4. 4Once the butter is golden brown, reduce the heat to medium-low and add the olive oil (this helps keep the garlic from burning). Add the sliced garlic and red pepper flakes. Stir and cook for about 60 to 90 seconds until the garlic is fragrant and just turning pale gold at the edges.
  5. 5Add the drained pasta directly into the pan. Pour in 1/4 cup of the reserved pasta water and toss everything together with tongs. The starchy water will start to create a light, glossy sauce that coats every strand.
  6. 6Remove the pan from heat and add the grated Parmesan a small handful at a time, tossing constantly. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if the sauce looks tight or dry — you're going for silky, not soupy.
  7. 7Taste and adjust with salt and black pepper. Plate it up, shower with extra Parmesan and the chopped parsley, and eat immediately. This one does not wait.
💡 Improv Tip

The pasta water is not optional — it's basically free sauce. That starchy, salty liquid is what turns butter and cheese from a greasy mess into a real emulsified sauce that actually clings to the noodles. Scoop it out before you even think about draining the pot.
