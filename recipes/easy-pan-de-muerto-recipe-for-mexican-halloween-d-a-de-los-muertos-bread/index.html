Easy Pan de Muerto Recipe for Mexican Halloween (Día de los Muertos Bread)

Published October 17, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy pan de muerto recipe is the one to make when Día de los Muertos rolls around and you want something that feels genuinely special without spending a fortune or stressing out your kitchen. Pan de muerto is a soft, lightly sweet Mexican bread flavored with orange zest and anise, topped with a buttery sugar crust — and yes, it's absolutely achievable at home. Think of it as a slightly enriched dough that's more forgiving than it looks, and way more impressive than anything you'd grab at a bakery.

Prep
30 mins
Cook
30 mins
Total
3 hrs (including rise time)
Serves
1 loaf (8 slices)

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour, plus extra for kneading
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar, plus more for topping
  • 1 packet (2 1/4 tsp) active dry yeast
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp anise seeds (or 1/4 tsp anise extract)
  • 1/2 cup whole milk
  • 1/4 cup unsalted butter, cut into small pieces
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • Zest of 1 large orange
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter, melted (for topping)
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar (for topping)

Instructions

  1. 1Warm your milk in a small saucepan or microwave until it's just warm to the touch — around 110°F. You don't want it hot enough to kill the yeast, just cozy. Sprinkle in your yeast and a pinch of sugar, stir gently, and let it sit for 5–10 minutes until it looks foamy and alive. If nothing happens, your yeast is old — grab a fresh packet.
  2. 2In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, salt, and anise seeds. Make a well in the center and pour in your foamy yeast mixture, eggs, vanilla, and orange zest. Stir until a shaggy dough forms.
  3. 3Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and knead it for about 8–10 minutes, gradually working in the butter pieces a few at a time as you go. This is the most important step — don't rush it. The dough will feel sticky and weird at first, but keep kneading and it'll come together into something smooth, soft, and slightly tacky. If it's sticking to everything, add flour one tablespoon at a time.
  4. 4Shape the dough into a ball and place it in a lightly greased bowl. Cover with a clean kitchen towel or plastic wrap and let it rise in a warm spot for about 1.5 to 2 hours, until doubled in size. A turned-off oven with just the light on works perfectly.
  5. 5Once risen, punch the dough down gently and pull off two small pieces — roughly golf-ball sized each. Set those aside. Shape the remaining large piece into a round ball and place it on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Roll the two small pieces into logs for the bone shapes and one small ball for the top. Arrange the two logs in a cross pattern over the top of the big round loaf, and press the small ball into the center where they cross. That's the skull. Press gently so everything sticks.
  6. 6Cover the shaped loaf loosely and let it rise again for 45 minutes to 1 hour, until puffed and pillowy.
  7. 7Preheat your oven to 350°F. Bake the pan de muerto for 28–32 minutes, until the top is golden brown and it sounds hollow when you tap the bottom. If the top is browning too fast, tent it loosely with foil for the last 10 minutes.
  8. 8As soon as the bread comes out of the oven, brush the entire surface generously with the melted butter — don't be shy. Then immediately sprinkle an even, thick layer of granulated sugar all over the top and sides. The butter makes it stick and creates that iconic slightly crunchy, sweet crust. Let it cool for at least 20 minutes before slicing.
💡 Improv Tip

Room temperature eggs matter more than you think — cold eggs can make the dough seize up and slow everything down. Set them out on the counter when you warm your milk and you'll be good to go. Also, don't skip the double rise. Pan de muerto gets its soft, pillowy texture from time, not shortcuts. If you want to prep ahead, let it do its second rise in the fridge overnight and bake it fresh the next morning.
