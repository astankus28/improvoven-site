Easy Cuban Picadillo Ground Beef Recipe (Ready in 30 Minutes)

Published November 12, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy Cuban picadillo ground beef recipe is the kind of dish that makes you feel like you've got a secret — a few pantry staples, one pan, and about 30 minutes standing between you and something truly special. Picadillo is a classic Cuban comfort food: seasoned ground beef simmered with tomatoes, olives, and raisins in a savory-sweet sauce that hits every single note. Serve it over white rice, stuff it into peppers, or eat it straight from the pan — no judgment here.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground beef (80/20 works great here)
  • 1 small yellow onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1/3 cup pimiento-stuffed green olives, sliced (plus a splash of the brine)
  • 1/4 cup raisins
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tablespoon white wine vinegar or red wine vinegar
  • Cooked white rice, for serving
  • Fresh cilantro or parsley, for garnish (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a large skillet or wide saucepan over medium-high heat. Add the diced onion and green bell pepper and cook for about 4–5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and just starting to pick up a little color at the edges.
  2. 2Add the minced garlic and cook for another 30 seconds until fragrant — just enough for it to bloom without burning. This is your sofrito base, and it's where all the flavor starts.
  3. 3Add the ground beef to the pan. Break it up with a wooden spoon and cook, stirring occasionally, until it's fully browned and no pink remains, about 6–7 minutes. If there's a lot of excess fat in the pan, carefully tilt the skillet and spoon most of it out — you want some for flavor, not a swimming pool.
  4. 4Stir in the tomato paste and let it cook for about 1 minute, stirring it into the beef so it darkens slightly and loses that raw tomato-paste edge. This step is small but it makes a big difference.
  5. 5Pour in the diced tomatoes (with all their juices), then add the cumin, oregano, smoked paprika, salt, pepper, and bay leaves. Stir everything together and bring to a gentle simmer.
  6. 6Add the sliced olives, a small splash of olive brine (about 1 tablespoon), the raisins, and the vinegar. Stir to combine. Reduce the heat to medium-low and let the picadillo simmer uncovered for 10–12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and everything melds together beautifully.
  7. 7Taste and adjust salt as needed. Fish out the bay leaves. Serve hot over white rice and scatter fresh cilantro or parsley on top if you've got it.
💡 Improv Tip

Don't skip the raisins — I know it sounds like a weird move, but they're not there to make it sweet, they're there to balance the brine from the olives and the acidity from the tomatoes. That little back-and-forth between sweet, salty, and tangy is exactly what makes picadillo taste so layered and interesting. If you're out of raisins, a small drizzle of honey works in a pinch.
