Easy Bulgur Pilaf with Vegetables

Published June 10, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy bulgur pilaf with vegetables is one of those dishes that makes you feel like a genius for using what you already have in the pantry. Bulgur cooks up fluffy and nutty in about 15 minutes, and whatever vegetables are hanging out in your fridge? They belong here. It's the kind of meal that's humble, hearty, and honestly way more satisfying than it has any right to be.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
30 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup coarse bulgur wheat
  • 2 cups vegetable broth (or water with a bouillon cube)
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 medium carrot, diced
  • 1 bell pepper, any color, diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 cup frozen peas or corn (or both)
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Juice of half a lemon
  • Fresh parsley or cilantro for garnish (optional but encouraged)

Instructions

  1. 1Heat the olive oil in a medium saucepan or deep skillet over medium heat. Add the diced onion and carrot and cook for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion is soft and just starting to turn golden.
  2. 2Add the bell pepper and garlic and cook for another 2 minutes until the garlic smells amazing and the pepper softens slightly.
  3. 3Stir in the cumin, smoked paprika, and turmeric, coating all the vegetables in the spices. Let them toast for about 30 seconds — this is where the flavor really wakes up.
  4. 4Add the dry bulgur to the pan and stir it around so it gets coated in all that spiced oil. Toast it for about 1 minute, stirring constantly.
  5. 5Pour in the vegetable broth and stir to combine. Bring everything to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover the pan, and let it cook for 12 to 15 minutes until the bulgur has absorbed all the liquid.
  6. 6Uncover and stir in the frozen peas or corn. The residual heat will warm them through in about 2 minutes — no extra cooking needed.
  7. 7Squeeze the lemon juice over the top, taste for salt and pepper, and fluff everything gently with a fork. Top with fresh parsley or cilantro and serve warm.
💡 Improv Tip

Bulgur is incredibly forgiving but the one thing that makes or breaks it is using broth instead of plain water — even just a bouillon cube dissolved in hot water gives it that deep savory backbone. And don't skip the toasting step before adding the liquid. That one minute of toasting the dry bulgur in the spiced oil is the difference between a flat pilaf and one that tastes like you actually know what you're doing.
