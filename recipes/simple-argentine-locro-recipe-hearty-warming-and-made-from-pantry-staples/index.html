Simple Argentine Locro Recipe (Hearty, Warming, and Made From Pantry Staples)

Published September 2, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple Argentine locro recipe is the kind of dish that wraps you up like a blanket — a thick, smoky stew of white beans, squash, and whatever cured meat you've got hanging around. Locro is Argentina's national winter stew, deeply comforting and built entirely from humble ingredients that stretch far and feed a crowd. Once you taste how rich and satisfying a pot of locro gets with just dried beans, smoked sausage, and a little patience, you'll understand why Argentines have been making this for centuries.

Prep
20 mins
Cook
2 hrs
Total
2 hrs 20 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1 lb dried white beans (navy or Great Northern), soaked overnight or quick-soaked
  • 1 lb butternut squash or calabaza, peeled and cubed (about 2 cups)
  • 3/4 lb smoked kielbasa or smoked sausage, sliced into coins
  • 4 oz thick-cut bacon or salt pork, roughly chopped
  • 1 medium white onion, diced
  • 1 medium green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 teaspoons sweet paprika
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon chili flakes (optional, for heat)
  • 6 cups water or low-sodium chicken broth
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Fresh scallions or parsley for garnish

Instructions

  1. 1If you didn't soak your beans overnight, do a quick soak: cover the dried beans in cold water in a pot, bring to a boil, boil for 2 minutes, then remove from heat and let them sit for 1 hour. Drain and rinse. This step is non-negotiable — it cuts down cooking time and helps the beans cook evenly.
  2. 2Heat the vegetable oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the chopped bacon or salt pork and cook for about 4 minutes until some fat renders out and it starts to get a little color. Don't rush this — that rendered fat is flavor.
  3. 3Add the sliced smoked sausage to the pot and let it brown lightly on each side, about 3 minutes. The sausage will release even more smoky flavor into the oil. Remove the sausage and bacon from the pot and set aside — you'll add them back later.
  4. 4In the same pot with all that beautiful rendered fat, add the diced onion and green bell pepper. Cook over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until the onion is soft and translucent, about 5 to 6 minutes.
  5. 5Add the minced garlic, sweet paprika, smoked paprika, cumin, oregano, and chili flakes if using. Stir constantly for about 60 seconds until the spices bloom and smell amazing. This is the sofrito base that gives the whole stew its depth.
  6. 6Add the drained soaked beans, cubed squash, the browned sausage and bacon back into the pot, and pour in the water or broth. Stir everything together and bring to a boil over high heat.
  7. 7Once boiling, reduce heat to low, partially cover the pot, and let it simmer for 90 minutes to 2 hours, stirring occasionally. The beans should become completely tender and the squash will break down and naturally thicken the stew. That's the magic of locro — the squash becomes the body.
  8. 8After 90 minutes, check the consistency. Locro should be thick and almost porridge-like, not soupy. If it's still too thin, mash some of the beans and squash against the side of the pot with a wooden spoon and keep simmering uncovered for another 15 minutes. If it's too thick, add a splash of warm water.
  9. 9Taste and adjust salt and black pepper. Locro can take more salt than you'd expect because the beans absorb a lot. Season boldly.
  10. 10Serve hot in deep bowls, garnished with sliced scallions or fresh parsley. Crusty bread on the side is absolutely not optional.
💡 Improv Tip

The overnight bean soak is worth it — but if you forgot, the quick-soak method in Step 1 genuinely works. The real secret to great locro is not rushing the simmer. That long, slow cook is what lets the squash fully break down and turn the broth silky and thick. If your locro looks too brothy at the 90-minute mark, just uncover it and give it another 20 minutes — it'll come together. Leftovers thicken up even more overnight in the fridge, so add a little water when reheating and you've got an even better bowl the next day.
