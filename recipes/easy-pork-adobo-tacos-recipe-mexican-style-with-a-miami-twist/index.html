Easy Pork Adobo Tacos Recipe (Mexican-Style with a Miami Twist)

Published September 25, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy pork adobo tacos recipe brings all the bold, smoky, garlicky goodness of Mexican adobo marinade together with juicy pan-seared pork — and yes, it's a legit lunch you can pull off on a Tuesday. We're talking tender pork shoulder or pork chops (whatever's in your fridge), rubbed down in a pantry-built adobo, seared until caramelized, and tucked into warm tortillas with bright, punchy toppings. Miami vibes meet Mexican street food and it is a beautiful thing.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1.5 lbs boneless pork shoulder or pork chops, cut into thin strips or small chunks
  • 3 tablespoons white or apple cider vinegar
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 8 small corn or flour tortillas
  • 1/2 white onion, finely diced
  • 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • 2 limes, cut into wedges
  • 1 avocado, sliced or roughly mashed
  • 1/4 cup sour cream or Mexican crema (optional but highly recommended)
  • Hot sauce of your choice, to serve

Instructions

  1. 1Make your adobo marinade: In a bowl, combine the vinegar, minced garlic, oregano, cumin, smoked paprika, chili powder, black pepper, and salt. Stir it all together — it should smell incredible already. Add your pork strips or chunks and toss well to coat every piece. Let it sit for at least 10 minutes on the counter, or up to overnight in the fridge if you're planning ahead. Even 10 minutes makes a real difference.
  2. 2While the pork marinates, prep your toppings. Dice your onion, tear up that cilantro, slice the avocado, and get your lime wedges and hot sauce ready to go. Set everything out on the counter — taco assembly should feel fun, not frantic.
  3. 3Heat your oil in a large skillet or cast iron pan over medium-high heat. When the oil shimmers and just starts to smoke a tiny bit, add the pork in a single layer — don't crowd it or it'll steam instead of sear. Cook for about 4 to 5 minutes without touching it so it gets that gorgeous golden-brown crust. Then flip and cook another 3 to 4 minutes until cooked through and caramelized on the edges. Work in batches if needed.
  4. 4Once the pork is seared and done, let it rest in the pan for a minute off the heat. The residual juices and those browned bits in the pan? That's flavor gold. You can deglaze with a tiny splash of water or lime juice and toss the pork back in to coat if you want even more sauce.
  5. 5Warm your tortillas: Either lay them directly on a gas burner for 20 to 30 seconds per side for that charred edge, or wrap them in a damp paper towel and microwave for 45 seconds. Both work — just don't skip this step, cold tortillas are a vibe killer.
  6. 6Build your tacos: Lay down a few pieces of adobo pork, add a spoonful of avocado, a pinch of diced onion, a good handful of cilantro, a drizzle of crema, and a squeeze of lime. Hit it with hot sauce. Eat immediately. Marvel at yourself.
💡 Improv Tip

Thin is the move here — the thinner you slice your pork before marinating, the faster it cooks and the more surface area you get for that adobo crust to do its thing. Budget pork shoulder is your best friend for this recipe; it's flavorful, forgiving, and usually one of the cheapest cuts at the store. If you only have pork chops, those work great too — just pound them slightly to even thickness before slicing.
