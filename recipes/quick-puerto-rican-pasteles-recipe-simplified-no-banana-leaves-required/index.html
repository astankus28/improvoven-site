Quick Puerto Rican Pasteles Recipe (Simplified, No Banana Leaves Required)

Published October 3, 2026 · Improv Oven

This quick Puerto Rican pasteles recipe takes the soul of the real thing — that savory, masa-wrapped pork filling steamed into something magical — and makes it totally doable on a weeknight without a two-day production. We're using green bananas and yuca for the masa, a fast sofrito-spiced picadillo for the filling, and foil instead of banana leaves so you can pull this off any time the craving hits. Pasteles are one of those dishes that feel like a hug, and this version keeps that feeling intact without the marathon.

Prep
35 mins
Cook
45 mins
Total
1 hr 20 mins
Serves
8 pasteles

Ingredients

  • 4 green bananas, peeled and grated
  • 1 cup yuca, peeled and finely grated (fresh or frozen thawed)
  • 3 tablespoons achiote oil or vegetable oil mixed with 1/2 teaspoon paprika
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 lb ground pork (or ground beef as a swap)
  • 1/2 cup sofrito (store-bought or homemade)
  • 1 packet (1.41 oz) Sazón with achiote
  • 1 teaspoon oregano
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1/4 cup pimento-stuffed olives, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon capers (optional but classic)
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • 8 sheets of aluminum foil (about 12x12 inches each)
  • Achiote oil or vegetable oil for spreading the masa

Instructions

  1. 1Make the masa: In a large bowl, combine the grated green bananas and grated yuca. Add the achiote oil, salt, and garlic powder and mix well until the masa is uniform, slightly sticky, and has a warm orange-yellow tint. Taste it — it should be well-seasoned on its own. Set aside.
  2. 2Make the picadillo filling: Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground pork and cook, breaking it up, until no longer pink, about 5 to 6 minutes. Drain any excess fat, leaving about a tablespoon in the pan.
  3. 3Add the sofrito to the pork and cook for 2 minutes until fragrant. Stir in the Sazón packet, oregano, and tomato paste. Pour in the water and stir everything together. Let it simmer for 5 to 7 minutes until the liquid reduces and the filling is thick and saucy but not dry. Stir in the olives and capers, taste for salt and pepper, and remove from heat. Let the filling cool for at least 10 minutes — this makes assembly much easier.
  4. 4Prep your steamer: Fill a large pot with about 3 inches of water and fit it with a steamer basket or rack. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat while you assemble the pasteles.
  5. 5Assemble the pasteles: Lay a sheet of foil flat on your counter. Brush or rub a thin layer of achiote oil in the center. Scoop about 1/3 cup of masa onto the oiled area and spread it into a rough rectangle, about 5x4 inches — not too thin, not too thick. Add a heaping 2 tablespoons of the pork picadillo down the center of the masa.
  6. 6Fold the pasteles: Fold one long side of the foil over so the masa wraps around the filling, then fold the other side over to seal the two edges of masa together. Fold and crimp the short ends tightly like a burrito so no steam escapes. Repeat with remaining foil, masa, and filling.
  7. 7Steam the pasteles: Place the foil packets in the steamer basket in a single layer (or stack slightly). Cover the pot tightly and steam for 40 to 45 minutes, checking the water level halfway through and adding more boiling water if needed.
  8. 8Rest and serve: Carefully remove the pasteles from the steamer — they'll be hot. Let them rest for 5 minutes before unwrapping. Peel back the foil gently, the masa should be firm, slightly shiny, and pull away cleanly. Serve with hot sauce, a side of arroz blanco, or just as they are straight off the foil.
💡 Improv Tip

The greenest bananas you can find are the key to the right masa texture — if they have any yellow at all, the masa gets too sweet and won't firm up properly when steamed. Hit up a Latin grocery store or the international aisle; they're usually cheaper there too. And don't skip the resting time after steaming — opening them too soon is the number one reason pasteles fall apart on you.
