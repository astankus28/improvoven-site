Sweet Corn & Pea Sammy Pudding Cups (Budget Dessert with a Miami Twist)

Published October 20, 2026 · Improv Oven

This sammy peas corn budget recipe takes two humble pantry staples — sweet corn and green peas — and transforms them into something that'll genuinely surprise you: creamy, lightly sweetened pudding cups with a Latin Caribbean soul. Think of it like a cross between a Venezuelan quesillo and a Southern corn pudding, but dessert-forward, dead simple, and made almost entirely from canned and frozen goods you probably already have. Yes, peas in dessert sounds wild — but trust the process, because the texture they add is silky, smooth, and absolutely genius.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
25 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1 cup frozen green peas, thawed
  • 1 cup canned sweet corn, drained (or frozen, thawed)
  • 1 can (14 oz) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1/2 cup whole milk or evaporated milk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • Pinch of nutmeg
  • Optional topping: a drizzle of honey and a pinch of flaky sea salt

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Lightly grease 6 standard muffin cups or 6 small ramekins with butter or nonstick spray. If using ramekins, place them inside a baking dish — you'll add water for a water bath later.
  2. 2Add the thawed peas to a blender or food processor and blend until completely smooth. You want a thick, creamy green purée with no chunks. This is your secret weapon — it adds an almost custard-like silkiness without any detectable 'pea' flavor once sweetened. Scrape down the sides and blend again if needed.
  3. 3Add the drained sweet corn to the blender with the pea purée. Pulse 4 to 5 times — you want the corn partially blended, leaving some small texture. This gives the pudding cups a gentle bite and natural sweetness that's really lovely.
  4. 4Add the sweetened condensed milk, whole milk, eggs, melted butter, vanilla extract, cinnamon, salt, and nutmeg to the blender. Blend everything together on medium speed for about 30 seconds until smooth, pale, and well combined.
  5. 5Pour the batter evenly into your prepared muffin cups or ramekins, filling each about three-quarters full.
  6. 6If using ramekins in a baking dish, pour about an inch of hot water into the baking dish around the ramekins to create a gentle water bath — this keeps the pudding cups creamy and prevents cracking. For muffin tins, skip the water bath and bake directly on the center rack.
  7. 7Bake for 22 to 26 minutes, until the edges are set and the center has just a slight jiggle — like Jell-O, not liquid. The tops should look pale golden and feel barely firm to the touch.
  8. 8Remove from the oven and let cool completely at room temperature for at least 20 minutes. Then refrigerate for at least 1 hour (or overnight) before serving — they firm up beautifully and taste even better cold.
  9. 9To serve, run a thin knife around the edge of each cup and pop them out onto a small plate, or serve straight in the ramekin. Drizzle with honey and a tiny pinch of flaky sea salt if you want to take it to another level. That salty-sweet contrast is *chef's kiss*.
💡 Improv Tip

The pea purée is the whole magic trick here — don't skip the full blend on the peas. If your blender leaves any texture, push the purée through a fine mesh strainer before mixing everything together. Also, these keep covered in the fridge for up to 4 days, which makes them a sneaky little meal-prep dessert win for the whole week.
