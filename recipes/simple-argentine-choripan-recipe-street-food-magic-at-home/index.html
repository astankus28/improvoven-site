Simple Argentine Choripan Recipe (Street Food Magic at Home)

Published July 16, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple Argentine choripan recipe brings one of Buenos Aires' most beloved street foods straight to your kitchen — and all you really need is a good chorizo and some bread. Think of it like a Latin hot dog that hits completely different: smoky, juicy sausage tucked into a crusty roll and loaded with chimichurri. We're giving it a little Miami love with some extra garlic and a touch of heat, but the soul is pure Argentina.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
15 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 4 links fresh chorizo (Argentine-style or Mexican pork chorizo — see tips)
  • 4 crusty sandwich rolls or hoagie rolls, split
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1/2 cup fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 3 tablespoons red wine vinegar
  • 1/4 cup olive oil (for chimichurri)
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • Optional: sliced tomato and chimichurri mayo (mix chimichurri with 2 tablespoons mayo)

Instructions

  1. 1Make your chimichurri first so the flavors have time to come together. In a small bowl, combine the chopped parsley, minced garlic, red pepper flakes, dried oregano, red wine vinegar, 1/4 cup olive oil, salt, and black pepper. Stir well and set aside — even 10 minutes at room temperature makes a big difference in flavor.
  2. 2Score each chorizo link a few times on one side with a sharp knife. This helps them cook evenly and gives you those gorgeous caramelized edges without the casing splitting and losing all the juices.
  3. 3Heat a large cast iron skillet or grill pan over medium heat and add the 2 tablespoons of olive oil. Once shimmering, place the chorizo links scored-side down. Cook for about 5 to 6 minutes per side, turning once, until deeply browned and cooked through. The internal temp should hit 160°F. If they're browning too fast, lower the heat — you want them cooked through, not charred.
  4. 4While the chorizo rests for a couple minutes, place your split rolls cut-side down in the same pan for about 60 to 90 seconds. You want them golden and lightly toasted, picking up all those delicious pan drippings. Do not skip this step — it's the move.
  5. 5Place one chorizo link in each toasted roll. Spoon a generous amount of chimichurri right over the top. Add sliced tomato if you're using it, and if you made the chimichurri mayo, slather that on the cut side of the roll before loading it up. Serve immediately while everything is hot.
💡 Improv Tip

Argentine chorizo and Mexican fresh chorizo are different beasts — Argentine style is a coarser, less spiced fresh pork sausage that you might find at a Latin butcher or specialty market. If you can't find it, Mexican fresh chorizo works great here and is easier to find at most grocery stores. It's a little spicier and more seasoned, but honestly? Totally delicious in a choripan. Just avoid the dry-cured Spanish chorizo for this one — it won't give you that juicy, grilled sausage vibe you're going for.
