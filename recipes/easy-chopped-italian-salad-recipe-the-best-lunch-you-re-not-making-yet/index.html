Easy Chopped Italian Salad Recipe (The Best Lunch You're Not Making Yet)

Published September 7, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy chopped Italian salad recipe is the kind of lunch that makes you feel like you have your life together — sharp provolone, salty salami, crisp lettuce, briny olives, pepperoncini, and a dead-simple red wine vinaigrette, all chopped small and tossed together in one bowl. It comes together in about 15 minutes, it's wildly satisfying, and it works just as well for a quick weekday lunch as it does alongside dinner. No sad desk salads here — this one actually keeps you full.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
0 mins
Total
15 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 small head romaine lettuce, chopped (about 5 cups)
  • 1/2 small head iceberg lettuce, chopped (about 3 cups)
  • 3 oz salami, sliced thin and cut into strips or quarters
  • 3 oz provolone cheese, cut into small cubes or strips
  • 1/2 cup canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1/2 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1/3 cup sliced pepperoncini peppers
  • 1/3 cup black or Kalamata olives, halved
  • 1/4 small red onion, very thinly sliced
  • 3 tablespoons red wine vinegar
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon sugar or a squeeze of honey
  • Salt and black pepper to taste

Instructions

  1. 1Make the dressing first so the flavors have a minute to hang out: in a small jar or bowl, whisk together the red wine vinegar, olive oil, dried oregano, garlic powder, sugar or honey, a big pinch of salt, and several grinds of black pepper. Taste it — it should be punchy and tangy. Set aside.
  2. 2Chop your romaine and iceberg into roughly half-inch pieces. You want everything small enough that each forkful gets a little of everything — that's the whole point of a chopped salad. Add both to a large bowl.
  3. 3Add the salami strips, provolone cubes, chickpeas, cherry tomatoes, pepperoncini, olives, and red onion right on top of the lettuce.
  4. 4Pour about three-quarters of the dressing over everything and toss well — really get in there and mix it so the dressing coats every little piece. Taste, then add more dressing, salt, or pepper as needed.
  5. 5Serve immediately if you like it crisp, or let it sit for 5 minutes if you want the flavors to meld a little more. Either way it's great.
💡 Improv Tip

The chop is everything here — resist the urge to leave things in big chunks. When everything is uniformly small, you get a little salami, a little cheese, a little olive, and a little crunch in every single bite, which is exactly why this salad is addictive. A mezzaluna or just a good sharp chef's knife and a little patience goes a long way.
