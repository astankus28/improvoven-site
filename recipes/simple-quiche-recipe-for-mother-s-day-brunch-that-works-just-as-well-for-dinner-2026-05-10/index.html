Simple Quiche Recipe for Mother's Day Brunch (That Works Just as Well for Dinner)

Published May 10, 2026 · Improv Oven

If you're searching for a simple quiche recipe for Mother's Day brunch but need it to pull double duty as a dinner, you've landed in the right kitchen. This golden, custardy quiche is packed with spinach, caramelized onions, and plenty of cheese — the kind of dish that feels fancy but comes together with stuff you probably already have. Serve it warm with a simple salad and suddenly your Tuesday night feels like a celebration.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
45 mins
Total
60 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 1 store-bought or homemade 9-inch pie crust
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 cup whole milk (or whatever milk you have)
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream (or evaporated milk for a budget swap)
  • 1 1/2 cups shredded cheese (Swiss, cheddar, or a mix — use what's in the fridge)
  • 2 cups fresh baby spinach, roughly chopped
  • 1 small yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons butter or olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg (optional but trust the process)
  • Pinch of red pepper flakes (optional, for a little Miami kick)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 375°F. If you're using a store-bought crust, press it into a 9-inch pie dish and crimp the edges. To keep the crust from puffing up, prick the bottom all over with a fork, then blind bake it for 10 minutes — just pop it in the oven as-is, no weights needed. Pull it out and let it cool slightly while you work on the filling.
  2. 2Heat butter or olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the sliced onions and a pinch of salt, then cook low and slow for about 12 to 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they're soft, golden, and a little sweet. Add the garlic and cook for another minute. Toss in the spinach and stir until it wilts down, which only takes about 2 minutes. Take the pan off the heat and let it cool for a few minutes.
  3. 3In a large bowl, whisk together the eggs, milk, cream, salt, pepper, nutmeg, and red pepper flakes until smooth and well combined. This is your custard base — don't rush the whisking, you want everything fully incorporated so the quiche sets up silky and even.
  4. 4Scatter half the cheese across the bottom of your pre-baked crust. Spread the spinach and onion mixture evenly on top, then pour the egg custard over everything slowly and carefully. Top with the remaining cheese. If the filling is close to the rim, place the pie dish on a baking sheet before it goes in the oven — easier cleanup if anything bubbles over.
  5. 5Bake at 375°F for 35 to 40 minutes, until the center is just barely set — it should have a slight jiggle in the very middle but not be liquid. If the crust edges start browning too fast, tent them loosely with foil. Let the quiche rest for at least 10 minutes before slicing. This rest time is not optional — it's what keeps your slice from falling apart on the plate.
💡 Improv Tip

The secret to a quiche that tastes restaurant-level is patience with those onions. Don't rush them on high heat — low and slow is what turns them from sharp and raw to sweet and jammy. That caramelization is doing a LOT of the flavor work here, so give it the time it deserves. Also, evaporated milk from the pantry is a genuine swap for heavy cream and nobody will know the difference.
