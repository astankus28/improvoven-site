Easy Malanga Fritters Recipe (Caribbean-Style, Crispy Outside and Tender Inside)

Published August 29, 2026 · Improv Oven

This easy malanga fritters recipe is the kind of Caribbean comfort food that turns a humble root vegetable into something you'll want to eat for breakfast, lunch, or a snack at any hour. Malanga — also called yautía or taro — grates up into a sticky, starchy batter that fries into golden, crispy patties with a soft, creamy center. They're dead simple, totally affordable, and wildly satisfying with just a handful of pantry staples.

Prep
15 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
35 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 2 lbs malanga (yautía), peeled
  • 3 cloves garlic, finely minced or grated
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1/4 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 2 tablespoons fresh cilantro, finely chopped (or flat-leaf parsley)
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour (optional — only if batter feels too loose)
  • 1/4 cup neutral oil for frying (vegetable, canola, or avocado oil)
  • Lime wedges, for serving
  • Hot sauce or garlic aioli, for serving

Instructions

  1. 1Peel the malanga using a sturdy vegetable peeler or a paring knife — the skin is thick so take your time. Cut the peeled malanga into chunks small enough to handle, then grate it on the large holes of a box grater into a big mixing bowl. Fair warning: this is the most hands-on part of the process, but it goes faster than you'd think.
  2. 2Grab a clean kitchen towel or a few layers of paper towels, dump the grated malanga into it, and squeeze out as much moisture as you possibly can. This step is non-negotiable — excess water is what causes fritters to fall apart and steam instead of fry. You want the grated malanga to feel almost dry and clumpy when you're done.
  3. 3Return the squeezed malanga to the mixing bowl. Add the egg, garlic, salt, black pepper, cumin, smoked paprika, and cilantro. Mix everything together well with a fork or your hands until combined. The mixture should hold its shape when you press a handful together — if it feels too wet or loose, add the flour one tablespoon at a time until it firms up.
  4. 4Heat your oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. You want about a quarter inch of oil in the pan — enough to shallow-fry, not deep-fry. The oil is ready when a small pinch of the batter sizzles immediately on contact.
  5. 5Scoop about 3 tablespoons of the malanga mixture per fritter and gently press it into a flat patty in your palm. Carefully lower it into the hot oil. Work in batches — don't crowd the pan or the temperature drops and you'll get soggy instead of crispy. Fry for 3 to 4 minutes per side until deeply golden brown.
  6. 6Transfer the finished fritters to a plate lined with paper towels to drain for a minute. Hit them with a pinch of flaky salt while they're still hot if you have it. Serve immediately with lime wedges and your dipping sauce of choice — garlic aioli, hot sauce, or even a simple sour cream with a squeeze of lime all work beautifully.
💡 Improv Tip

Malanga oxidizes (turns slightly pinkish or grayish) fast once peeled and grated — that's totally normal and won't affect the flavor, but if it bothers you, work in batches and fry as you go rather than grating everything first and letting it sit. The real secret to fritters that hold together perfectly is that squeeze step: the drier your grated malanga, the crispier and more cohesive your fritters will be. Don't skip it.
