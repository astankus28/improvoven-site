Freezing Bread Budget Recipe: Classic French Toast Casserole

Published August 31, 2026 · Improv Oven

This freezing bread budget recipe is the dessert move you never knew you needed — take that sad loaf you froze before it went stale, thaw a few slices, and transform them into a golden, custardy, cinnamon-brown-sugar French toast casserole that tastes like bread pudding's cooler cousin. It's sweet, a little crispy on top, soft and pillowy in the middle, and ready with pantry staples you probably already have. Perfect for a lazy weekend morning that wants to be dessert.

Prep
10 mins
Cook
35 mins
Total
45 mins
Serves
6

Ingredients

  • 6 slices frozen bread, thawed (any kind — white sandwich bread, brioche, Texas toast, even hot dog buns work)
  • 3 large eggs
  • 3/4 cup milk (whole, 2%, or whatever you have — oat milk works too)
  • 3 tablespoons granulated sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon nutmeg (optional but lovely)
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
  • For the brown sugar crumble topping: 3 tablespoons brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon
  • 1 tablespoon cold butter, cut into small pieces
  • Powdered sugar, maple syrup, or whipped cream for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. 1Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Lightly butter or spray an 8x8 inch baking dish — or any similar-sized oven-safe pan you have.
  2. 2Take your thawed bread slices and tear or cut them into rough 1 to 2 inch chunks. Don't stress about being neat — rustic is the whole point here. Spread them in the prepared baking dish in an even-ish layer.
  3. 3In a medium bowl, whisk together the eggs, milk, granulated sugar, vanilla extract, cinnamon, nutmeg (if using), and melted butter until smooth and combined. This is your custard base.
  4. 4Pour the custard evenly over the bread pieces. Use a fork or the back of a spoon to gently press the bread down so every piece gets coated and starts soaking up all that eggy goodness. Let it sit for 5 minutes while you make the topping.
  5. 5Make the brown sugar crumble: in a small bowl, mix together the brown sugar, flour, and cinnamon. Drop in the cold butter pieces and use your fingers to pinch and rub everything together until it looks like coarse, clumpy sand. Scatter this all over the top of the casserole.
  6. 6Bake uncovered for 30 to 35 minutes, until the top is golden brown and slightly crispy, the edges are set, and the center no longer jiggles when you gently shake the pan.
  7. 7Let it cool for 5 to 10 minutes before serving — it firms up beautifully as it rests. Dust with powdered sugar, drizzle with maple syrup, or add a dollop of whipped cream if you want to go full dessert mode.
💡 Improv Tip

Frozen bread is actually perfect for this — thawed bread that's slightly stale absorbs the custard without turning to mush, which is exactly what you want in a baked French toast casserole. If you thawed a whole loaf and only need six slices, freeze the rest right back. No guilt, no waste, just your future self thanking you.
