Simple Champurrado Recipe for Mexican Halloween Night

Published October 25, 2026 · Improv Oven

This simple champurrado recipe is the Mexican Halloween drink your whole crew needs warming up with after trick-or-treating — thick, chocolatey, and spiced just right. Think of it like the abuela-approved cousin of hot cocoa, made with masa harina and Mexican chocolate for that signature velvety body. It's one of those pantry-friendly drinks that costs almost nothing to make and tastes like you spent all day on it.

Prep
5 mins
Cook
20 mins
Total
25 mins
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 3 cups whole milk (or any milk you have — oat milk works great too)
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/3 cup masa harina (find it in the Latin aisle — Maseca is the most common brand)
  • 3.1 oz Mexican chocolate, roughly chopped (1 disc of Abuelita or Ibarra brand)
  • 2 tablespoons brown sugar or piloncillo, grated (adjust to taste)
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1/4 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt
  • Optional: pinch of cayenne or ancho chili powder for a Halloween kick
  • Optional garnish: whipped cream and a sprinkle of cinnamon sugar

Instructions

  1. 1In a medium saucepan over medium-low heat, whisk together the masa harina and water until completely smooth — no lumps. This step takes about 60 seconds of whisking. Don't rush it; this is what gives champurrado its thick, gorgeous body.
  2. 2Pour in the milk and add the cinnamon stick. Raise the heat to medium and stir frequently as the mixture warms up. You'll notice it starting to thicken after about 5 minutes — that's exactly what you want.
  3. 3Once it's steaming and starting to thicken (not boiling), add the chopped Mexican chocolate and brown sugar. Keep stirring until the chocolate is fully melted and everything is silky and combined, about 3 to 4 more minutes.
  4. 4Stir in the vanilla extract and pinch of salt. Taste it — add more sugar if you like it sweeter, or a little cayenne if you want that sneaky Halloween heat hiding at the back of your throat.
  5. 5Reduce heat to low and let it simmer gently for another 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it reaches your desired thickness. Champurrado should coat the back of a spoon — thick enough to feel like a hug in a mug.
  6. 6Fish out the cinnamon stick, pour into mugs, and top with whipped cream and a dusting of cinnamon sugar if you're feeling festive. Serve immediately while hot.
💡 Improv Tip

If you can't find Mexican chocolate (Abuelita or Ibarra), just swap in 2 tablespoons of unsweetened cocoa powder plus an extra tablespoon of sugar plus an extra pinch of cinnamon — it's not identical but it's genuinely delicious and totally improvised, which is kind of our whole thing here.
